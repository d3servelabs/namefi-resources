The **.work domain** is a plain-English top-level domain built for jobs, recruiting, and career brands — and one of the more literal domain-hack extensions in the new gTLD lineup, best known through laptop maker Framework Computer's `frame.work`. It is an open new gTLD that anyone can register, delegated in the 2012-round ICANN New gTLD Program and now operated by GoDaddy Registry. This page covers .work's ownership history, real uses, and its documented reputation trade-offs, so you can judge whether it fits your project.

## What is .work?

.work is a [generic top-level domain](/en/glossary/tld), not a country-code extension, so it carries no geographic meaning. The string is the ordinary English word for employment or labor, which makes it directly legible for job boards, recruiters, and career-focused brands — and a natural fit for the domain-hack pattern where the suffix completes the word, as in `frame.work`.

Because it is a generic gTLD, Google treats .work like any other new extension: no automatic ranking advantage or penalty, and no geo-targeting applied, per [Google Search Central's guidance on generic vs. country-targeted domains](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites?hl=en#:~:text=more%20generic%20than%20country-targeted). You can confirm the delegation record and current operator on the [IANA root-zone entry for .work](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/work.html).

Google also states that [keywords in a TLD do not give any advantage or disadvantage in search](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2015/07/googles-handling-of-new-top-level?hl=en#:~:text=Keywords%20in%20a%20TLD%20do%20not%20give%20any%20advantage%20or%20disadvantage%20in%20search.), so the extension itself receives no inherent ranking boost or penalty.

## History of .work

.work has changed registry operators once since launch, and the [IANA delegation record](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/work.html) documents both stages:

- **Original delegation (2014):** The [ICANN Registry Agreement for .work](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/work) was signed on 19 December 2013, and IANA's report shows the string was delegated to **Top Level Domain Holdings Limited** on 19 September 2014 (registration in the root dates to 18 August 2014). Top Level Domain Holdings later rebranded as Minds + Machines (MMX), a well-known new-gTLD portfolio operator from the 2012 application round.
- **Transfer to GoDaddy Registry (2021):** IANA recorded the "Transfer of the .work domain to Registry Services, LLC" on 14 September 2021. Registry Services, LLC is the registry entity behind **GoDaddy Registry**, which now operates .work's technical and administrative functions.

So .work began under one of the original 2012-round portfolio registries and has been part of GoDaddy's registry business since 2021.

## How people use .work

Real, specific niches where .work shows up:

- **Recruiting and HR** — agencies and internal talent-acquisition teams building a career-portal domain distinct from the corporate main site.
- **Job boards and freelance marketplaces** — platforms connecting employers and workers, where the suffix reinforces the site's purpose.
- **Career coaching and professional-development brands** — consultants and coaches whose services are literally about work.
- **The "[word].work" domain hack** — the clearest real example is laptop maker **Framework Computer**, which runs its primary site at `frame.work` rather than a .com.
- **Short, brandable names** — the .work namespace still has real availability for concise strings long gone on legacy zones.

**Who it's not ideal for:** consumer brands, e-commerce stores, and other businesses with no career, employment, or literal "work" angle — the meaning is specific enough that it reads oddly outside that context.

## Notable sites using .work

The clearest verifiable example is **`frame.work`**, the primary domain for Framework Computer, the modular and repairable laptop and desktop maker — a genuine domain hack where "frame" plus ".work" spells the company's name. Beyond that flagship case, .work's visible footprint mostly consists of recruiting portals, job boards, and small career-services sites rather than a broad roster of major consumer brands.

## Why choose .work?

- **Literal, unambiguous meaning** — "work" reads clearly to any audience without explanation.
- **Open and unrestricted** — no paperwork or eligibility checks; register and launch.
- **Real domain-hack upside** — the frame.work example shows the pattern can carry a serious brand.
- **Budget-friendly** — a low-cost way to secure a career-focused or word-hack name.

## Things to consider

- **Narrow meaning.** Outside a career, recruiting, or literal work-related context, the suffix can feel mismatched.
- **Ownership change.** The registry has already moved from Top Level Domain Holdings/MMX to GoDaddy once; administrative details can shift again with future ownership changes.
- **Historical abuse flag.** Independent trackers have flagged .work for high abuse ratios in the past (see Reputation below) — worth weighing for anything email-dependent.
- **Lower mainstream familiarity.** Many users still default to trusting .com first at a glance.

## Who can register a .work domain?

**Registration restrictions: open to all.** .work is an unrestricted generic TLD — there is no credential, professional-membership, community, or local-presence requirement. Any individual or organization worldwide can register a .work name through an accredited [registrar](/en/glossary/registrar).

Names can run from 1–63 characters, use letters, numbers, and hyphens (not in positions three and four), and are handled first-come, first-served, per [GoDaddy's .work domain policy summary](https://www.godaddy.com/help/about-work-domains-12354). The extension supports internationalized domain names (IDNs) and [DNSSEC](/en/glossary/dnssec), and registrations run 1–10 years at a time. The binding legal terms are set out in the [ICANN Registry Agreement for .work](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/work).

## .work pricing and value

.work sits in the budget tier of the gTLD market, and that shapes its pricing dynamics rather than any single number. **First-year and renewal pricing typically differ** — an inexpensive introductory rate is common, while the standing renewal rate is what matters long-term. The registry also reserves a set of **premium names** (short or high-demand strings, including common domain-hack prefixes) that carry higher registration and renewal pricing. The main cost drivers are string length and desirability, premium classification, and registrar margin. We list no figures here; check current rates at the point of purchase.

## Reputation and email deliverability

Buyers deserve candor here, and .work has a documented history worth knowing. In a widely cited 2016 ranking, [Spamhaus reported](https://dig.watch/updates/spamhaus-lists-top-10-worst-tlds-terms-spam#:~:text=According%20to%20this%20list%2C%20.diet%2C%20.click%2C%20.download%2C%20.review%2C%20.work%2C%20and%20.tokyo%20are%20the%20five%20TLDs%20with%20more%20than%20%2050%25%20%E2%80%98bad%20domains%E2%80%99%20each) that .work was among a small group of TLDs — alongside .diet, .click, .download, .review, and .tokyo — with more than 50% of their domains classified as "bad." That data point is nearly a decade old and predates the 2021 transfer of the registry to GoDaddy; we found no current, fetchable report confirming whether the abuse ratio has changed since the operator switch, so treat the 2016 figure as historical context, not a present-day rating.

More recently, Interisle's [Cybercrime Supply Chain 2025 research](https://interisle.net/insights/cybercrimesupplychain2025#:~:text=though%20they%20hold%20just%2012%25%20of%20the%20market%2C%20they%20accounted%20for%20nearly%20half%20of%20all%20cybercrime%20domains%20reported) (sponsored by APWG, CAUCE, and M3AAWG) found that new gTLDs as a category — which includes .work — hold just 12% of the domain market yet accounted for nearly half of all reported cybercrime domains. **Mitigation:** configure SPF, DKIM, and DMARC correctly for any email sent from a .work domain, warm up new sending domains gradually, and keep content and registrant information clean and verifiable.

## Branding and naming tips

- **Use the domain hack deliberately.** `frame.work` succeeds because "frame" plus "work" spells a real word — look for the same fit before committing.
- **Keep the career context clear.** If the name doesn't relate to jobs, hiring, or labor, consider whether .work is the right signal.
- **Keep it short.** Short prefixes plus .work read most naturally and are easiest to type.
- **Watch for confusable characters.** Zeros vs. the letter "o" and repeated letters are exactly what scammers exploit; a clean, pronounceable name avoids that pattern.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone register a .work domain?

Yes. .work is an open, unrestricted generic top-level domain. There is no credential, membership, or local-presence requirement, so anyone worldwide can register a .work name through an accredited registrar on a first-come, first-served basis.

### Does a .work domain affect SEO?

Google treats .work as a generic top-level domain with no built-in ranking boost or penalty and no geographic bias. Historically, some spam-tracking services have flagged .work among extensions with a high ratio of abusive registrations, which can indirectly affect trust and deliverability rather than search rankings directly.

### Who operates the .work domain?

Registry Services, LLC — part of GoDaddy Registry — has operated .work since September 2021. The string was originally delegated to Top Level Domain Holdings Limited (later Minds + Machines) in 2014.

### Who should register a .work domain?

It suits recruiters, HR departments, job boards, freelancers, and career-coaching brands, plus companies using the "[word].work" domain hack, as laptop maker Framework Computer does at frame.work. It is a weaker choice for consumer brands with no career or employment angle.
