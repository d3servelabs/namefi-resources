The **.studio domain** is an open generic top-level domain built for anyone running a creative or production space: design and branding agencies, video and photo studios, dance and fitness studios, music and recording studios, and independent game or art studios. It has a longer, more tangled corporate history than most new gTLDs — passing through three different registry operators since 2015 — but the string itself has settled into a genuinely useful niche. This page covers where .studio came from, who runs it today, real use cases, and the honest trade-offs before you register one.

## What is .studio?

.studio is a [generic top-level domain](/en/glossary/gtld) (gTLD) approved through ICANN's 2012 New gTLD Program, not a country-code extension, so it carries no geographic meaning. The [IANA root-zone entry for .studio](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/studio.html) records a **registration date of 2015-06-25** and lists **Dog Beach, LLC, c/o Identity Digital Inc.** as the current sponsoring organization.

Because it is a plain generic extension, Google Search Central treats [new gTLDs](/en/glossary/new-gtld) like .studio the same as legacy generics such as [.com](/en/tld/com): there is no automatic ranking benefit or penalty tied to the suffix, and no geo-targeting signal is applied.

Google also states that [keywords in a TLD do not give any advantage or disadvantage in search](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2015/07/googles-handling-of-new-top-level?hl=en#:~:text=Keywords%20in%20a%20TLD%20do%20not%20give%20any%20advantage%20or%20disadvantage%20in%20search.), so the extension itself receives no inherent ranking boost or penalty.

## History of .studio

- **Original launch under Rightside (2015).** IANA's delegation report shows .studio was delegated to **United TLD Holdco, Ltd.** — the registry-services arm of **Rightside Group** — on **2015-07-06**. The underlying [ICANN Registry Agreement for .studio was signed on 11 February 2015](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/studio). A trademark [sunrise period ran from August 18 to October 17, 2015](https://www.americaregistry.com/domains/studio-domain-registration), followed by a landrush phase from October 21–28, before **general availability opened on October 28, 2015**.
- **Donuts acquires Rightside (2017).** On [June 14, 2017, Rightside and rival new-gTLD operator Donuts announced a definitive merger agreement](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2017/06/14/1018714/0/en/Rightside-and-Donuts-Announce-Definitive-Merger-Agreement.html), with Donuts acquiring Rightside for $10.60 per share in an all-cash deal valued at roughly **$213 million**. This brought .studio's registry operations under Donuts's umbrella alongside Donuts's own portfolio of strings.
- **Consolidation into Dog Beach, LLC.** As part of restructuring the newly combined portfolio, the Rightside-heritage strings — including .studio — were reassigned into a dedicated registry entity, **Dog Beach, LLC**. IANA's record shows the formal transfer to Dog Beach, LLC completed on **2021-06-02**.
- **Identity Digital era (present).** Dog Beach remains the contracted registry operator of record, now operating as part of the **Identity Digital** family of companies — the merged, rebranded Donuts/Afilias registry group — which runs the shared technical back-end and registration services for .studio today.

## How people use .studio

Real, specific niches where .studio shows up:

- **Design, branding, and digital agencies** — a natural, discipline-matching extension for creative studios (`agencyname.studio`).
- **Video, photo, and film production studios** — production houses and freelance photographers/videographers using the suffix to name their craft directly.
- **Dance and fitness studios** — dance schools, yoga studios, Pilates studios, and boutique fitness businesses whose real-world "studio" maps directly onto the domain.
- **Music and recording studios** — independent recording spaces and producers.
- **Independent game and art studios** — small game-development teams and art collectives that identify as a "studio" rather than a corporation.

**Who it's not ideal for:** retail, e-commerce, or professional-services businesses with no studio identity, since the suffix specifically implies a creative or production space rather than a general company.

## Notable sites using .studio

.studio is a functional, discipline-matching extension whose adoption skews toward small and mid-size creative businesses — independent agencies, dance and fitness studios, and production houses — rather than a short list of globally recognized flagship brands. That is consistent with its history: it emerged from Rightside's and Donuts's broad new-gTLD strategy rather than a single design-community launch the way [.design](/en/tld/design) did. Rather than name a specific site that may not last, the honest characterization is that .studio is used mostly by independent creative operators and small production businesses whose real-world work matches the word "studio."

Pick .com when maximum universal trust matters most and the exact-match string is available; pick [.design](/en/tld/design) when the work is specifically design rather than broader creative/production work; pick .studio when the business runs a physical or creative "studio" — video, photo, dance, fitness, music, or art — and you want the domain to say so.

## Why choose .studio?

- **Broad but accurate meaning.** "Studio" covers a wide range of real creative and production spaces without being vague — video, photo, dance, fitness, music, and game studios all fit naturally.
- **Exact-match availability.** Businesses whose trade name includes "Studio" can often secure the precise match here when the equivalent .com is unavailable.
- **Open and unrestricted.** No paperwork, credentials, or eligibility checks — register and go.
- **Backed by an established operator.** Identity Digital is one of the largest gTLD registry operators, running shared, stable technical infrastructure across hundreds of extensions.

## Things to consider

- **Narrow semantic fit.** The suffix implies a creative/production space; a mismatch (e.g., a law firm or grocery store) is more obvious than with a neutral extension.
- **Complex corporate history.** .studio has changed hands from Rightside to Donuts to Dog Beach/Identity Digital since 2015 — operationally stable throughout, but worth knowing if provenance matters to you.
- **Still a newer gTLD.** Some users default to trusting .com first at a glance, though this matters less within creative-industry audiences already familiar with .studio.

## Who can register a .studio domain?

**Registration restrictions: open to all.** .studio is an unrestricted generic TLD — there is no credential, professional-membership, community, or local-presence requirement. Any individual or organization worldwide can register a .studio name through an accredited [registrar](/en/glossary/registrar).

The trademark **sunrise period** ran from August 18 to October 17, 2015, so today registrations are first-come, first-served. The extension supports internationalized domain names (IDNs) and [DNSSEC](/en/glossary/dnssec). Standard ICANN lifecycle rules apply — auto-renew grace, redemption grace, and pending-delete periods — so a lapsed .studio name is not immediately available again. The binding rules are set out in the [ICANN Registry Agreement for .studio](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/studio) and Identity Digital's own [domain policies](https://identity.digital/policies).

## .studio pricing and value

.studio sits in the standard new-gTLD pricing tier, and the dynamics matter more than any single figure. Expect **first-year and renewal pricing to differ** — introductory rates are often promotional, while the standing renewal price is what you pay long term, so budget for renewal, not the teaser rate. The registry also reserves a set of **premium names** (short, generic, or high-demand strings) that carry higher registration and renewal pricing than standard names. The main cost drivers are string length, dictionary-word desirability, premium classification, and registrar margin. We list no figures here; check current rates at the point of purchase.

## Reputation and email deliverability

.studio does not carry a documented history of spam or abuse issues comparable to some cheaper, high-volume new gTLDs — its adoption by creative agencies, studios, and small production businesses tends to attract legitimate, low-volume registrants rather than bulk abuse. As with any newer gTLD, some conservative mail providers may apply marginally more scrutiny to unfamiliar domains than to decades-old legacy extensions. **Mitigation:** configure SPF, DKIM, and DMARC correctly, warm up any new sending domain gradually, and keep registrant and content information clean and consistent.

## Branding and naming tips

- **Let the extension name the space.** `dance.studio`, `sound.studio`, or `yourname.studio` tells visitors what kind of studio it is before they click.
- **Drop redundant words.** If your business is "Acme Studio," `acme.studio` is cleaner than `acmestudio.studio`.
- **Match the real-world word.** .studio reads most naturally when the business genuinely calls itself a studio — dance studio, recording studio, design studio — rather than as a generic company suffix.
- **Watch for confusion outside creative circles.** Audiences unfamiliar with new gTLDs may misread `.studio` as part of the name rather than the extension — a clear logo lockup or tagline helps.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone register a .studio domain?

Yes. .studio is an open, unrestricted generic top-level domain operated by Dog Beach, LLC (part of Identity Digital). There is no credential, membership, or local-presence requirement, so any individual or organization worldwide can register one through an accredited registrar.

### Does a .studio domain affect SEO?

No. Google treats .studio as a standard generic top-level domain with no ranking boost or penalty and no geographic bias. Ranking still depends on content quality, relevance, and links, not the extension itself.

### Who should register a .studio domain?

Creative agencies, video and photo studios, dance and fitness studios, music and recording studios, and independent game or art studios are the strongest fit, since the suffix names the kind of physical or creative space they run.

### Is .studio a good choice for a business that has no physical studio?

It can still work if the business is fundamentally creative or production-based, but the fit is weaker for retail, professional-services, or e-commerce brands with no studio identity, where a neutral extension like .com reads more naturally.
