The **.meme domain** is Google Registry's generic top-level domain built for internet culture: humor, virality, and the communities that live on it. Operated by [Charleston Road Registry](https://www.registry.google), it is home to genuinely recognizable names — **Know Your Meme**, **Grumpy Cat**, and **Nyan Cat** among them. This page covers .meme's unusual launch history, who uses it, and the honest trade-offs of building a brand on a domain whose entire identity is "meme."

## What is .meme?

.meme is a [generic top-level domain](/en/glossary/tld) (gTLD), not a country-code TLD, so it carries no geographic association. Its string is the plainest possible signal of intent: a site built on memes, internet humor, and shareable culture. Google Registry's own positioning is direct — ".meme is for the culture," meant to help brands "spread humor, ideas, style, and culture." You can confirm the delegation record and operator in the [IANA root-zone database entry for .meme](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/meme.html).

Google also states that [keywords in a TLD do not give any advantage or disadvantage in search](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2015/07/googles-handling-of-new-top-level?hl=en#:~:text=Keywords%20in%20a%20TLD%20do%20not%20give%20any%20advantage%20or%20disadvantage%20in%20search.), so the extension itself receives no inherent ranking boost or penalty.

Because .meme is a generic extension, [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites?hl=en#:~:text=Generic%20top-level%20domains%20(gTLDs)%20are%20domains%20that%20aren't%20associated%20with%20specific%20locations.) confirms gTLDs "aren't associated with specific locations," so it carries no automatic geo-targeting signal, and no inherent ranking boost or penalty.

## History of .meme

- **2014 — contracted but dormant.** Charleston Road Registry signed the [ICANN Registry Agreement for .meme](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/meme) on January 30, 2014, during the 2012 New gTLD Program round, and the string was delegated to the root zone on June 12, 2014. As with many strings from that round, it went unlaunched for nearly a decade.
- **2023 — a launch with an unusual middle phase.** Google Registry opened .meme on a four-stage schedule: a trademark-holder [sunrise period](/en/glossary/tmch/) from September 20 to October 24, 2023; then, uniquely among Google's TLDs, a **limited registration period from October 31 to November 28, 2023** restricted specifically to "content creation platforms specializing in memes"; an early access period from November 28 to December 5; and full [general availability from December 5, 2023](https://www.registry.google/announcements/launch-details-for-ing-and-meme/).
- **Since 2023.** Adoption has skewed toward exactly the audience the limited phase targeted — meme databases, meme-licensing businesses, and internet-culture media brands.

## How people use .meme

Real, specific niches where .meme shows up:

- **Meme databases and reference sites** — cataloguing and explaining internet culture.
- **Iconic meme-character brands** — official pages for characters that became cultural phenomena.
- **Meme licensing and clearinghouses** — businesses that license meme rights to advertisers and media.
- **Marketing and media-strategy firms specializing in meme campaigns** — agencies whose entire pitch is memes.
- **GIF, sticker, and meme-creation tools** — platforms for finding or making shareable content.

**Who it's not ideal for:** banks, law firms, healthcare providers, and any brand that needs maximum formal credibility — the extension's identity works against a serious, institutional tone.

Google Registry's own [get.meme showcase](https://get.meme/) lists real, currently active adopters:

- **GrumpyCat.meme** — the official page for **Grumpy Cat**, one of the most widely shared cat memes.

Pick .com for a project that needs to outgrow the "meme site" label; pick [.fun](/en/tld/fun) for a broader playful-but-not-meme-specific tone; pick .meme when the content genuinely is memes, internet culture, or humor and you want the domain to say so outright.

## Why choose .meme?

- **Unambiguous positioning.** No other extension states "this is about memes" as directly.
- **Real, recognizable adopters.** Know Your Meme, Grumpy Cat, and Nyan Cat lend the extension actual cultural credibility, not just theoretical appeal.
- **Built-in security.** Like all Google Registry TLDs, .meme is [included on the HSTS preload list](https://get.meme/), enforcing HTTPS on every connection without per-site configuration.
- **Availability.** Short, punchy names remain findable in a namespace that is still young.

## Things to consider

- **Narrow brand fit.** The extension only works when the project's identity is genuinely built on memes or internet humor — everything else, it undersells.
- **Lower mainstream familiarity.** Users unfamiliar with newer gTLDs may pause before treating a .meme site as a "real" business domain.
- **A brief history of restriction.** The limited-registration phase means some of the earliest, most desirable names went to meme-platform applicants before general registration opened — worth knowing if you're chasing a specific short name.
- **Longevity of humor.** Meme relevance ages fast; a name tied to a specific joke or format can feel dated quickly.

## Who can register a .meme domain?

**Registration restrictions: open to all today.** .meme launched with a temporary gate — a [limited registration period](https://www.registry.google/announcements/launch-details-for-ing-and-meme/) from October 31 to November 28, 2023, open only to content-creation platforms specializing in memes — but that restriction lifted before early access and general availability. Since December 5, 2023, any individual or organization worldwide can register a .meme name through an accredited [registrar](/en/glossary/registrar), with no ongoing credential, community, or local-presence requirement.

[DNSSEC](/en/glossary/dnssec) is supported, and [WHOIS privacy](/en/glossary/whois-privacy/) is available through most registrars. Standard ICANN lifecycle protections apply, including auto-renew grace and redemption-grace periods, so a lapsed name is not instantly re-available. The binding rules are set out in the [ICANN Registry Agreement for .meme](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/meme) and Google Registry's [Domain Registration Policy](https://www.registry.google/policies/).

## .meme pricing and value

.meme pricing follows the dynamics common to newer Google gTLDs: **first-year promotional pricing typically differs from the standing renewal rate**, so plan around the renewal cost rather than any introductory offer. The registry also designates certain **premium names** (short, dictionary, or high-demand strings) at elevated registration and renewal pricing. Cost is primarily driven by string length, dictionary-word status, premium classification, and registrar margin. We list no figures here — check current rates at the point of purchase.

## Reputation and email deliverability

.meme does not carry a documented spam-abuse reputation comparable to some ultra-cheap new gTLDs, and its highest-profile adopters — Know Your Meme, Tenor, established meme-media brands — lean legitimate and well-known. As a Google Registry TLD, .meme is [included on the HSTS preload list](https://get.meme/), so browsers require HTTPS on every .meme connection by default, closing off unencrypted-connection attacks outright. Because the extension is still young, mail providers have less historical signal to score it on; standard email hygiene (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) applies as it would for any newer domain used for transactional mail.

## Branding and naming tips

- **Lead with the joke or the community, not the product spec.** .meme rewards names that signal culture and humor over formal descriptions.
- **Keep it short and shareable.** Memes spread through quick recall — a long .meme URL undercuts the format.
- **Avoid tying the name to a single fading trend.** A meme-specific name ages faster than a generic brand name; weigh longevity against topicality.
- **Pair with visual branding.** Since the TLD does the positioning work, the second-level name can focus on being memorable rather than descriptive.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone register a .meme domain?

Yes, today. .meme launched with a temporary limited-registration phase reserved for meme content-creation platforms, but that restriction ended before general availability, and .meme has been an open, unrestricted generic top-level domain since December 5, 2023, registerable by anyone through an accredited registrar.

### Does a .meme domain affect SEO?

No. Google treats .meme as a generic top-level domain with no built-in ranking boost or penalty and no geographic association, the same as .com or .xyz. Ranking still depends entirely on the site itself.

### Was .meme ever restricted to meme platforms only?

Briefly. Between October 31 and November 28, 2023, Google Registry ran a limited registration period open only to content-creation platforms specializing in memes, ahead of the wider early access and general-availability phases that opened registration to everyone.

### Who should register a .meme domain?

It suits meme databases, internet-culture brands, entertainment and media pages, and any project whose identity is built on humor or virality. It is a weaker fit for formal institutions, financial services, or anything needing maximum mainstream gravitas.
