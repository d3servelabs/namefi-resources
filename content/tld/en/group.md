The **.group domain** is an open, unrestricted generic top-level domain built for exactly what its name says: teams, communities, and organizations made up of more than one part. It has quietly become a natural fit for **corporate holding companies** and business conglomerates whose legal name ends in "Group," as well as clubs, collectives, and membership communities. This page covers where .group came from, who actually runs it today, real use cases, and the honest trade-offs before you register one.

## What is .group?

.group is a [generic top-level domain](/en/glossary/gtld) (gTLD) approved through ICANN's 2012 New gTLD Program, not a country-code extension, so it carries no geographic meaning. The [IANA root-zone entry for .group](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/group.html) lists **Binky Moon, LLC, c/o Identity Digital Inc.** as the current sponsoring organization and shows a **registration date of 2015-07-30** for the delegation record.

Because it is a plain generic extension, Google Search Central treats [new gTLDs](/en/glossary/new-gtld) like .group the same as legacy generics such as [.com](/en/tld/com): there is no automatic ranking benefit or penalty tied to the suffix itself, and no geo-targeting signal is applied.

## History of .group

- **Contested string, private auction (2014).** Multiple applicants sought the .group string during ICANN's New gTLD Program; the rights were won by **Donuts Inc.** through a private auction among the competing applicants in July 2014. Donuts signed the underlying [ICANN Registry Agreement for .group on 15 August 2014](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/group).
- **Delegation and launch (2015).** IANA's report shows the .group domain was delegated to **Romeo Town, LLC** — one of Donuts's many single-TLD registry subsidiaries — on 2015-08-05, with general availability effectively beginning 2015-07-30.
- **Consolidation under Binky Moon (2018).** As part of a broader restructuring, Donuts transferred .group and 195 other strings (including .academy) into a single operating entity, **Binky Moon, LLC**, simplifying what had been nearly 200 separate single-purpose registry subsidiaries.
- **Identity Digital era (present).** Binky Moon remains the contracted registry operator of record, now operating as part of the **Identity Digital** family of companies (the rebranded, merged Donuts/Afilias registry group), which runs the technical back-end and registration services for .group today.

## How people use .group

Real, specific niches where .group shows up:

- **Corporate holding companies and conglomerates** — businesses whose legal or trade name literally ends in "Group" use it as a natural, exact-match domain (`example.group` instead of `examplegroup.com`).
- **Teams and internal collaboration tools** — a short, descriptive namespace for team sites, intranets, or project hubs.
- **Clubs, associations, and community organizations** — hobby clubs, alumni networks, and membership bodies where "group" describes the entity itself.
- **Volunteer and cause-driven collectives** — grassroots organizing pages that want the domain to say plainly what the site is.
- **Multi-brand or multi-subsidiary businesses** — an umbrella site that links out to several sub-brands under one holding entity.

**Who it's not ideal for:** solo consultants, single-product startups, and personal brands, where "group" misrepresents a one-person or one-product operation.

## Notable sites using .group

.group is a young, low-profile extension, and unlike [.io](/en/tld/io) or [.co](/en/tld/co) it has not yet produced widely recognized flagship consumer brands. In practice its visible footprint skews toward corporate holding-company sites, internal team tools, and community or association pages rather than major public brands — an honest reflection of a suffix that is functional and descriptive rather than aspirational. Buyers evaluating .group should weigh it on that functional fit rather than expecting brand-name peers.

Pick .com when maximum universal trust matters most and the exact-match string is available; pick [.company](/en/tld/company) as a close synonym; pick .group specifically when the entity is a genuine holding company, team, or collective and you want the domain to say so directly.

## Why choose .group?

- **Literal, self-explanatory meaning.** Unlike many new gTLDs, "group" is a real English word that describes exactly what a corporate group, team, or community is.
- **Exact-match availability.** Businesses whose name ends in "Group" can often secure the precise match here when the equivalent .com is long gone.
- **Open and unrestricted.** No paperwork, credentials, or eligibility checks — register and go.
- **Backed by an established operator.** Identity Digital is one of the largest gTLD registry operators, running the shared technical infrastructure behind hundreds of extensions.

## Things to consider

- **Low mainstream brand recognition.** .group has not produced the kind of widely known consumer sites that [.io](/en/tld/io) or [.app](/en/tld/app) have, so visitors may be less familiar with it at a glance.
- **Narrow semantic fit.** The word "group" only reads naturally for entities that genuinely are groups — a mismatch here is more obvious than with a neutral suffix like .com.
- **Still a newer gTLD.** Some users default to trusting .com first; a .group site may need to work slightly harder to establish first-glance credibility.

## Who can register a .group domain?

**Registration restrictions: open to all.** .group is an unrestricted generic TLD — there is no credential, professional-membership, community, or local-presence requirement. Any individual or organization worldwide can register a .group name through an accredited [registrar](/en/glossary/registrar).

A trademark **sunrise period** preceded general availability in 2015, so today registrations are first-come, first-served. The extension supports internationalized domain names (IDNs) and [DNSSEC](/en/glossary/dnssec). Standard ICANN lifecycle rules apply — auto-renew grace, redemption grace, and pending-delete periods — so a lapsed .group name is not immediately available again. The binding rules are set out in the [ICANN Registry Agreement for .group](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/group) and Identity Digital's own [domain policies](https://identity.digital/policies).

## .group pricing and value

.group sits in the standard new-gTLD pricing tier, and the dynamics matter more than any single figure. Expect **first-year and renewal pricing to differ** — introductory rates are often promotional, while the standing renewal price is what you pay long term, so budget for renewal, not the teaser rate. The registry also reserves a set of **premium names** (short, generic, or high-demand strings such as common business terms) that carry higher registration and renewal pricing than standard names. The main cost drivers are string length, dictionary-word desirability, premium classification, and registrar margin. We list no figures here; check current rates at the point of purchase.

## Reputation and email deliverability

.group does not carry the kind of abuse reputation seen with some cheaper, high-volume new gTLDs — it has not featured prominently in spam-abuse rankings from anti-abuse organizations, which is itself a point in its favor for a business-facing suffix. That said, as with any newer gTLD, some conservative mail providers may apply marginally more scrutiny to unfamiliar domains than to legacy extensions with decades of sending history. **Mitigation:** configure SPF, DKIM, and DMARC correctly, warm up any new sending domain gradually, and keep content and registrant information clean and consistent.

## Branding and naming tips

- **Use it for what it literally says.** .group works best when the underlying entity is a real group — a corporate conglomerate, team, club, or collective — not as a generic substitute for .com.
- **Drop the redundant word "group" from the name itself.** If your company is "Acme Group," `acme.group` is cleaner than `acmegroup.group`.
- **Keep it short.** The suffix already communicates plurality/organization, so a concise second-level name reads best.
- **Pair with a clear tagline.** Since the extension is still relatively unfamiliar to casual users, a short on-page explanation of what the "group" comprises builds instant clarity.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone register a .group domain?

Yes. .group is an open, unrestricted generic top-level domain operated by Binky Moon, LLC (part of Identity Digital). There is no credential, membership, or local-presence requirement, so any individual or organization worldwide can register one through an accredited registrar.

### Does a .group domain affect SEO?

No. Google treats .group as a standard generic top-level domain with no ranking boost or penalty and no geographic bias. Ranking still depends on content quality, relevance, and links, not the extension itself.

### Who should register a .group domain?

Corporate holding companies, business conglomerates, teams, clubs, and community or membership organizations whose name literally is or contains "group" are the best fit, since the suffix reinforces that identity directly in the domain.

### Is .group a good choice for a single-owner business or personal brand?

Generally no. The word "group" implies plurality and organization, so it reads oddly for a solo brand or single-product startup. It works best when the underlying entity genuinely is a group of people, companies, or teams.
