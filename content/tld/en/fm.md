The **.fm domain** is the [country-code top-level domain](/en/glossary/cctld/) (ccTLD) of the **Federated States of Micronesia**, a Pacific island nation — and, for most of the world, the address of choice for anything that sounds like radio. Because "FM" is the universal shorthand for frequency-modulation broadcasting, the suffix was repurposed for global use as early as 1998, making it one of the first ccTLDs ever rebranded around an industry rather than a place. Today it anchors some of the best-known names in music tracking, podcasting, and streaming audio.

This page covers who operates .fm, its unusual history, which real brands use it, how Google treats it, and the honest trade-offs.

## What is .fm?

.fm is the two-letter country code assigned to the **Federated States of Micronesia** under ISO 3166-1, and the [IANA root-zone entry for .fm](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/fm.html) records **FSM Telecommunications Corporation** — the nation's telecom carrier — as its sponsoring organization. On paper, that makes it a national namespace like [.us](/en/tld/us) or .de.

In practice, almost nobody registers a .fm name to signal Micronesia. The letters spell the **FM radio band**, and the [registry](/en/glossary/registry/) leaned into that reading decades ago: **dotFM**, a division of San Francisco-based BRS Media, markets .fm worldwide to broadcasters, podcasters, and streaming services — the "repurposed ccTLD" pattern that also gave the world [.io](/en/tld/io), [.tv](/en/tld/tv), and [.ai](/en/tld/ai).

Search engines have caught up with reality. Google's multi-regional documentation places .fm on its short list of ccTLDs it [treats as generic domains rather than country-targeted ones](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites#:~:text=more%20generic%20than%20country%2Dtargeted), alongside .io, .tv, .me, and .co — because users and site owners see these extensions as generic. A .fm site can therefore rank for audiences anywhere, with no built-in geographic bias.

## History of .fm

- **1995 — delegation.** The .fm string was delegated in the root zone on April 19, 1995, per the [IANA record](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/fm.html), with the Micronesian national telecom as sponsor.
- **1998 — the commercial relaunch.** BRS Media partnered with FSM Telecommunications Corporation and launched .fm to the global market as a radio-and-media extension — one of the earliest ccTLDs rebranded around an industry, predating the mass-market rise of .io and .ai by over a decade.
- **2000s–2010s — audio's home address.** Internet radio, music services, and then the podcast boom adopted the suffix; Last.fm made it famous, and podcast hosts and players followed.
- **2024 — partnership renewed.** BRS Media and FSM Telecommunications Corporation [signed a new agreement in November 2024](https://brsmedia.com/30yrs/) extending the partnership beyond the 30-year mark, keeping dotFM as operator and global marketer.

Registration revenue flows through the national carrier, so the extension's global popularity benefits the sponsoring country — a common arrangement covered in our [ccTLD market-share overview](/en/blog/cctld-market-share-by-registration-volume/).

## How people use .fm

- **Podcast hosting and player apps** — hosts and players building their entire brand on the suffix (`transistor.fm`, `overcast.fm`).
- **Music tracking and discovery** — the archetype being Last.fm, where ".fm" became part of the product name.
- **Radio stations and internet radio** — broadcasters using the literal reading of the letters; the registry also operates companion offerings like RADIO.fm.
- **Streaming and audio-first startups** — voice, audio-social, and music-tech products that want "audio" baked into the address.
- **Short brandable names** — with far fewer registrations than [.com](/en/tld/com), concise one-word names are still attainable.

**Who it's not ideal for:** projects with no audio, music, or broadcast angle, where the FM reading does nothing and a more neutral extension communicates better.

## Notable sites using .fm

- **[Last.fm](https://www.last.fm/about)** — the long-running music tracking ("scrobbling") and discovery service, live on .fm since the 2000s and still active today. Arguably the single site that made the extension famous.
- **[Transistor.fm](https://transistor.fm/)** — a professional podcast hosting and analytics platform that distributes shows to Apple Podcasts, Spotify, and YouTube.
- **[Overcast.fm](https://overcast.fm/)** — the award-winning independent podcast player for iPhone, iPad, and Apple Watch.

One cautionary counter-example: Anchor.fm, once among the most visible podcast brands on the extension, was absorbed into Spotify's creator platform, and its old links now redirect to spotify.com addresses.

## Why choose .fm?

- **Instant audio meaning.** No mainstream extension says "sound" as directly — the FM band is recognized worldwide, across languages.
- **Proven company.** Last.fm, Transistor.fm, and Overcast.fm demonstrate that serious, durable products build on it.
- **No geo-penalty.** Google's generic-ccTLD listing means a .fm site competes globally, unlike most country codes.
- **Availability.** Short, pronounceable names long gone in .com are frequently still open in .fm — and the extension can even become part of the spoken brand, as "Last-F-M" shows.

## Things to consider

- **Price tier.** Repurposed ccTLDs with strong niche demand typically cost more than mainstream gTLDs, both to register and to renew (see pricing dynamics below).
- **ccTLD governance.** .fm is administered under Micronesian sponsorship and registry policy, not an ICANN gTLD registry agreement. Its long operational track record is reassuring, but the rules are set by the ccTLD manager.
- **Niche meaning.** Outside audio and media, "fm" reads as arbitrary letters, and you lose the main reason to pay the premium.
- **Registry-held names.** Some legacy second-level strings (such as com.fm, net.fm, and org.fm) have been registered and held by the registry operator BRS Media itself since 1999, so they are not available for open registration.

## Who can register a .fm domain?

**Registration restrictions: none — .fm is open to everyone.** Despite being a country code, the [dotFM registration policy](https://dot.fm/policy.cfm#:~:text=first%20come%2C%20first%20served) allocates names on a **"first come, first served"** basis, with no residency, local-presence, citizenship, or credential requirement. Any individual or organization worldwide can register through the registry or an accredited [registrar](/en/glossary/registrar/).

Key administrative facts:

- **Dispute resolution.** The registry has adopted the [UDRP](/en/glossary/udrp/), so trademark holders can challenge abusive registrations through providers such as WIPO — the same mechanism used for gTLDs.
- **Registrant responsibility.** Registrants must warrant that a name does not infringe third-party rights and is not registered for an unlawful purpose; the registry does not pre-screen names.
- **Emoji domains.** Unusually, [the registry offers emoji domains](https://dot.fm/) — for example 📻.fm — one of the few TLDs to support them.
- **WHOIS and privacy.** The registry runs a public WHOIS (whois.nic.fm) and RDAP service; [WHOIS privacy](/en/glossary/whois-privacy/) availability depends on your registrar.

## .fm pricing and value

.fm sits in the **premium ccTLD tier**: repurposed country codes with strong niche demand price well above commodity gTLDs, and renewal pricing — not the first-year rate — is what you will live with. The registry also reserves and auctions **premium and expired names**, so short, generic, or high-demand strings carry a further premium. We quote no figures here; check live pricing when you register, and budget on the renewal rate.

## Reputation and email deliverability

.fm carries a **clean, professional reputation**. Its relatively high price discourages the bulk registrations that drag down cheap extensions, and its most visible tenants are legitimate consumer products rather than throwaway sites.

For email, the extension itself is rarely the deciding factor: modern filters weigh **sender authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) and sending history** far more than the TLD, a dynamic we unpack in our [sender-reputation guide](/en/blog/email-sender-reputation-arms-race/). A properly authenticated .fm sender should land normally. The practical consideration is recognition: media and tech audiences read .fm instantly; some mainstream users may not.

## Branding and naming tips

- **Let the extension finish the thought.** The best .fm names read as station idents or audio brands: `yourshow.fm`, `city.fm`, `genre.fm`.
- **Use it as a spoken brand element.** Words ending in "-fm" are rare, so this is less a classic [domain hack](/en/glossary/domain-hack/) than a pronounced suffix — "something-F-M". More patterns in [domain hacks explained](/en/blog/domain-hacks-explained/).
- **Say it out loud.** Podcast and radio brands live in audio; if your name is awkward with "dot F M" after it, keep looking.
- **Check the .com anyway.** Pairing the matching .com closes off confusion.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone register a .fm domain?

Yes. Although .fm is the country-code TLD of the Federated States of Micronesia, registration is open worldwide on a first-come, first-served basis. The dotFM registry policy imposes no residency, local-presence, or credential requirement, so any individual or business can register one.

### Does a .fm domain affect SEO?

Google explicitly treats .fm as a generic top-level domain rather than a country-targeted one, so a .fm site is not boxed into Micronesia in search results. Rankings depend on your content and links, not the extension, and .fm sites can target any market.

### Who runs the .fm domain?

IANA lists FSM Telecommunications Corporation, the national carrier of the Federated States of Micronesia, as the sponsoring organization. Day-to-day registry operations and global marketing are handled by dotFM, a division of San Francisco-based BRS Media, under a partnership that began in 1998 and was renewed in November 2024.

### Who should register a .fm domain?

It suits podcasts, radio stations, streaming and music services, and audio-first brands, because the letters read as the FM radio band. Last.fm, Transistor.fm, and Overcast.fm all built on it. It is a weaker fit for projects with no audio angle, where the meaning is lost.
