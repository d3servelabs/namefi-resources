The **.nl domain** is the official [country-code top-level domain](/en/glossary/cctld/) (ccTLD) for the **Netherlands**, and a genuine internet pioneer: it was the first active ccTLD registered anywhere outside the United States. Run today by the foundation [SIDN](https://www.sidn.nl/), .nl is open to anyone worldwide, backed by one of the highest domain-registration densities of any country. This guide covers .nl's origin story, current eligibility, and the real trade-offs to weigh before registering.

## What is .nl?

.nl is the Netherlands' [ccTLD](/en/glossary/cctld/), and its [IANA root-zone record](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/nl.html) lists SIDN as the registry manager. As a ccTLD it sits outside ICANN's standard registry-agreement system, governed instead by SIDN's own [registration terms](https://www.sidn.nl/en/nl-domain-name/registering-a-domain-name).

For search visibility, Google's own [multi-regional sites guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites?hl=en) treats ccTLDs as country-targeted signals by default, and .nl is not on Google's short list of ccTLDs (such as .co or .io) that it treats as generic. A .nl site sends Google — and Dutch users — a genuine signal that the content is meant for the Netherlands.

## History of .nl

.nl has an unusually well-documented origin. On **25 April 1986**, the creation of a top-level domain for the Netherlands was approved, and — as SIDN's own [40th-anniversary retrospective](https://www.sidn.nl/en/news-and-blogs/40-years-of-the-nl-domain) states — **.nl became the first active country-code domain outside the United States**. Six days later, on 1 May 1986, **cwi.nl** was registered by the Centrum Wiskunde & Informatica (CWI), becoming the first .nl domain ever issued. The person behind it, **Piet Beertema**, personally handled domain registration by hand for the next decade, registering roughly the first 10,000 .nl names himself before **SIDN was founded in 1996** by CWI, SURFnet, and NLnet to take over as a dedicated registry.

Growth accelerated sharply after **2003**, when SIDN opened .nl registration to private individuals for the first time — until then, names had been reserved for companies and organizations registered with the Dutch Chamber of Commerce. From just over 1 million names in 2003, .nl grew to 4 million by 2010, 5 million by 2012, and passed its 6-millionth registration in 2020. Today [SIDN Labs](https://stats.sidnlabs.nl/en/) confirms .nl has **over 6 million registered domain names**, making it, in SIDN's own words, "the world's fourth-largest country-code domain."

## How people use .nl

- **Dutch businesses and organizations** for whom a .nl address is the default, expected way to be found online in the Netherlands.
- **E-commerce and retail** targeting Dutch shoppers, given the Netherlands' unusually deep domain-adoption culture.
- **International companies opening a Dutch storefront**, often running .nl alongside a global .com.
- **Individuals and personal projects**, a segment SIDN itself unlocked by opening registration to private individuals in 2003.
- **Brand-protection registrations** by companies that trade in or serve the Netherlands but are headquartered elsewhere.

**Who it's not ideal for:** projects targeting a global, English-first audience, since search engines read .nl as Netherlands-specific, and buyers who need accented or non-Latin characters, since .nl names are ASCII-only.

## Notable sites using .nl

Well-known active .nl sites include **nu.nl** (one of the Netherlands' most-visited news sites) and **marktplaats.nl** (the country's dominant online classifieds and marketplace platform). And there's the historical anchor for the whole namespace: **cwi.nl**, the first .nl domain ever registered, still run by the Centrum Wiskunde & Informatica research institute that registered it back in 1986.

## Why choose .nl?

- **Deep local trust.** The Netherlands has one of the highest domain-registration densities in the world, and .nl is the namespace Dutch users expect by default.
- **Genuinely open registration.** No residency or nationality test blocks a first-time buyer, and it has been that way since 2003.
- **Historic pedigree.** As the first ccTLD active outside the US, .nl carries real internet history.
- **Active abuse mitigation.** SIDN says demonstrable abuse is lower in .nl than in the vast majority of other TLDs, thanks to active collaboration with registrars on threats like phishing and botnets.

## Things to consider

- **No IDN support.** Names are restricted to a–z, 0–9, and hyphens — no accents, umlauts, or non-Latin scripts.
- **Strong Netherlands association.** The same geo-signal that helps domestic ranking works against a brand chasing an international, geography-neutral audience.
- **Domicile-address handling for foreign registrants.** It's a minor administrative wrinkle, not a barrier, but worth understanding before you register (see below).
- **Unusual nameserver rule.** SIDN requires each nameserver to use a unique IP address, a stricter technical requirement than most TLDs impose.

## Who can register a .nl domain?

**Registration restrictions: open to all.** SIDN has allowed anyone — anywhere in the world, private individual or organization — to register a .nl domain since **2003**; before that, registration was limited to Dutch Chamber of Commerce-registered entities. There is no residency or citizenship test today.

One administrative detail is worth knowing: under SIDN's [domicile address procedure](https://www.sidn.nl/en/nl-domain-name/domicile-address), any registrant whose address is outside the Netherlands automatically has SIDN's own Arnhem address recorded as a **domicile address** — the address where a bailiff can formally serve legal documents such as a summons. SIDN forwards any such documents it receives to the registrant's admin-c contact; it does not otherwise restrict or gatekeep the registration itself. Domain names must be 2–63 characters and use only a–z, 0–9, and hyphens (with a letter or number required on each side of any hyphen), per SIDN's [technical requirements](https://www.sidn.nl/en/nl-domain-name/technical-requirements-for-the-registration-and-use-of-nl-domain-names). [DNSSEC](/en/glossary/dnssec/) is supported and actively promoted by SIDN alongside DMARC for email security.

## .nl pricing and value

SIDN publishes a basic wholesale price for every `.nl` registration and explains that [each registrar or reseller is free to decide what to charge customers](https://www.sidn.nl/en/our-prices#:~:text=Each%20registrar%20and%20reseller%20is%20free%20to%20decide%20what%20to%20charge%20their%20customers.). Registrars may package hosting or offer introductory discounts. Because many concise Dutch terms are already registered, buyers may encounter higher **aftermarket** asking prices from existing owners; that is not a registry premium registration or renewal tier. Compare the registrar's first-year and renewal fees before purchase.

## Reputation and email deliverability

.nl has an unusually strong reputation for a namespace of its size. SIDN states directly that demonstrable abuse is **less common in .nl than in the vast majority of other top-level domains**, crediting active collaboration with its registrar community against botnets, malware, phishing, and fake webshops, plus its promotion of DNSSEC and DMARC. For email deliverability specifically, sender authentication and sending history matter far more than the TLD itself; a properly configured .nl sender reaches inboxes normally, and .nl's clean reputation is, if anything, a mild asset rather than a liability.

## Branding and naming tips

- **Keep it ASCII.** No accented or IDN characters are supported, so plan Dutch-language names with that constraint in mind.
- **Lean into "NL" as a trust cue.** For Netherlands-facing commerce or services, the ccTLD itself signals local legitimacy.
- **Expect competition on short names.** With over 6 million names registered since 1986, exact-match short Dutch words are scarce.
- **Consider .nl alongside .com.** Many international brands run .nl as a localized storefront next to a global .com.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone register a .nl domain?

Yes. SIDN has allowed open registration since 2003, with no residency or local-presence requirement. Registrants outside the Netherlands automatically get a SIDN-provided domicile address on file so that legal documents can still be formally served, but this does not block or restrict who can register.

### Does a .nl domain affect SEO?

Google treats ccTLDs like .nl as country-targeted by default, so a .nl domain signals to Google that a site is intended for a Dutch audience. That is an advantage for businesses targeting the Netherlands and a limitation for a brand chasing a global, English-first audience.

### Who runs the .nl domain?

SIDN (Stichting Internet Domeinregistratie Nederland), a foundation based in Arnhem. It has managed .nl registrations since 1996, taking over from Piet Beertema, who personally registered the first roughly 10,000 .nl domains by hand starting in 1986.

### Does .nl support accented characters or IDNs?

No. Under SIDN's technical requirements, .nl domain names may only use the letters a to z, the digits 0 to 9, and hyphens — accented characters and other internationalized domain name (IDN) scripts are not supported.
