The **.au domain** is Australia's official [country-code top-level domain](/en/glossary/cctld/) (ccTLD). For decades, an Australian web address meant `.com.au`; since March 2022, it can also mean a shorter **direct .au** name (`yourname.au`). Both paths run through the same gate — the **Australian Presence** requirement — administered by the policy body **auDA** and operated on the back end by **Identity Digital**. This page covers what .au is, exactly who is eligible, how direct .au registration works, and the honest trade-offs on pricing, SEO, and reputation.

## What is .au?

**.au** is the [ccTLD](/en/glossary/cctld/) delegated to Australia under the ISO 3166-1 country-code system used by [IANA](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/au.html). Unlike a generic top-level domain such as [.com](/en/tld/com/), it has no [ICANN](/en/glossary/icann/) Registry Agreement — ccTLDs are governed by the national manager's own rules. IANA lists **.au Domain Administration (auDA)** as the ccTLD manager: a not-for-profit, industry self-regulatory body endorsed by the Australian government. auDA does not run the technical registry itself; **Identity Digital Australia** (formerly Afilias Australia) has held the operator contract since a 2017 tender, reappointed after a 2023 Request for Tender, with the current agreement running from July 1, 2024.

Because .au functions as a genuine national namespace, Google treats it as country-targeted rather than generic: it is absent from [Google Search Central's short list of ccTLDs treated as generic](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites?hl=en), unlike vanity extensions such as [.tv](/en/tld/tv/) or [.me](/en/tld/me/).

## History of .au

- **Early era.** `.com.au` and the other second-level namespaces (`net.au`, `org.au`, `edu.au`, `gov.au`, `id.au`, `asn.au`) were the only way to register under .au for most of the TLD's history, with auDA formalizing industry self-regulation over time.
- **2017 registry transformation.** auDA ran a global tender for the technical back end; Afilias Australia (now Identity Digital Australia) won and took over registry operations.
- **March 24, 2022 — direct .au launch.** auDA opened **direct second-level registration**, letting registrants apply for `yourname.au` instead of a third-level name like `yourname.com.au`.
- **March–September 2022 — Priority Allocation Period.** Existing holders of a matching `.com.au`, `.net.au`, or other established .au name had roughly six months of priority to claim the exact-match direct name, split into two categories by original registration date, before contested names moved to open eligibility.
- **2023 — registry re-tender.** Identity Digital Australia was reappointed as technical operator, with the new agreement taking effect July 1, 2024.

## How people use .au

- **Australian small and mid-size businesses** using `.com.au` as their primary commercial address, often alongside a shorter direct `.au`.
- **Startups and tech companies** picking up short, brandable direct `.au` names unlocked by the 2022 launch.
- **Non-profits, associations, and government-adjacent bodies** using the matching `.org.au` / `.gov.au` / `.asn.au` structures.
- **International brands with Australian trade marks**, who can qualify for direct .au via the exact-match trade mark presence category even without local incorporation.
- **Domain hacks and short brandables** exploiting the shorter direct-.au string versus the longer `.com.au`.

**Who it's not ideal for:** anyone without a genuine Australian Presence — the requirement blocks straightforward registration for individuals or businesses with no Australian citizenship, residency, ABN/ACN, or matching Australian trade mark — and global-first brands where an Australia-specific geo signal is unwanted.

## Notable sites using .au

Direct `.au` is still young relative to the decades-old `.com.au` namespace, so most large Australian organizations continue to anchor their primary presence on `.com.au` while increasingly also holding the matching direct `.au` name defensively. Rather than name specific brands whose domain choices can change, the durable and verifiable fact is structural: auDA's Priority Allocation Process existed specifically because so many existing `.com.au` and `.net.au` holders wanted the matching direct `.au` name — strong evidence of real, active organizational adoption rather than speculative registration.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Eligibility | Australian Presence required | Australian Presence required | Open to all |

Pick `.com` for a global, non-geographic brand; pick `.com.au` for the long-established, maximally recognizable Australian address; pick direct `.au` when you qualify under the Australian Presence rule and want the shortest possible Australian domain, or to defensively match your existing `.com.au` name.

## Why choose .au?

- **Clear Australian identity** — instantly recognizable as a local address to Australian customers and partners.
- **Shorter option now available** — direct `.au` gives an alternative to the longer `.com.au` string without losing the geo signal.
- **Gated namespace** — the Australian Presence requirement keeps the zone tied to real, verifiable registrants.
- **Broad IDN support** — five non-Latin scripts are supported at the registry level.

## Things to consider

- **Presence gate.** You must document citizenship/residency, an active ABN/ACN, or an exact-match Australian trade mark; a company with no Australian tie cannot register directly.
- **Ongoing compliance.** Presence must be maintained for the full licence period — if an ABN lapses, the licence can be cancelled.
- **Two competing conventions.** Businesses must choose (or hold both) between the legacy `.com.au` and the newer direct `.au`.
- **Geo-targeted, not global.** The Australia signal that builds local trust works against ranking for a worldwide audience.

## Who can register a .au domain?

**Registration restrictions: gated by the Australian Presence requirement.** Under auDA's [.au Domain Administration Rules: Licensing](https://www.auda.org.au/au-domain-names/au-rules-and-policies/au-domain-administration-rules-licensing-2/), eligibility spans roughly 18 defined categories, including:

- **Australian citizens** and **permanent-resident visa holders**.
- **Companies registered under the Corporations Act**, or entities holding an active **Australian Business Number (ABN)**.
- **Foreign companies with an Australian Registered Body Number (ARBN)**.
- **Associations, cooperatives, charities, political parties, and Indigenous corporations** registered in Australia.
- **Owners or applicants of an Australian trade mark**, limited to an exact-match domain name — the route that lets some non-resident trade mark holders qualify.

Direct `.au` registration follows the same Australian Presence test; its 2022 priority window has since closed, and new direct-.au names are now allocated on standard first-come, first-served terms among eligible applicants. auDA supports IDN registrations in five scripts, and [DNSSEC](/en/glossary/dnssec) is available. Presence must be maintained for the entire licence period, or the registration can be cancelled. The binding rules are set out in auDA's [Licensing rules](https://www.auda.org.au/au-domain-names/au-rules-and-policies/au-domain-administration-rules-licensing-2/).

## .au pricing and value

.au pricing dynamics follow the pattern common to gated ccTLDs: **first-year and renewal pricing can differ**, so an attractive first-year rate is not necessarily the long-term cost — budget for the renewal. Because eligibility is gated, .au lacks the large open premium-name marketplace of unrestricted gTLDs; where premium pricing exists it is generally registrar-set for short or high-demand strings, with cost otherwise driven by string length and registrar margin. We list no figures here; check current rates at the point of purchase.

## Reputation and email deliverability

Because .au requires a verified Australian Presence for every registration, the namespace has not developed the bulk-registration spam reputation documented on some wide-open new gTLDs. auDA's ongoing compliance checks — including cancelling licences when an ABN lapses — add friction that discourages disposable registrations, and mail providers generally treat .au and `.com.au` no differently from other established national ccTLDs. Correctly configuring SPF, DKIM, and DMARC remains standard practice for reliable delivery.

## Branding and naming tips

- **Decide between `.com.au` and direct `.au` deliberately** — the shorter direct name reads cleaner for a startup or app, while `.com.au` still carries more default trust with established Australian consumers.
- **Claim the matching pair where it matters** — many organizations hold both the legacy `.com.au` and the new direct `.au` to protect their brand.
- **Use the Australian signal on purpose** — .au is a strong local-trust cue; lean into it if your audience is Australia-first.
- **Mind the exact-match trade mark rule** — if qualifying via a trade mark, the domain name must match that mark exactly, which constrains creative spelling.

## How to register a .au domain

1. **Search** for your desired `.au` or `.com.au` name to check availability.
2. **Confirm eligibility** under the Australian Presence requirement — citizenship/residency, ABN/ACN, or an exact-match Australian trade mark.
3. **Register** and configure [DNS](/en/glossary/dns) to point your site or email.


## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone register a .au domain?

No. Registering any .au domain, including .com.au or direct .au, requires an Australian Presence — evidence such as Australian citizenship, permanent residency, an active ABN or ACN, or an exact-match Australian trade mark. There is no open, presence-free registration.

### Does a .au domain affect SEO?

Yes. Google treats .au as a country-code TLD geo-targeted to Australia, since it is not on Google's short list of ccTLDs treated as generic. That helps Australian-focused sites and works against sites aiming for a global, non-Australian audience.

### What is the difference between .com.au and direct .au?

Both require the same Australian Presence test. .com.au is the long-established, commercially oriented second-level namespace; direct .au (registering yourname.au) launched in March 2022 as a shorter alternative, with an initial priority period letting existing .com.au or .net.au holders claim the matching direct name first.

### Who runs the .au domain?

auDA (.au Domain Administration) is the industry self-regulatory body that sets policy for .au and is listed as the ccTLD manager in the IANA root-zone database. auDA contracts the technical registry operations to Identity Digital Australia, which has run the back end since 2018 and was reappointed after a 2023 tender.
