The **.cc** domain is one of the internet's most enduring "borrowed" country codes — a national namespace that grew into a short, brandable, global extension. Officially the [ccTLD](/en/glossary/cctld/) for the **Cocos (Keeling) Islands**, a tiny Australian territory in the Indian Ocean, .cc has been marketed worldwide for decades as a flexible stand-in for ".com." The string "cc" is endlessly interpretable — **commercial company**, **creative commons**, **carbon copy**, or simply a clean pair of matching initials — which makes it a favorite for memorable names and clever [domain hacks](/en/glossary/domain-hack/).

This guide covers what .cc really is, who runs it, who can register one, how it is priced, and how it is perceived for SEO and email — so you can decide whether it fits your brand.

## What is .cc?

**.cc** is the country-code [Top-Level Domain](/en/glossary/tld/) (ccTLD) assigned to the **Cocos (Keeling) Islands** under the ISO 3166-1 standard, the same two-letter system the [Internet Assigned Numbers Authority (IANA)](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/cc.html) uses to delegate country codes. On paper it is the national namespace of a remote Australian external territory of a few hundred residents in the Indian Ocean.

Google lists .cc among the country-code TLDs it treats as generic. The classification does not guarantee indexing or ranking. Content, language, and other search signals still matter. [Google Search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites)

## IANA identifies the registry manager

The [IANA record](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/cc.html) names eNIC Cocos (Keeling) Islands Pty. Ltd., doing business as Island Internet Services, as the manager. IANA lists Verisign Global Registry Services as the technical contact. [Verisign markets .cc](https://www.verisign.com/domain-names/cc-domains/) directly on its domain-name product site. The delegation record identifies the manager. Registration conditions come from the applicable operator and registrar agreements.

## How people use .cc

Because "cc" maps onto so many readings, .cc attracts a broad, branding-driven crowd:

- **Short, memorable brands** whose preferred [.com](/en/tld/com/) is unavailable or unaffordable, and who want a punchy two-letter suffix.
- **Domain hacks**, where the suffix completes or abbreviates a word or phrase — one of the most common reasons people reach for .cc. (See our guide to [domain hacks](/en/blog/domain-hacks-explained/).)
- **Creative and "creative commons" projects**, where "cc" carries a fitting connotation.
- **Communities, link shorteners, and redirect domains**, where a concise suffix keeps URLs compact.
- **Personal and portfolio sites** that want a tidy, distinctive alternative to .com.

**Who it's not ideal for:** Brands that absolutely cannot tolerate any confusion with their .com twin, or audiences who will reliably expect ".com" and may mistype the address.

## Notable sites using .cc

[Arduino uses arduino.cc](https://www.arduino.cc/) for its official website, including links to products, documentation, and its community. The example shows a technical project using .cc. One project’s choice does not establish reputation, availability, or search performance for other .cc domains.

## .cc vs other domains

| Feature | .cc | [.com](/en/tld/com/) | [.co](/en/tld/co/) | [.io](/en/tld/io/) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Type | ccTLD (used globally) | Legacy gTLD | ccTLD (used globally) | ccTLD (used globally) |
| Core association | Flexible / matching initials | The default web standard | Company / corporation | Tech / "Input-Output" |

Choose **[.com](/en/tld/com/)** when you can get the exact name — it remains the trust default. Reach for **.cc** when you want a short, flexible, brandable suffix and a clever construction or domain hack, especially if the .com is taken. Consider **[.co](/en/tld/co/)** when you specifically want "company" connotations, and **[.io](/en/tld/io/)** for developer- and infrastructure-focused tech brands.

## Why choose .cc?

- **Short and brandable.** Two clean letters that read as matching initials make for compact, memorable URLs.
- **Flexible meaning.** "cc" bends to fit — commercial company, creative commons, carbon copy, or whatever your brand wants it to say.
- Check the exact name and renewal price before payment.
- Google lists .cc among the country-code TLDs it treats as generic. The classification does not guarantee indexing or ranking. Content, language, and other search signals still matter.
- **Backed by Verisign.** It runs on the same robust registry infrastructure as .com and .net.

## Things to consider

- **Confusion with .com.** Some visitors will instinctively expect ".com," so a memorable, unambiguous name matters.
- **A ccTLD by nature.** Because .cc technically belongs to the Cocos (Keeling) Islands and is administered under that delegation, its long-term policy is set by the sponsoring operator rather than by an [ICANN](/en/glossary/icann/) gTLD registry agreement.
- **No built-in meaning.** The flexibility of "cc" is also a slight cost: unlike ".com" or ".co," it does not pre-load a single clear association, so the brand has to supply the meaning.

## Who can register a .cc domain?

[Verisign offers .cc through registrars](https://www.verisign.com/domain-names/cc-domains/). [WebNIC states that individuals and businesses in any country can register](https://faq.webnic.cc/kb/cc/). WebNIC lists registration terms of 1–10 years and names of 1–63 characters. Those are WebNIC’s published service conditions. Confirm your chosen registrar’s agreement, supported characters, contact-data requirements, renewal deadline, and transfer conditions before payment.

Verisign’s [registrar FAQ](https://www.verisign.com/resources/become-a-registrar) also describes registration terms of 1–10 years. Registration availability does not establish trademark rights. A search result does not reserve a name. Read the applicable agreement before registering.

## .cc pricing and value

Compare the first registration charge, annual renewal charge, transfer charge, and any restoration charge for the exact name. Check taxes and included services separately. A low first-year offer does not establish the long-term cost.

A seller’s aftermarket asking price differs from registry or registrar registration fees. Do not infer a special recurring registry fee from a listing marked “premium.” Obtain the exact renewal quote before buying. This guide does not assert that .cc names follow a registry premium-renewal schedule.

## Reputation and email deliverability

A registry operator does not guarantee a website’s trustworthiness or an email’s delivery. Assess the individual website or sender rather than relying on the suffix.

For mail, follow the recipient provider’s requirements. [Gmail’s sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126) cover authentication, DNS, spam rates, and additional requirements for bulk senders. SPF, DKIM, and DMARC support authentication. Authentication alone does not guarantee inbox placement. Test the intended mail service before moving business email.

## Branding and naming tips

- **Lean into the initials.** .cc shines when "cc" doubles as your brand's initials or a natural abbreviation.
- **Build a clever hack.** Names that read across the dot, or compact two-letter constructions before it, can produce memorable URLs.
- **Watch the .com twin.** Before committing, consider who owns the matching .com — if a competitor holds it, weigh the potential for confusion.
- **Say it out loud.** Pick a name that is unambiguous when spoken, so "dot cc" lands clearly in conversation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone register a .cc domain?

Verisign offers .cc through registrars. WebNIC currently accepts individuals and businesses from any country. Confirm the current registration agreement with your chosen registrar.

### Does a .cc domain affect SEO?

Google lists .cc among the country-code TLDs it treats as generic. The classification does not guarantee indexing or ranking. Content, language, and other search signals still matter.

### What does "cc" stand for?

Officially "cc" is the ISO country code for the Cocos (Keeling) Islands. In practice it is read flexibly as "commercial company," "creative commons," "carbon copy," or simply a memorable pair of matching initials, which is exactly why it is popular as a short, brandable extension.

### Who operates the .cc domain?

The .cc registry is operated by eNIC Cocos (Keeling) Islands Pty. Ltd. (doing business as Island Internet Services), a wholly owned subsidiary of Verisign. The IANA root-zone database lists eNIC / Island Internet Services as the sponsoring organization and registry manager.
