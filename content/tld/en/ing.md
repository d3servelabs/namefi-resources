The **.ing domain** is a generic top-level domain built around a linguistic trick: in English, most present participles and gerunds end in "-ing," so a name like `design.ing`, `bank.ing`, or `edit.ing` reads as a complete word split naturally between the second-level name and the suffix. Operated by Google's [Charleston Road Registry](https://www.registry.google), .ing is a **domain hack** TLD first — brands from Canva to ING Bank itself already use it that way. This page covers where .ing came from, who is using it, what it costs to weigh up, and the honest trade-offs.

## What is .ing?

.ing is a [generic top-level domain](/en/glossary/tld) (gTLD), not a country-code TLD, so it carries no geographic association. Its entire value proposition is wordplay: because so many English words end in "-ing," registering `word.ing` frequently spells out a real term — `go.ing`, `mak.ing`, `adapt.ing`. Google's own registry describes it plainly: build your website "in a single word with .ing." You can confirm the delegation record and operator in the [IANA root-zone database entry for .ing](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/ing.html).

Because .ing is a generic extension, [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites#:~:text=Generic%20top-level%20domains%20(gTLDs)%20are%20domains%20that%20aren't%20associated%20with%20specific%20locations.) confirms gTLDs "aren't associated with specific locations," so .ing carries no automatic geo-targeting signal and no inherent ranking boost or penalty.

## History of .ing

- **2014 — contracted but dormant.** Google's Charleston Road Registry signed the [ICANN Registry Agreement for .ing](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/ing) on January 23, 2014, as part of the 2012 New gTLD Program round, and the string was delegated to the root zone on June 12, 2014. Like many 2012-round strings, it then sat unlaunched for nearly a decade while Google built out its registry business.
- **2023 — public launch.** Google Registry opened .ing on a phased schedule: a trademark-holder [sunrise period](/en/glossary/tmch/) from September 20 to October 24, 2023, an early access period from October 31 to December 5, and full [general availability from December 5, 2023](https://www.registry.google/announcements/launch-details-for-ing-and-meme/).
- **2024 — early traction.** By its first anniversary, Google Registry reported [over 20,000 .ing registrations and "thousands of new websites"](https://www.registry.google/announcements/ing-one-year/) since launch — modest by legacy-TLD standards, but notable adoption for a domain-hack extension roughly a year old.

## How people use .ing

Real, specific niches where .ing shows up:

- **Product and feature pages spelling out an action** — `design.ing`, `edit.ing`, `sign.ing` for tools that do exactly that verb.
- **Travel and lifestyle brands** — names like `go.ing` lean into motion and momentum.
- **Personal portfolio and creator sites** — professionals building a one-word gerund identity (`surf.ing`, `ink.ing`).
- **Corporate self-defense and brand protection** — established companies whose name already ends in "ing" (banks, engineering firms) securing the exact-match string.
- **Community and trade-association sites** — organizations spelling out their field of work in the URL itself.

**Who it's not ideal for:** names that don't naturally end in "-ing" — forcing the pattern (adding "ing" where it doesn't belong) reads as gimmicky rather than clever, and undercuts the whole appeal of the suffix.

Pick .com when universal trust matters most; pick [.io](/en/tld/io) for a tech-sector signal; pick [.app](/en/tld/app) for a mobile or web app with mandatory HTTPS; pick .ing when your name genuinely ends in "-ing" and the domain-hack reading does real branding work.

## Why choose .ing?

- **Genuine wordplay, not a stretch.** Unlike many domain hacks that require awkward abbreviation, .ing completes an actual English word for any "-ing" verb or noun.
- **Built-in security.** Like all Google Registry TLDs, .ing is on the [HSTS preload list](https://get.ing/), so browsers enforce HTTPS on every .ing connection without any per-site configuration.
- **Open registration.** No sunrise-only gatekeeping today — anyone can register.
- **Availability.** Short, exact-match names are still findable, unlike in three-decade-old zones.

## Things to consider

- **Narrow naming fit.** The suffix only works cleanly when your brand word already ends in "-ing" — forcing it elsewhere looks contrived.
- **Newer, lower recognition.** Users unfamiliar with the extension may hesitate before it earns the instant trust of .com.
- **Small installed base.** At roughly 20,000 registrations after its first year, .ing is still a niche extension rather than a mainstream default.
- **Confusable rendering.** In some fonts or at small sizes, "ing" can be misread; test your specific name before committing.

## Who can register a .ing domain?

**Registration restrictions: open to all.** .ing is an unrestricted generic TLD with no credential, professional-membership, community, or local-presence requirement. Any individual or organization worldwide can register through an accredited [registrar](/en/glossary/registrar).

The trademark sunrise period ran September 20–October 24, 2023, so today registrations are first-come, first-served. [DNSSEC](/en/glossary/dnssec) is supported, and [WHOIS privacy](/en/glossary/whois-privacy/) is available through most registrars. Standard ICANN lifecycle protections apply, including auto-renew grace and redemption-grace periods, so a lapsed name is not instantly re-available. The binding rules are set out in the [ICANN Registry Agreement for .ing](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/ing) and Google Registry's [Domain Registration Policy](https://www.registry.google/policies/).

## .ing pricing and value

.ing pricing dynamics follow the pattern common to newer Google gTLDs: **first-year promotional pricing typically differs from the standing renewal rate**, so budget for the renewal rather than the introductory offer. The registry also designates certain **premium names** (short, dictionary "-ing" words, high-demand strings) at elevated registration and renewal pricing. The main cost drivers are string length, dictionary-word status, premium classification, and registrar margin. We list no figures here — check current rates at the point of purchase.

## Reputation and email deliverability

.ing does not carry the abuse reputation that dogs some ultra-cheap new gTLDs, and its adopter list — Canva, ING Bank, Adobe — leans toward established brands rather than disposable spam operations. As a Google Registry TLD, .ing is [included on the HSTS preload list](https://get.ing/), meaning browsers require HTTPS on every .ing site by default, which removes one common attack surface (unencrypted connections) outright. Because the extension is still young, mail providers and spam filters have less historical signal to score it on either way; standard email hygiene (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) applies as it would for any newer domain.

## Branding and naming tips

- **Say it out loud first.** The hack only lands if `word.ing` reads naturally as English — test it on someone unfamiliar with your brand.
- **Prefer real verbs and gerunds.** `bank.ing`, `edit.ing`, and `design.ing` work because they're genuine words; invented endings feel forced.
- **Watch letterforms.** Double-check "ing" doesn't visually collide with adjacent letters in your chosen typeface.
- **Keep the root short.** The shorter the word before ".ing," the more the wordplay pops.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone register a .ing domain?

Yes. .ing is an open, unrestricted generic top-level domain operated by Charleston Road Registry, a Google subsidiary. There are no credential, membership, or local-presence requirements, so anyone worldwide can register a .ing name through an accredited registrar.

### Does a .ing domain affect SEO?

No. Google treats .ing as a generic top-level domain with no built-in ranking boost or penalty and no geographic association, the same as .com or .xyz. Any ranking benefit comes from the site itself, not the extension.

### Is .ing good for a domain hack?

Yes, that is its main draw. Because .ing matches the English gerund/present-participle ending, words like design, edit, or bank naturally split into word.ing, letting brands spell out a full verb or noun as the second-level plus TLD.

### Who should register a .ing domain?

It suits brands, products, and personal sites whose name genuinely ends in "-ing" — action verbs, gerunds, or words like "king" or "ring." It is a weaker fit for names that need to force an ending that does not naturally occur.
