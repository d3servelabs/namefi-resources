The **.news domain** is an open generic top-level domain built specifically for journalism and news publishing. Operated by **Dog Beach, LLC**, part of the [Identity Digital](https://www.identity.digital/) registry family with roots in the old Rightside/Donuts registry lineage, .news has been live since 2015 and is used by outlets as different as Apple's own news product and independent subscription journalism. This page covers where .news came from, who actually uses it, and — because the string implies credibility it doesn't actually confer — the reputation angle buyers should understand.

## What is .news?

.news is a [generic top-level domain](/en/glossary/tld) (gTLD), unrelated to any country or region, approved through [ICANN](/en/glossary/icann)'s 2012 New gTLD Program. As with any descriptive gTLD, the string itself carries only a naming signal, not an editorial guarantee — the registry sets no journalistic standard for who can register.

Because it's a generic string, Google applies no geo-targeting rule to .news — it's evaluated like any other gTLD. You can confirm the delegation record directly in the [IANA root-zone entry for .news](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/news.html).

Google also states that [keywords in a TLD do not give any advantage or disadvantage in search](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2015/07/googles-handling-of-new-top-level?hl=en#:~:text=Keywords%20in%20a%20TLD%20do%20not%20give%20any%20advantage%20or%20disadvantage%20in%20search.), so the extension itself receives no inherent ranking boost or penalty.

## History of .news

- **Delegation (2015):** .news was delegated to the root zone on **March 16, 2015** (registration date March 12, 2015), initially assigned to **United TLD Holdco, Ltd** — a Donuts Inc. entity from the same 2012-round application wave that later became known through the Rightside/Donuts registry brand. The underlying [ICANN Registry Agreement for .news](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/news) was signed **December 18, 2014**.
- **Transfer to Dog Beach (2021):** IANA recorded the transfer of .news to **Dog Beach, LLC** on **June 2, 2021**, consolidating it under the Donuts/Ethos Capital registry umbrella alongside sibling strings.
- **Identity Digital era (2021–present):** Following Ethos Capital's 2021 acquisition of Donuts and its 2022 merger with Afilias, the combined company rebranded as **Identity Digital**, which operates Dog Beach — and .news — today.

## How people use .news

- **Independent and subscription journalism** — several notable outlets built around paid, opinionated, or niche coverage use .news as their primary domain.
- **News aggregation and media-bias tools** — services that compile or rate coverage across outlets.
- **Trade and vertical newsletters** — sector-specific reporting (crypto, policy, tech) that outgrew a generic .com.
- **Legacy media companies' digital extensions** — some established newsrooms use .news for a spinoff product or app-linked domain.
- **Platform-level news products** — at least one major technology company points a dedicated news product at a .news address.

**Who it's not ideal for:** general content, lifestyle, or opinion brands that aren't specifically in the news-reporting business — the suffix sets a specific expectation that a broader content site may not satisfy.

## Notable sites using .news

- **Apple News** (`apple.news`) — Apple routes its Apple News product through its own `.news` domain, a strong signal of the extension's technical legitimacy.
- **Ground News** (`ground.news`) — a news-aggregation platform that rates outlets and stories for political bias and factuality.
- **Puck** (`puck.news`) — a subscription journalism outlet covering media, politics, Hollywood, and Wall Street through named writers.
- **Platformer** (`platformer.news`) — Casey Newton's independent tech-industry journalism newsletter and site.

Pick [.com](/en/tld/com/) when universal trust matters most and it's available. Pick [.media](/en/tld/media/) when the identity is broader content or production rather than news specifically. Pick [.blog](/en/tld/blog/) for a personal or editorial voice rather than a news operation. Pick .news when the site's core product is reported news coverage.

## Why choose .news?

- **Immediate, accurate meaning.** ".news" tells a visitor exactly what kind of site they're on before they read a headline.
- **Open and unrestricted.** No accreditation or eligibility checks — any publisher, journalist, or platform can register directly.
- **Real, credible adoption.** Apple routing its own news product through `apple.news`, alongside independent outlets like Puck and Platformer, gives the string genuine legitimacy beyond marketing copy.
- **Availability.** Short, on-brand names remain attainable here where the matching .com is long gone.

## Things to consider

- **The suffix implies credibility it doesn't enforce.** Because registration is completely open, a .news domain carries no editorial, fact-checking, or journalistic-standards guarantee — anyone can register one regardless of what they publish.
- **Narrower positioning than .media.** ".news" specifically promises news coverage; a broader content or entertainment brand may be a mismatch.
- **Moderate mainstream recognition.** Many users still default to expecting .com first.

## Who can register a .news domain?

**Registration restrictions: open to all.** .news is an unrestricted generic TLD with no credential, professional-membership, editorial-standards, or local-presence requirement. Any individual or organization worldwide can register through an accredited [registrar](/en/glossary/registrar) — the registry does not vet registrants for journalistic legitimacy.

A trademark sunrise period ran ahead of general availability in 2015, so today registration is first-come, first-served. [DNSSEC](/en/glossary/dnssec) is supported at the registry, and [WHOIS privacy](/en/glossary/whois-privacy/) is available through most registrars. Standard ICANN lifecycle rules apply — auto-renew grace, redemption grace, and pending-delete periods — so a lapsed name doesn't become instantly available again. The binding rules are set out in the [ICANN Registry Agreement for .news](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/news).

## .news pricing and value

.news pricing follows standard new-gTLD dynamics rather than a single number. **First-year and renewal pricing typically differ** — promotional first-year rates are common, while the standing renewal rate is what you pay long term. The registry also reserves certain short, dictionary, or high-demand strings as **premium names**, which can carry higher registration and renewal pricing than standard names. Length, desirability, premium classification, and registrar margin are the main cost drivers. No figures are quoted here — check current rates at the point of purchase.

## Reputation and email deliverability

This is the section buyers most need candor on, because the word "news" itself invites a trust assumption the registry does not back up. .news is an open gTLD with no editorial gatekeeping, so the suffix alone tells a reader nothing about accuracy, sourcing, or bias — that judgment still has to be made outlet by outlet, exactly as with a .com address. On the technical side, .news does not carry the bulk-spam reputation associated with some ultra-low-cost new gTLDs; its visible registrant base — Apple's own news product among them — skews toward legitimate publishers rather than disposable domains. **Mitigation:** for both readers and registrants, treat the suffix as a category label, not a trust signal, and back any .news site with clear sourcing, a real masthead, and (for registrants) properly configured SPF, DKIM, and DMARC for email deliverability.

## Branding and naming tips

- **Let the word carry the category.** ".news" already signals reported journalism, so the second-level name can be short and memorable (`puck.news`, `ground.news`).
- **Avoid redundancy.** Don't pair it with a name that repeats "news," "daily," or "report" unless it reads naturally.
- **Set real editorial expectations.** Because readers may (incorrectly) assume the suffix implies vetted credibility, back it with a visible masthead, bylines, and sourcing.
- **Say it out loud.** Confirm the full domain is easy to say and spell over the phone before committing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone register a .news domain?

Yes. .news is an open, unrestricted generic top-level domain operated by Dog Beach, LLC. There is no credential, membership, journalistic-standards, or local-presence requirement, so any individual or organization worldwide can register a .news name through an accredited registrar.

### Does a .news domain affect SEO?

No. Google treats .news as a generic top-level domain with no automatic ranking boost, penalty, or geographic bias, so a .news site can rank globally exactly like a .com site would.

### Does a .news domain make a site more trustworthy or fact-checked?

No. The registry imposes no journalistic-standards, editorial, or fact-checking requirement on registrants, so the suffix by itself says nothing about accuracy. Judge each .news site on its own sourcing and reputation, the same way you would judge any domain.

### Who should register a .news domain?

Publishers, independent journalists, newsletters, news aggregators, and any outlet whose core product is news coverage. It is a weaker choice for a general content brand that is not specifically about news.
