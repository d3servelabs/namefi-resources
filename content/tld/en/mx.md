The **.mx domain** is Mexico's official [country-code top-level domain](/en/glossary/cctld/) (ccTLD) — and, unusually among major national ccTLDs, one with **no local-presence requirement**. Anyone worldwide can register a `.mx` or `.com.mx` name. Operated by **NIC Mexico** under the trading name **Registry .MX**, with roots tracing back to a technical-university delegation in the 1980s, .mx combines an open eligibility policy with a distinctive category structure. This page covers what .mx is, its history through a 13-year freeze and 2009 reopening, who is actually eligible, and the honest trade-offs on pricing, SEO, and reputation.

## What is .mx?

**.mx** is the [ccTLD](/en/glossary/cctld/) assigned to Mexico under the ISO 3166-1 country-code system used by [IANA](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/mx.html). As with other ccTLDs, .mx has no [ICANN](/en/glossary/icann/) Registry Agreement — governance comes from the national registry's own policy. That registry, **Network Information Center Mexico, S.A. de C.V.**, trades as **Registry .MX** and operates under a delegation originally granted to **ITESM** (Instituto Tecnológico y de Estudios Superiores de Monterrey), Mexico's leading technical university, in November 2003.

What sets .mx apart from most major national ccTLDs is its open eligibility policy: unlike [.ca](/en/tld/ca/), [.au](/en/tld/au/), or [.br](/en/tld/br/), registering `.mx` or `.com.mx` does **not** require Mexican citizenship, residency, or a local presence. Even so, because .mx functions as a genuine national namespace, Google treats it as country-targeted rather than generic — it does not appear on [Google Search Central's short list of ccTLDs treated as generic](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites?hl=en), unlike vanity extensions such as [.tv](/en/tld/tv/) or [.me](/en/tld/me/).

## History of .mx

- **February 1, 1989 — Delegation.** .mx was delegated to Mexico, with early management under ITESM.
- **1996 — Registration freeze.** General .mx registration was effectively halted, and the namespace was reserved largely for academic use for over a decade.
- **November 2003 — ITESM delegation to Registry .MX.** The current operator, Network Information Center, S.A. de C.V., took over the delegation from ITESM's Monterrey campus.
- **2009 — Reopening in three phases.** NIC México relaunched general .mx registration through a structured rollout: a **Sunrise Period** (May 1 – July 31, 2009) letting existing registrants reserve names, a **Waiting Period** (August 1–31, 2009) for processing and disputes, and **Initial Registration** (September 1 – October 31, 2009) before general public availability. This reopening also introduced **direct second-level registration** (`yourname.mx`) alongside the older category structure.
- **Today.** Registry .MX's own live statistics show roughly 1.45 million total .mx domains, split among `.com.mx` (about 67%), direct `.mx` (about 30%), and smaller categories including `.edu.mx`, `.org.mx`, `.gob.mx`, and `.net.mx`.

## How people use .mx

- **Businesses in Mexico**, historically defaulting to `.com.mx` as the standard commercial address.
- **International and cross-border businesses targeting Mexican consumers**, taking advantage of the lack of any local-presence requirement.
- **Startups and shorter-brand seekers**, increasingly using direct `.mx` since its 2009 reopening — now roughly a third of the whole namespace.
- **Government entities**, restricted to the dedicated `.gob.mx` category.
- **Educational institutions**, using `.edu.mx`, a legacy of the TLD's academic origins under ITESM.

**Who it's not ideal for:** organizations needing one of the small number of restricted specialty categories (like `.net.mx`, reserved for telecom/ISP entities registered with Mexico's regulator) without the qualifying credentials, and global-first brands where a Mexico-specific geo signal is unwanted.

## Notable sites using .mx

`.com.mx` remains the dominant category by a wide margin — Registry .MX's own statistics put it at roughly two-thirds of all .mx registrations — making it the practical default for Mexican commercial websites, similar in role to `.com.br` in Brazil. Direct `.mx`, reopened in 2009, has grown to roughly 30% of the namespace, evidence of real, sustained adoption rather than a niche experiment. Because individual site ownership changes over time, this registry-level category split is a more durable, verifiable fact than naming specific brands.

Pick `.com` for a global, non-geographic brand; pick `.mx` (as `.com.mx` or direct `.mx`) when Mexico is your target market — no local presence needed; compare against [.br](/en/tld/br/) if you're weighing another major Latin American ccTLD with a similar category-plus-direct structure but a meaningfully stricter, tax-ID-gated eligibility policy.

## Why choose .mx?

- **No local-presence barrier** — a rare trait among major national ccTLDs; anyone can register regardless of citizenship or location.
- **Strong Mexico-market signal** — a clear SEO and trust cue for a Mexican audience, without any eligibility paperwork.
- **Two viable formats** — the well-established `.com.mx` and the shorter direct `.mx`, both open to the same registrants.
- **Long operating history** — delegated in 1989, with a mature, DNSSEC-supporting registry infrastructure.

## Things to consider

- **No IDN support.** Unlike [.br](/en/tld/br/) or [.ca](/en/tld/ca/), .mx does not support internationalized domain names, so accented Spanish characters (á, é, í, ó, ú, ñ) are not registrable in the domain string itself.
- **Category confusion.** New registrants have to choose between the dominant `.com.mx`, the growing direct `.mx`, and several smaller restricted categories.
- **Restricted specialty categories.** `.net.mx` and `.gob.mx` require Mexico-specific credentials (e.g. telecom regulator registration, government status) even though the general namespace is open.
- **Geo-targeted, not global.** The Mexico-specific SEO signal that builds local relevance works against ranking for a worldwide, non-Mexico-specific audience.

## Who can register a .mx domain?

**Registration restrictions: open to all** for `.com.mx` and direct `.mx` — there is no citizenship, residency, or local-presence requirement, per [Registry .MX's own policy documentation](https://beta.dominios.nic.mx/policies/). Every registration requires providing data for four contact roles: registrant, administrative, technical, and billing. A limited set of specialty categories carry their own additional requirements set out in the registry's category annex — for example, `.net.mx` is intended for telecom/ISP entities and historically required proof of registration with Mexico's telecom regulator, and `.gob.mx` is reserved for government entities.

.mx does **not** support internationalized domain names — registrations are ASCII-only. [DNSSEC](/en/glossary/dnssec) is supported at the registry level. Registration must go through an accredited registrar; the registry does not accept direct public registrations. The authoritative policy documentation is published by [Registry .MX](https://beta.dominios.nic.mx/policies/).

## .mx pricing and value

.mx pricing dynamics follow the common ccTLD pattern: **first-year and renewal pricing can differ**, so a promotional first-year rate is not necessarily what you'll pay ongoing — budget for the renewal. Because eligibility is open rather than gated, cost is driven primarily by category choice (`.com.mx` versus direct `.mx` versus a specialty category), string desirability, and registrar margin, rather than any presence-verification overhead. We list no figures here; check current rates at the point of purchase.

## Reputation and email deliverability

.mx does not carry a documented history of the kind of bulk-abuse flagging seen on some low-cost, unrestricted new gTLDs, and mail providers generally treat `.com.mx` and direct `.mx` no differently from other established national ccTLDs. Because registration has no local-presence gate, the namespace is somewhat more open than a presence-restricted ccTLD like [.br](/en/tld/br/) or [.ca](/en/tld/ca/) — which makes standard hygiene (properly configured SPF, DKIM, and DMARC) worth prioritizing for any commercial sender, as with any open namespace.

## Branding and naming tips

- **Default to `.com.mx` for maximum local recognition**, or choose direct `.mx` for a shorter, modern-feeling name — both are equally legitimate and open to the same registrants.
- **Skip accented characters.** Since .mx has no IDN support, plan names in plain ASCII rather than relying on á, é, í, ó, ú, or ñ.
- **Verify the category before you build a brand around it.** `.net.mx` and `.gob.mx` are restricted; make sure you actually qualify before designing around one of the specialty categories.
- **Lean into the Mexico signal deliberately** if your audience is Mexico-first — the geo-targeting is a feature for local relevance, not a limitation, when used intentionally.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone register a .mx domain?

Yes, in general. Direct .mx and .com.mx registration has no Mexican citizenship, residency, or local-presence requirement, so individuals and businesses worldwide can register. A small number of restricted categories, such as .net.mx and .gob.mx, do require Mexico-specific credentials.

### Does a .mx domain affect SEO?

Yes. Google treats .mx as a country-code TLD geo-targeted to Mexico, since it is not on Google's short list of ccTLDs treated as generic. That helps Mexico-focused sites rank for Mexican searches and works against sites targeting a global audience.

### What is the difference between .com.mx and direct .mx?

Both are open to registrants worldwide. .com.mx is the larger, longer-established commercial category; direct .mx (yourname.mx, no category) reopened for general registration in 2009 and has grown to roughly 30% of all .mx registrations according to Registry .MX's own statistics.

### Who runs the .mx domain?

Network Information Center Mexico, S.A. de C.V. ("Registry .MX") operates the registry under a delegation originally granted to ITESM (Instituto Tecnologico y de Estudios Superiores de Monterrey) in 2003. IANA lists NIC-Mexico as the ccTLD manager.
