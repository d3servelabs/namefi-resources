The **.inc domain** spells out the word most American companies already have in their legal name — "incorporated" — as a top-level domain in its own right. Delegated in the 2012 New gTLD Program and operated by Intercap Registry, it launched in 2019 positioned squarely at the premium end of the market: a short, literal corporate suffix rather than a budget alternative. This page covers .inc's real launch history, who actually operates and uses it, and the honest trade-offs of registering `yourbrand.inc`.

## What is .inc?

.inc is a [generic top-level domain](/en/glossary/gtld) delegated through ICANN's 2012 New gTLD Program. As a generic suffix with no country tie, Google's [multi-regional site guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites#:~:text=A%20generic%20top-level%20domain%20%28gTLD%29) treats it exactly like any other gTLD — no automatic geo-targeting, and no inherent SEO advantage from the string itself.

The registry is [Intercap Registry Inc.](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/inc.html), a Cayman Islands company that also runs the related [.dealer](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/dealer.html) and .box extensions, with CentralNic providing back-end registry infrastructure. You can confirm the current sponsoring organization and delegation record directly in the [IANA root-zone entry for .inc](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/inc.html).

## History of .inc

- **10 March 2018 — ICANN Registry Agreement signed.** Intercap Registry Inc. executed a standard "Base, Non-Sponsored" agreement with ICANN, confirmed in the [ICANN Registry Agreement details for .inc](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/inc).
- **3 July 2018 — delegated to the root zone**, per the IANA delegation record.
- **27 March 2019 – 30 April 2019 — sunrise period.** Trademark holders with a record in the [Trademark Clearinghouse](/en/glossary/tmch/) could register first, as announced in the registry's own [launch release](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2019/03/27/1773783/0/en/inc-Launches-as-the-Domain-Ending-that-Means-Business.html).
- **30 April – 7 May 2019 — priority access period**, allowing organizations to secure name variants ahead of full public release.
- **7 May 2019, 12:00 ET — general availability.** .inc opened to any registrant worldwide, as confirmed in the registry's [general-availability announcement](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2019/05/07/1818693/0/en/Over-20-Percent-of-Forbes-Most-Valuable-Brands-Have-Purchased-inc-Domains.html), which reported that more than 20% of Fortune US 100 companies and Forbes' 100 World's Most Valuable Brands had already registered a .inc domain on launch day.

## How people use .inc

- **Primary corporate homepage.** Some incorporated companies run their main site directly on `.inc`.
- **Investor-relations pages.** Public companies pointing an investor-facing subdomain or redirect at a .inc address, distinct from their main .com.
- **Brand-defensive registration.** Large corporations registering their name in .inc primarily to prevent squatting or lookalike use, then redirecting it to their main domain.
- **New incorporations wanting a distinctive address.** Startups and newly formed companies choosing `yourbrand.inc` as a short, literal alternative when the matching .com is unavailable.

**Who it's not ideal for:** solo freelancers, early-stage side projects, or anyone price-sensitive — .inc's premium positioning (see pricing section below) makes it a deliberate choice for established or well-funded entities, not a default budget pick.

## Notable sites using .inc

Unlike many 2012-round gTLDs, .inc has a documented and verifiable list of notable adopters, published directly by the [registry itself](https://www.get.inc/companies). Confirmed uses include:

- **Primary homepage use** by companies including Safe Superintelligence Inc. and Combustion Inc.
- **Investor-relations redirects** by public companies including eBay, Fox, PayPal, Docebo, and Globe Life.
- **Brand-protection registrations** by major corporations including AWS, BMW, Facebook, Mastercard, Morgan Stanley, and Walmart.
- **Redirect use** by household names including IBM, Intel, Twitch, WordPress, and Old Navy.

This is a meaningfully different adoption profile than a typical budget new gTLD — .inc's registrant base skews toward established, often publicly traded, companies.

## Why choose .inc?

- **Literal, unambiguous meaning.** "Inc." is already in many US companies' legal names — the domain matches the entity name exactly.
- **Signals established-company positioning.** Because of its pricing tier, a `.inc` address itself can read as a deliberate, considered choice rather than an impulse registration.
- **Verified adoption by recognizable brands.** The registry's published adopter list, not marketing copy, backs up the "corporate identity" positioning.
- **Short and clean.** Three letters, no ambiguity, works well as a primary or investor-relations address.

## Things to consider

- **Premium pricing limits the buyer pool.** [Industry coverage at launch](https://domainnamewire.com/2018/06/12/llc-and-inc-domain-names-are-on-their-way/) specifically noted .inc's flat, elevated pricing would exclude casual or budget registrants — factor this into planning rather than assuming standard-gTLD economics.
- **Registration does not require incorporation.** Anyone can register `.inc`, including entities that are not actually incorporated, so the suffix itself is not legal proof of corporate status.
- **Smaller registrant base than mainstream gTLDs.** .inc's adoption, while notable among large brands, remains a fraction of the size of .com or even mid-size new gTLDs.
- **Some visitors may not recognize the extension yet.** As with any newer gTLD, expect a short trust-building period versus .com.

## Who can register a .inc domain?

**Registration restrictions: open to all.** There is no requirement to be legally incorporated, hold a trademark, or demonstrate local presence — [Intercap's registry agreement with ICANN](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/inc) establishes .inc as a standard, non-sponsored gTLD. A sunrise period for [Trademark Clearinghouse](/en/glossary/tmch/) holders ran from late March to end of April 2019, followed by a priority-access phase, before general availability opened on 7 May 2019 — so all phases are long closed and registration today is first-come, first-served. Standard ICANN lifecycle rules apply, including auto-renew grace, redemption grace, and pending-delete periods, and [WHOIS privacy](/en/glossary/whois-privacy/) is available through most [registrars](/en/glossary/registrar).

## .inc pricing and value

.inc's defining pricing characteristic, without quoting figures, is that it sits at the **high end of the gTLD market** — flat and elevated rather than following the typical low first-year, higher-renewal pattern of many new gTLDs. Coverage at launch specifically flagged this as a deliberate positioning choice that narrows the realistic buyer pool to companies and brand-protection budgets rather than individual registrants. As with any TLD, confirm current registration and renewal rates with your registrar before buying — pricing structure, not any specific number, is the takeaway here.

## Reputation and email deliverability

.inc has no documented history of spam or abuse concerns. Its registrant base — driven by its price positioning and adoption by publicly traded companies — skews toward legitimate corporate use rather than the bulk, low-cost registration patterns that tend to attract abuse on cheaper new gTLDs. Ordinary email-authentication hygiene (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) still applies regardless of suffix, but .inc does not carry the elevated-scrutiny reputation that some budget extensions do.

## Branding and naming tips

- **Use it as a literal corporate suffix.** `yourbrand.inc` reads cleanly when your actual legal name includes "Incorporated" or "Inc."
- **Consider it for investor-relations or brand-defense use** even if your primary domain stays on .com — the registry's own adopter data shows this is a common real-world pattern.
- **Don't rely on it to signal legal status to customers.** Since registration doesn't verify incorporation, pair the domain with your actual entity name in visible copy.
- **Weigh the cost against the audience.** Its premium pricing makes the most sense once a brand has real revenue or funding behind it, not for a pre-launch side project.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone register a .inc domain?

Yes. .inc is an open generic top-level domain with no requirement to be legally incorporated, hold a trademark, or prove local presence. Any individual or organization worldwide can register one through an ICANN-accredited registrar.

### Does a .inc domain affect SEO?

No. Google treats .inc as a generic top-level domain with no inherent ranking boost or geographic bias. Rankings depend on content quality, links, and user experience, not the suffix.

### Why is .inc priced so much higher than most gTLDs?

Intercap Registry positions .inc as a premium, brand-protection-oriented extension aimed at established companies rather than a budget alternative, and prices it well above typical new gTLDs accordingly. This page does not quote figures; check current registrar pricing directly.

### Who should register a .inc domain?

Incorporated companies, especially those wanting a short, literal "yourbrand.inc" corporate address, and brand-protection teams at larger organizations defending their name across relevant extensions. It is a weaker fit for solo freelancers or early-stage projects on a tight budget.

### Do major companies actually use .inc domains?

Yes. The registry's own published adopter list includes redirect or investor-relations use by companies such as eBay, Fox, PayPal, IBM, Mastercard, and Morgan Stanley, and brand-defensive registrations by companies including AWS, BMW, Facebook, and Walmart.
