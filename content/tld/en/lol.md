The **.lol domain** takes the internet's most recognizable shorthand — "laugh out loud" — and turns it into a top-level domain built for comedy, memes, and entertainment. It is an open [new gTLD](/en/glossary/new-gtld/) that anyone can register, run today by the same registry that operates [.xyz](/en/tld/xyz). This page covers where .lol came from, who actually uses it, how it is priced, and its honest reputation among spam filters, so you can judge whether it fits your project.

## What is .lol?

.lol is a [generic top-level domain](/en/glossary/tld) approved through ICANN's 2012-round New gTLD Program, not a country-code extension, so it carries no built-in geographic meaning. The registry positions .lol as "the go-to online destination for everything fun and entertaining," aimed at comedy shows, entertainment venues, meme accounts, and playful URL shortening, according to the [official .lol registry site](https://nic.lol/).

Because it is a generic gTLD, Google treats .lol like any other new extension: there is no automatic ranking advantage or penalty, and no geo-targeting is applied, per [Google Search Central's guidance on multi-regional and generic domains](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites#:~:text=more%20generic%20than%20country-targeted). You can confirm the delegation record and current operator on the [IANA root-zone entry for .lol](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/lol.html).

## History of .lol

.lol has changed hands once since launch, and the [IANA delegation record](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/lol.html) documents both moves precisely:

- **Original delegation (2015):** ICANN's registry agreement for .lol was signed by **XYZ.COM LLC** on 30 January 2015 as a standard Base, Non-Sponsored agreement — confirmed on the [ICANN Registry Agreement page for .lol](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/lol) — but IANA's delegation report shows the string was first delegated to **Uniregistry, Corp.**, the registry founded by domain investor Frank Schilling, on 29 April 2015.
- **Transfer to XYZ.COM LLC (2022):** IANA recorded the "Transfer of the .lol domain to XYZ.COM LLC" on 23 March 2022. XYZ.COM LLC — the operator behind [.xyz](/en/tld/xyz), confirmed independently via its own [ICANN Registry Agreement](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/xyz) — has run .lol ever since, alongside dozens of other extensions such as .beauty and .icu.

So .lol began as one of Uniregistry's original 2012-round gTLDs and has been part of the XYZ registry family since 2022.

## How people use .lol

Real, specific niches where .lol shows up:

- **Comedy and entertainment brands** — stand-up shows, sketch groups, and comedy venues promoting events and merchandise.
- **Meme and content creators** — short, self-explanatory URLs for sharing humorous videos, images, and memes.
- **Casual community and fan projects** — Discord-adjacent communities, fan pages, and informal group sites that want a name that signals "not serious."
- **Playful URL shortening** — brands using .lol as a short, memorable redirect for a lighthearted campaign rather than their primary domain.
- **Short, brandable names** — the .lol namespace still has real availability for concise, pronounceable strings.

**Who it's not ideal for:** law firms, financial services, healthcare, B2B software, or any brand whose primary domain needs to project authority and gravitas. The suffix reads as informal by design.

## Notable sites using .lol

.lol does not yet have a widely recognized flagship brand running its primary site on the extension the way [.io](/en/tld/io) or [.app](/en/tld/app) do for tech. In practice its visible footprint skews toward comedy and meme accounts, short-lived campaign redirects, and small entertainment projects rather than major companies. Rather than name a specific site whose ownership may not persist, the honest characterization is that .lol is used mostly by individual creators and informal projects, consistent with the registry's own comedy/entertainment positioning.

## Why choose .lol?

- **Instantly legible meaning** — almost every reader recognizes "lol" without explanation.
- **Open and unrestricted** — no paperwork or eligibility checks; register and launch.
- **Strong short-name availability** — three- and four-letter combinations that are long gone on .com are often still open.
- **Budget-friendly** — a low-cost way to secure a name for a side project or campaign.

## Things to consider

- **Informal by design.** The humor framing that makes .lol memorable also makes it a poor fit for anything that needs to read as authoritative.
- **Lower mainstream familiarity.** Many users still default to trusting .com first, which can cost a first-glance credibility check.
- **Email scrutiny.** As a low-cost, easy-to-bulk-register extension, .lol can draw closer scrutiny from spam filters (see Reputation below).
- **History of ownership changes.** The registry has already changed hands once (Uniregistry to XYZ); administrative and support details can shift with future ownership changes.

## Who can register a .lol domain?

**Registration restrictions: open to all.** .lol is an unrestricted generic TLD — there is no credential, professional-membership, community, or local-presence requirement. Any individual or organization worldwide can register a .lol name through an accredited [registrar](/en/glossary/registrar).

Names can run from 1–63 characters, use letters, numbers, and hyphens (not in positions three and four), and are handled first-come, first-served, per [GoDaddy's .lol domain policy summary](https://www.godaddy.com/help/about-lol-domains-19076). The extension supports internationalized domain names (IDNs) and [DNSSEC](/en/glossary/dnssec), and registrations run 1–10 years at a time. The binding legal terms are set out in the [ICANN Registry Agreement for .lol](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/lol).

## .lol pricing and value

.lol sits in the budget tier of the gTLD market, and that shapes its pricing dynamics rather than any single number. **First-year and renewal pricing typically differ** — an inexpensive introductory rate is common, while the standing renewal rate is what matters long-term, so budget for renewal, not the teaser price. The registry also reserves a set of **premium names** (short or high-demand strings) that carry higher registration and renewal pricing than standard names. The main cost drivers are string length and desirability, premium classification, and registrar margin. We list no figures here; check current rates at the point of purchase.

## Reputation and email deliverability

Buyers deserve candor here. We could not find a current, fetchable Spamhaus or Interisle report that names .lol specifically among its worst- or most-abused TLD lists — if you find one, treat it as more current than this page. What is well documented at the category level: Interisle's [Cybercrime Supply Chain 2025 research](https://interisle.net/insights/cybercrimesupplychain2025#:~:text=though%20they%20hold%20just%2012%25%20of%20the%20market%2C%20they%20accounted%20for%20nearly%20half%20of%20all%20cybercrime%20domains%20reported) (sponsored by APWG, CAUCE, and M3AAWG) found that new gTLDs as a category hold just 12% of the domain market yet accounted for nearly half of all reported cybercrime domains — a reminder that cheap, easy-to-bulk-register extensions in general draw disproportionate abuse, and .lol's low pricing and open registration put it in that same risk category by design, even without a .lol-specific figure to cite.

Practically, that means some spam filters and cautious users may apply extra scrutiny to unfamiliar .lol links. The extension itself is legitimate and ICANN-accredited — being on .lol does not make a site malicious. **Mitigation:** configure SPF, DKIM, and DMARC correctly for any email sent from a .lol domain, warm up new sending domains gradually, and keep content clean.

## Branding and naming tips

- **Lean into the internet-slang reading.** ".lol" reads instantly as "laugh out loud" — use it where humor or lightness is the actual message.
- **Avoid it for anything serious.** The playful connotation works against you if your brand needs to signal trust or authority.
- **Keep it short.** The main draw of .lol is availability of concise, pronounceable names — don't waste that on a long string.
- **Watch for confusable characters.** Zeros vs. the letter "o," repeated letters, and look-alike substitutions are exactly what scammers exploit; a clean name distances you from that pattern.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone register a .lol domain?

Yes. .lol is an open, unrestricted generic top-level domain operated by XYZ.COM LLC. There is no credential, membership, or local-presence requirement, so anyone worldwide can register a .lol name through an accredited registrar on a first-come, first-served basis.

### Does a .lol domain affect SEO?

Google treats .lol as a generic top-level domain with no built-in ranking boost or penalty and no geographic bias. Because it is a low-cost extension that has attracted bulk registrations, some spam and security filters may scrutinize .lol traffic more closely, which can indirectly affect click-through and deliverability.

### Who operates the .lol domain?

XYZ.COM LLC, the same company behind the .xyz domain, has operated .lol since March 2022. The string was originally delegated to Uniregistry, Corp. in April 2015 before XYZ acquired the registry contract.

### Who should register a .lol domain?

It suits comedians, meme creators, entertainment brands, and informal community or fan projects where a playful tone fits the content. It is a weaker choice for a flagship business site, a professional services firm, or anything that depends heavily on email deliverability.
