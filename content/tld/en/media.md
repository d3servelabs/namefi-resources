The **.media domain** is an open generic top-level domain built for exactly what its name says: journalism, broadcasting, publishing, and content production. Operated by **Binky Moon, LLC**, part of the [Identity Digital](https://www.identity.digital/) family of registries, .media has been live since 2014 and is now used by outlets ranging from a UK national news agency to a Ukrainian public broadcaster. This page covers where .media came from, who actually uses it, and the honest trade-offs before you register one.

## What is .media?

.media is a [generic top-level domain](/en/glossary/tld) (gTLD), not a country-code extension, so it carries no geographic association. It was one of hundreds of new strings approved through [ICANN](/en/glossary/icann)'s 2012 New gTLD Program, and its plain-English meaning does the branding work: a .media site signals content, coverage, or creative production before a visitor reads a word of copy.

Because it is a generic string, Google has no special geo-targeting rule for .media — it is evaluated like any other gTLD. You can confirm the delegation record directly in the [IANA root-zone entry for .media](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/media.html), the authoritative source for any delegated TLD's operator and status.

## History of .media

- **Delegation (2014):** .media was delegated to the root zone on **April 9, 2014** (registration date April 3, 2014), initially assigned to **Grand Glen, LLC** — one of the many single-purpose entities that Donuts Inc. used to file its 2012-round gTLD applications. The underlying [ICANN Registry Agreement for .media](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/media) was signed **March 6, 2014**.
- **Consolidation into Binky Moon (2018):** On **March 23, 2018**, IANA recorded the transfer of .media — along with 195 other Donuts-originated strings, including .academy — to **Binky Moon, LLC**, Donuts's consolidated registry entity.
- **Identity Digital era (2021–present):** Donuts was acquired by Ethos Capital in 2021 and merged with Afilias; in June 2022 the combined company rebranded as **Identity Digital**, which now operates Binky Moon (and .media) as part of its portfolio.

So .media's real lineage is: a Donuts shell company at launch, folded into Donuts's Binky Moon entity in 2018, now run under the Identity Digital brand.

## How people use .media

- **News agencies and broadcasters** — national wire services and public broadcasters publish directly on .media.
- **Cybersecurity and trade journalism** — specialist reporting outlets use the string to signal a media (not corporate) identity.
- **Social-video and youth news brands** — short-form, social-native news companies have adopted .media for their primary domain.
- **Content, PR, and creative agencies** — firms producing video, design, or campaign work for clients.
- **Independent publishers, podcasts, and creator hubs** — a plain-English alternative when a matching .com is taken.

**Who it's not ideal for:** businesses with no media, publishing, or content angle — the descriptive meaning works against you if visitors expect a press or broadcast outlet and find something else.

## Notable sites using .media

- **[PA Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PA_Media)** (`pa.media`) — the UK and Ireland's national news agency, formerly the Press Association, rebranded in 2019.
- **The Record** (`therecord.media`) — the cybersecurity journalism outlet published by threat-intelligence firm Recorded Future.
- **Brut** (`brut.media`) — the Paris-founded social-video news company, now owned by CMA CGM, known for short-form journalism distributed across social platforms.
- **Suspilne** (`suspilne.media`) — the digital platform of Ukraine's national public broadcaster, Suspilne Ukraine.

Pick [.com](/en/tld/com/) when universal trust matters most and the name is available. Pick [.tv](/en/tld/tv/) when the product is specifically video or streaming. Pick .media when your identity is content or journalism broadly, and pick [.news](/en/tld/news/) instead when the site is specifically a news publication rather than media production in general.

## Why choose .media?

- **Immediate, accurate meaning.** Unlike arbitrary short strings, ".media" tells a visitor exactly what kind of site they're on.
- **Open and unrestricted.** No paperwork or eligibility checks — any journalist, studio, or agency can register directly.
- **Real institutional adoption.** A national news agency (PA Media) and a national public broadcaster (Suspilne) both run their digital platforms on .media, which is a meaningful trust signal for the string.
- **Availability.** Short, on-brand names are far easier to secure here than in the exhausted .com namespace.

## Things to consider

- **Broader than "news."** .media reads as content/production in general, which is a slightly different promise than a dedicated news outlet — see .news below if that is the literal fit.
- **Moderate mainstream recognition.** Most casual users still default to expecting .com; a .media address may need a beat longer to register as legitimate.
- **Single-TLD brand risk.** As with any non-.com extension, anchoring an entire brand on .media without a backup plan carries the usual concentration risk.

## Who can register a .media domain?

**Registration restrictions: open to all.** .media is an unrestricted generic TLD with no credential, professional-membership, community, or local-presence requirement. Any individual or organization worldwide can register through an accredited [registrar](/en/glossary/registrar).

A trademark sunrise period ran ahead of general availability in 2014, so today registration is first-come, first-served. [DNSSEC](/en/glossary/dnssec) is supported at the registry, and [WHOIS privacy](/en/glossary/whois-privacy/) is available through most registrars. Standard ICANN lifecycle rules apply — auto-renew grace, redemption grace, and pending-delete periods — so a lapsed name is not instantly available again. The binding rules are set out in the [ICANN Registry Agreement for .media](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/media).

## .media pricing and value

.media pricing follows standard new-gTLD dynamics rather than a single number. **First-year and renewal pricing typically differ** — promotional first-year rates are common, while the standing renewal rate is what you pay long term. The registry also reserves certain short, dictionary, or high-demand strings as **premium names**, which can carry higher registration and renewal pricing than standard names. Length, desirability, premium classification, and registrar margin are the main cost drivers. No figures are quoted here — check current rates at the point of purchase.

## Reputation and email deliverability

.media does not carry the spam-heavy reputation seen on some ultra-low-cost new gTLDs. Its user base skews toward established media organizations, agencies, and publishers rather than bulk, disposable registrations, and outlets like PA Media and Suspilne lend the string institutional credibility. That said, .media is still a relatively young extension next to legacy TLDs, so some conservative spam filters may apply marginally more scrutiny than they would to .com. **Mitigation:** configure SPF, DKIM, and DMARC properly, warm up new sending domains gradually, and keep content clean — the same baseline practices that matter for any newer extension.

## Branding and naming tips

- **Let the meaning do the work.** ".media" already tells visitors what to expect, so the name itself can be short and punchy (`brut.media`, `pa.media`).
- **Avoid redundancy.** Don't pair it with a name that already says "media," "news," or "press" — the suffix supplies that context.
- **Consider the domain hack.** Words ending in "media" or where "media" reads naturally as a suffix can produce a clean, memorable domain hack.
- **Say it out loud.** Confirm the full name is easy to say and spell over the phone before committing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone register a .media domain?

Yes. .media is an open, unrestricted generic top-level domain operated by Binky Moon, LLC. There is no credential, membership, or local-presence requirement, so any individual or organization worldwide can register a .media name through an accredited registrar.

### Does a .media domain affect SEO?

No. Google treats .media as a generic top-level domain with no automatic ranking boost, penalty, or geographic bias, so a .media site can rank globally exactly like a .com site would.

### Who should register a .media domain?

Journalists, broadcasters, publishers, content agencies, filmmakers, and creators whose work is literally media production or distribution. It is a strong descriptive fit for that niche but a weaker choice for an unrelated flagship consumer brand.

### Is .media a good choice for a news publication?

It can be, and several real publishers use it, but the string reads as broad "media" rather than specifically "news" — a publication built around breaking news coverage may find .news a more literal fit.
