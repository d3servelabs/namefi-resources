The **.to domain** is the [country-code top-level domain](/en/glossary/cctld/) for **Tonga**. The [IANA record](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/to.html) identifies Tonga's government as the manager. [TONIC Domains Inc. operates the public registry website](https://www.tonic.to/) and says its automated registry has operated since 1997.

The letters also form the English word “to.” That makes the extension useful for [domain hacks](/en/glossary/domain-hack/) and short brand names. A readable name still needs a suitable registration policy. Check the term, redirect rules, privacy limits, and country signals before choosing `.to`.

![A .to address connects to a globe and a registration checklist](../../assets/to-og.jpg)

## The .to extension belongs to Tonga and serves a global market

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Which country does .to represent? | Tonga |
| Who manages the delegation? | Government of the Kingdom of Tonga, according to IANA |
| Who operates the public registry website? | TONIC Domains Inc. |
| Does the registry offer global registration? | Yes, according to Tonic's FAQ |
| Does Google list .to as a generic ccTLD? | No, as checked on October 21, 2026 |
| Does IANA show a secure delegation? | No. IANA labels the delegation insecure |

The [IANA record](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/to.html) establishes the country-code delegation and lists the registry website, WHOIS server, and RDAP endpoint. IANA does not set the retail registration conditions. Read [Tonic's own rules](https://www.tonic.to/faq.htm) for registration and permitted use.

Tonic describes a global automated registration service. Its public FAQ does not state a Tonga residency requirement. The FAQ also reserves some names. Check availability before planning a launch around a particular address.

## Tonic requires a two-year initial registration

[Tonic's FAQ](https://www.tonic.to/faq.htm) gives a two-year minimum for initial registration and a one-year minimum for renewal. Compare the total initial payment with the renewal cost. A two-year checkout total is not directly comparable with another extension's one-year offer.

Ask the provider to confirm the following details before payment:

1. Confirm the registration term and total charge.
2. Check the renewal price and renewal reminder settings.
3. Check the provider's ownership-transfer procedure and fee.
4. Confirm the DNS features required by your project.

Tonic's direct-registration FAQ treats a change of registrant as a new registration with new charges. Distinguish an ownership change from moving the domain between service providers. Ask the provider how the specific transaction works before buying an existing name.

## Tonic holds redirect operators responsible for abuse

The [registry's abuse warning](https://www.tonic.to/faq.htm) makes registrants responsible for spam involving their redirect users. Tonic warns against free URL shortening. Tonic says a first legitimate phishing report can cause permanent suspension of a free-shortening or redirection domain.

Do not read a recognizable branded `.to` link as approval for every redirect business model. A private redirect for your own links and an unrestricted public shortener create different abuse exposure. Ask Tonic to confirm your planned service. Maintain control of destinations and a way to stop abusive links.

For a company short-link project, test the whole workflow before launch. Check link creation permissions, destination changes, abuse reports, and emergency shutdown. A short address does not remove the operator's responsibilities.

## Tonic provides registration lookups with privacy limits

[Tonic says its WHOIS and RDAP services](https://www.tonic.to/faq.htm) expose registration and DNS data without sensitive personal details. That differs from having no public [WHOIS](/en/glossary/whois/) service. IANA lists both lookup endpoints.

Privacy does not establish anonymity. Do not promise customers that a `.to` registration conceals all identifying information or prevents abuse enforcement. Check the current provider's data policy when collecting customer information or handling a complaint.

## Google does not document generic treatment for .to

[Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites) describes ccTLDs as country signals. Google also lists country codes it treats as generic. The list includes `.io`, `.co`, and `.tv`, but excludes `.to` as of this check.

The omission does not prove a ranking penalty. The omission also does not support a promise that Google treats `.to` like `.com`. A global business should consider the country signal alongside its audience, content language, and naming needs.

Choose [.com](/en/tld/com/) when a suitable name meets the project's needs. Compare [.io](/en/tld/io/), [.co](/en/tld/co/), and [.tv](/en/tld/tv/) if Google's documented generic treatment matters. Compare each registry's registration rules separately.

## The current delegation does not provide a DNSSEC chain

[IANA labels .to an insecure delegation](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/to.html). As checked on October 21, 2026, the record does not show a secure root-to-`.to` chain. A project that requires an unbroken [DNSSEC](/en/glossary/dnssec/) chain should resolve that requirement before choosing `.to`.

DNSSEC and HTTPS solve different problems. The delegation status does not decide whether a website can use HTTPS. Ask the hosting and DNS providers which protections the intended setup supports.

Tonic also says it supports internationalized domain names. The [FAQ prohibits intentional look-alike registrations](https://www.tonic.to/faq.htm) and warns that malicious names can lose DNS service without notice. Test the intended name in browsers, email clients, and spoken conversation before relying on unusual characters.

## A good .to name must work beyond the visual wordplay

Use `.to` when the complete address reads clearly and the registry conditions fit the project. Test the name with someone who has not seen the spelling. Ask that person to type the address after hearing the name.

Avoid choosing the extension solely because another company uses a short `.to` link. A brand's existing use does not establish availability, permission, pricing, or suitability for a different project. Our [domain-hack guide](/en/blog/domain-hacks-explained/) explains how to assess the complete name.

## Check the provider's offer before registering .to

1. Search for the exact name at [Namefi](https://namefi.io) or [Tonic](https://www.tonic.to/newname.htm).
2. Confirm that the provider currently offers `.to` and the required registration term.
3. Read the provider's current terms and Tonic's use rules.
4. Register the name and configure [DNS](/en/glossary/dns/).
5. Test the website, redirects, and email before publishing the address.

A search or information page does not guarantee availability or a specific checkout price. Confirm the actual offer before payment. For general naming concepts, read [what a TLD is](/en/blog/what-is-a-tld/) and the [domain terminology guide](/en/blog/domain-terminology-guide/).

## These answers clarify common .to questions

### Can anyone register a .to domain?

Tonic offers .to registration globally. Its public FAQ does not state a Tonga residency requirement. Reserved names and registry use policies still apply.

### Does a .to domain affect SEO?

Google does not include .to in its published list of ccTLDs treated as generic. Do not assume the same documented treatment as .io, .co, or .tv, or infer a ranking penalty.

### Does .to have public WHOIS?

Yes. Tonic offers WHOIS and RDAP. The registry says these services disclose registration and DNS information without sensitive personal details. Privacy does not mean anonymity or immunity from abuse enforcement.

### Can I run a free URL shortener on .to?

Tonic warns against free URL shortening and redirection. The registry says it will permanently suspend such a domain after the first legitimate phishing report. Ask Tonic about your use case before building a redirect service.
