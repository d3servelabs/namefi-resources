The **.gg domain** is the [country-code top-level domain](/en/glossary/cctld/) (ccTLD) of the **Bailiwick of Guernsey**, a British Crown dependency in the Channel Islands — and one of the clearest examples of a ccTLD outgrowing its geography. Because **"gg" is the universal gamer shorthand for "good game,"** it has been adopted worldwide by game studios, esports teams, streamers, and gaming platforms — most visibly Discord, whose invite links all live under `discord.gg`.

This page covers what .gg actually is, who runs it, why gaming claimed it, the registration rules, and the honest trade-offs before you build a brand on it.

## What is .gg?

.gg is the two-letter country code assigned to Guernsey under ISO 3166-1, delegated as a [ccTLD](/en/glossary/cctld/) in the root zone. The [IANA root-database entry for .gg](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/gg.html) lists **Island Networks Ltd.**, based in Alderney (one of the Bailiwick's islands), as the registry manager, with registration services at nic.gg and WHOIS at `whois.gg`.

Unlike a [gTLD](/en/glossary/tld/), a ccTLD has no ICANN registry agreement; its rules are set by the national manager. For .gg that is the **Channel Isles registry** operated by Island Networks, which [was founded in August 1996 by Nigel Roberts and Laurie Brown](https://channelisles.net/#:~:text=founded%20in%20August%201996) to run both .gg and .je, the ccTLD of neighbouring Jersey — two islands, one registry.

One SEO nuance deserves precision. Google maintains a published list of ccTLDs it treats as generic (non-geo-targeted), and [.gg is not on that list](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites#:~:text=Google%20treats%20some%20ccTLDs) — the documented generic set includes .io, .tv, .me, .co, and .fm, but not .gg. By Google's own documentation, then, .gg is country-targeted to Guernsey. In practice, globally successful .gg platforms show that content and links dominate the suffix signal — but if worldwide organic search is mission-critical, know this fact rather than assume it away.

## History of .gg

.gg was delegated on **7 August 1996**, proposed to Jon Postel of IANA by Nigel Roberts and Laurie Brown, the co-founders of Island Networks. The string was something of a second choice — other candidate codes were already allocated — a historical accident that later proved to be the extension's greatest asset.

For its first decade and a half, .gg quietly served Guernsey businesses, organisations, and government (the gov.gg space), with registrations directly at the second level alongside legacy categories .co.gg, .net.gg, and .org.gg.

The turning point was cultural. As competitive online gaming exploded in the 2010s, "gg" — typed in chat at the end of a match to say *good game* — became one of the internet's most recognised abbreviations, and the country code that spelled gamers' favourite sign-off began attracting registrants with no Channel Islands connection. **Discord's decision to run its server invite system on `discord.gg`** put the extension in front of hundreds of millions of users; `discord.gg` still 301-redirects to discord.com and carries every invite link. Esports platforms followed, and .gg shifted from island namespace to global gaming badge — a trajectory similar to [.io](/en/tld/io/) with developers and [.tv](/en/tld/tv/) with streaming.

## How people use .gg

- **Gaming platforms and tooling** — stats trackers, tournament brackets, and community tools where the audience instantly reads "gg."
- **Esports teams and organisations** — team sites and fan hubs wanting a suffix native to their culture.
- **Discord communities** — every server invite is a `discord.gg/...` link, and many communities brand around matching .gg names.
- **Streamers and game studios** — short, chat-friendly URLs that are easy to say on stream, plus launch sites for individual titles.
- **Guernsey businesses and institutions** — the original constituency, including the gov.gg public-sector space.

**Who it's not ideal for:** projects with no gaming or community angle, where the strong subculture reading adds noise instead of meaning — a law firm on .gg reads as a Guernsey law firm; a fintech mostly reads as confused.

## Notable sites using .gg

- **discord.gg** — Discord's invite-link domain. Every server invite is a `discord.gg` URL, and the bare domain redirects to discord.com — one of the most-seen ccTLD [domain hacks](/en/blog/domain-hacks-explained/) in the world.
- **op.gg** — a major game-statistics platform, best known for League of Legends builds and player stats, now also covering Valorant, Teamfight Tactics, PUBG, and more.
- **start.gg** — an esports tournament platform ("community through competition") for running competitive events, brackets, and rankings.
- **gov.gg** — the States of Guernsey's public-sector space, a reminder that .gg remains a working national namespace.

Pick **.gg** when your audience is gamers and the "good game" reading does branding work for you; **[.io](/en/tld/io/)** for developer tools outside gaming culture; **[.tv](/en/tld/tv/)** when video is the product; **[.com](/en/tld/com/)** when you need the broadest mainstream trust and can get the name.

## Why choose .gg?

- **Instant cultural fit.** No other extension says "this is for gamers" as natively — the meaning is baked into two decades of chat etiquette.
- **Proven by the biggest names in the space.** Discord, op.gg, and start.gg give the suffix daily global visibility.
- **Genuinely open registration.** Unlike many ccTLDs, there is no residency or presence requirement.
- **Short and chat-friendly.** Two letters after the dot keeps URLs compact and easy to say on stream.
- **Signed zone.** The registry publishes a DS record for .gg, so [DNSSEC](/en/glossary/dnssec/) chains of trust work end to end.

## Things to consider

- **Country-targeted by the book.** Google's documented generic-ccTLD list omits .gg. Global .gg successes show this is survivable, but it is a real consideration for search-dependent projects.
- **A national registry sets the rules.** .gg is governed by Channel Islands policy, not an ICANN registry agreement; the registry may reserve certain words and string patterns, and policy can evolve under local law.
- **Niche outside gaming.** The strong "good game" association is an asset inside gaming and a puzzle outside it.
- **Renewal economics.** In-demand ccTLDs generally sit above commodity extensions in price; check the renewal rate, not just year one, before committing a brand.

## Who can register a .gg domain?

**Registration restrictions: open to all.** The registry's own terms state that ["in general, there are no restrictions whatsoever"](https://www.channelisles.net/legal/terms-and-conditions-1#:~:text=no%20restrictions%20whatsoever) as to who may register and use .GG and .JE internet names — no residency rule, no local-presence requirement (contrast [.us](/en/tld/us/) and its Nexus Requirement), and no credential gate. Registrants must provide accurate identity details, keep them current, and warrant they have the right to use the name they apply for.

Registrations happen at the second level (`yourname.gg`), with legacy category spaces (.co.gg, .net.gg, .org.gg) also in the system; certain words and string patterns may be reserved. Disputes go through the registry's independent online Dispute Resolution Procedure (DRP) rather than ICANN's UDRP, since ccTLDs set their own dispute policy. Registration runs through accredited [registrars](/en/glossary/registrar/); authoritative policy lives at the [Channel Isles registry](https://channelisles.net/).

## .gg pricing and value

.gg pricing reflects a small national registry with outsized global demand rather than a commodity namespace. **First-year and renewal pricing can differ**, so budget on the renewal rate. The registry's terms allow **reserved words and string patterns**, and short or gaming-relevant strings command aftermarket premiums driven by the esports and creator economy. The main cost drivers are string quality, gaming-vertical demand, and registrar margin; this page intentionally quotes no figures.

## Reputation and email deliverability

.gg carries a **strong, specific reputation: gaming.** Anchored by household-name platforms and three decades as a national ccTLD, it has largely avoided the bulk-spam associations that dog some cheap new gTLDs.

For email, the suffix is rarely decisive — filters weigh sender reputation and SPF/DKIM/DMARC authentication far more than the TLD, so a properly authenticated .gg sender should deliver normally. The main consideration is perception: a .gg address reads as gaming-adjacent, on-brand for a studio and slightly unusual for an accounting practice.

## Branding and naming tips

- **Let "gg" do the talking.** The best .gg names lean into the sign-off: short brandables and team names where the suffix itself signals the vertical.
- **Keep it say-able on stream.** These names get spoken aloud constantly — pick something viewers can type correctly after hearing it once.
- **Match your Discord identity.** A .gg site that mirrors your server name creates a coherent `discord.gg/...` + `yourbrand.gg` pairing.
- **Don't force it outside gaming.** If your audience wouldn't recognise "gg," the main asset is wasted — consider [.io](/en/tld/io/) or [.com](/en/tld/com/) instead.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone register a .gg domain?

Yes. The Channel Isles registry states that in general there are no restrictions whatsoever on who may register and use .gg names. There is no residency, local-presence, or credential requirement — anyone worldwide can register through an accredited registrar.

### Why do gaming sites use .gg domains?

In multiplayer gaming, "gg" is the standard shorthand for "good game," typed at the end of a match. That coincidence made Guernsey's country code instantly meaningful to gamers, and discord.gg, op.gg, and start.gg cemented it as the default extension for gaming and esports brands.

### Does a .gg domain affect SEO?

Google's published list of ccTLDs it treats as generic does not include .gg, so by the book it is country-targeted to Guernsey. In practice, major .gg platforms serve global audiences successfully, and content, links, and hreflang matter far more than the suffix.

### Who runs the .gg domain?

The .gg registry is operated by Island Networks, founded in August 1996 by Nigel Roberts and Laurie Brown. IANA lists Island Networks Ltd. of Alderney, Guernsey as the manager; the same organisation also operates .je for neighbouring Jersey.
