The **.so domain** is the country-code top-level domain for **Somalia**, and it has become a recognizable pick in tech branding for one simple reason: it completes the word **"so."** The most famous example was [Notion](https://www.notion.com), whose `notion.so` address defined the company for years — though as of June 2026, Notion has actually moved off `.so` entirely, a twist that says a lot about how this suffix's real-world story keeps evolving. This page covers what .so really is, its unusually turbulent registry history, the current registration rules, and how it behaves for SEO.

## What is .so?

**.so** is the [ccTLD](/en/glossary/cctld/) assigned to Somalia under the ISO 3166-1 country-code system. The [IANA root-zone database entry for .so](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/so.html) lists Somalia's **Ministry of Post and Telecommunications** as the sponsoring organization, though on-the-ground operational authority has shifted repeatedly, most recently to Somalia's **National Communications Authority (NCA)**.

Because .so is a ccTLD, not a gTLD, it carries no ICANN registry agreement of the kind that governs `.com` or new gTLDs. And despite its popularity in tech branding, Google does not treat .so as generic: it is absent from [Google's explicit list of ccTLDs treated as generic](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites#:~:text=.ad%2C%20.ai%2C%20.as%2C%20.bz%2C%20.cc%2C%20.cd%2C%20.co%2C%20.dj%2C%20.fm%2C%20.io%2C%20.la%2C%20.me%2C%20.ms%2C%20.nu%2C%20.sc%2C%20.sr%2C%20.su%2C%20.tv%2C%20.tk%2C%20.ws) (which includes lookalike vanity ccTLDs like .cc, .co, and .tv), meaning a .so site is geo-targeted toward Somalia by default rather than being read as a neutral "so" suffix.

## History of .so

.so has one of the most turbulent operating histories of any ccTLD:

- **1997 delegation, then collapse:** first delegated on August 28, 1997 to Monolith Innovation Group, a Pittsburgh-based operator that became defunct by 1998, leaving the namespace dormant during Somalia's civil conflict.
- **2009 redelegation, 2010 relaunch:** ICANN approved redelegation to Somalia's Ministry of Post and Telecommunications on **February 3, 2009**, and the domain officially relaunched for public registration on **November 1, 2010**.
- **2015 restriction:** on **July 8, 2015**, operations transferred to the **Somali Network Information Center (SONIC)**, which limited registration to those with a "legitimate, clear and provable connection to Somalia" — prompting several international registrars to suspend new .so sales.
- **2018 NCA takeover:** on **March 9, 2018**, Somalia's **National Communications Authority** took full control from SONIC. Major registrars (GoDaddy, Rebel.com) again advertise .so with **no residency requirement** — but given the repeated changes, confirm current eligibility before committing to a brand.

Through all of this, .so's international reputation grew from an unrelated direction: workspace tool **Notion** launched on `notion.so`, becoming one of the most recognized "so"-ending brand hacks in tech — until Notion itself moved away from it in 2026.

## How people use .so

Real, specific niches where .so shows up:

- **Developer tools and platforms whose name ends in "so,"** such as [Hiro](https://hiro.so), a Bitcoin-layer developer tools company that markets itself on `hiro.so`.
- **Startups seeking a short, sentence-completing domain hack** in the tradition Notion popularized, even after Notion's own move to `.com`.
- **Somalia-connected institutions and organizations**, the suffix's original and continuing use case, with second-level structures such as `.com.so`, `.org.so`, `.edu.so`, and `.gov.so`.

**Who it's not ideal for:** brands prioritizing long-term registration certainty, given the registry's documented history of eligibility changes, or anyone chasing global SEO neutrality rather than a wordplay effect.

## Notable sites using .so

- **[hiro.so](https://hiro.so)** — Hiro Systems PBC's live site for its Bitcoin-layer developer tools (Chainhook, the Hiro Platform, the Stacks Blockchain API), actively maintained with 2026 blog activity and a public status page at `status.hiro.so`.
- **notion.so** — for years the definitive .so brand hack, home to workspace tool Notion. As of **June 3, 2026**, Notion completed a move to `notion.com` — an acquisition its co-founder said had been in motion since 2018 — and `notion.so` now **redirects (HTTP 307) to notion.com**. It is the clearest cautionary tale in the ccTLD-hack world: even the flagship "so" brand eventually traded the wordplay for a `.com`.

Choose **.io** or **.co** when you want a hack-friendly ccTLD with a stable registration history and Google's generic treatment; pick **.com** when brand permanence matters most, following Notion's own eventual move; reach for **.so** only when the wordplay is essential and you've confirmed current eligibility terms.

## Why choose .so?

- **Genuine, well-known wordplay.** The "so"-ending hack is one of the most recognized in tech, thanks to Notion's years on `notion.so`.
- **Live proof it still works.** Hiro Systems actively runs its developer-tools business on `hiro.so` today.
- **Distinctive positioning.** A short, punchy suffix that stands out against the sea of `.com` and `.io` startups.
- **Multiple second-level structures available** (`.com.so`, `.org.so`, etc.) for registrants who want a categorized address.

## Things to consider

- **Genuine policy volatility.** .so has actually restricted, then reopened, registration within the last decade — a real risk for anyone planning a long-term brand around it.
- **Google geo-targets it by default.** .so is not on Google's generic-ccTLD list, so a global brand should explicitly configure international targeting rather than assume neutrality.
- **Even its most famous example moved on.** Notion's 2026 switch to `.com` after eight years of trying suggests some brands view `.so` as a stepping stone, not a permanent home.
- **No DNSSEC confirmation, and IDN is explicitly not supported** — less technical flexibility than more modern ccTLD registries.

## Who can register a .so domain?

**Registration restrictions: confirm before you commit.** Current registrar documentation from major providers, including GoDaddy's and Rebel.com's help centers, reports that .so registration today has **no residency, citizenship, or local-presence requirement** and runs first-come, first-served. This is not how the registry has always operated: from **2015 to 2018**, operator SONIC restricted registration to "institutions and organizations in Somalia, residents of Somalia, and others who have a legitimate, clear and provable connection to Somalia," causing several international registrars to pause .so sales. Since the **National Communications Authority (NCA)** took control in March 2018, the open policy in current registrar materials has held — but given this track record, verify current eligibility with your registrar or the [IANA-listed sponsoring authority](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/so.html) before building a brand on `.so`.

Domain names generally run **3 to 63 alphanumeric characters or hyphens**; [IDN](/en/glossary/dns/) registrations are **not supported**. Reserved second-level structures include `.com.so`, `.org.so`, `.net.so`, `.edu.so`, and `.gov.so`. Because .so is a ccTLD, it is not governed by a standard [ICANN Registry Agreement](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements) — the sponsoring authority remains Somalia's Ministry of Post and Telecommunications per IANA, with the NCA holding current operational control.

## .so pricing and value

.so pricing generally sits in a **mid-to-premium band** relative to mainstream gTLDs, reflecting its cachet as a recognized brand-hack suffix. As with most TLDs, **first-year and renewal pricing differ** — introductory reseller rates are not the standing renewal cost. **Short or dictionary-adjacent names** fitting the "so"-ending wordplay command the strongest demand and, where classified premium, higher pricing. This page intentionally quotes no figures; check current pricing with your registrar.

## Reputation and email deliverability

.so does not carry a widespread spam or abuse reputation — adoption has skewed toward legitimate developer tools and brand-name wordplay rather than bulk registrations. That said, its registry instability (the 2015–2018 restriction period, multiple operator transitions) is worth weighing separately from reputation: a suffix can be technically safe for deliverability while carrying operational risk. For [email deliverability](/en/blog/email-sender-reputation-arms-race/), sender authentication (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) and list hygiene matter far more than the TLD itself.

## Branding and naming tips

- **Make the "so" completion effortless.** The strongest examples (`notion.so`, `hiro.so`) land as a natural fragment — the visual/verbal snap of a short brand name plus "so."
- **Have a fallback plan.** Given the registry's history and Notion's own move to `.com`, consider registering the equivalent `.com` too, even if `.so` is your primary hack.
- **Keep the pre-".so" name short.** Wordplay domains lose their punch past a few syllables.
- **Verify eligibility before you fall in love with a name**, given the registry's documented policy changes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone register a .so domain?

Current registrar documentation (including GoDaddy and Rebel.com) reports no residency or local-presence requirement today, though the registry imposed a Somalia-connection requirement between 2015 and 2018. Confirm current eligibility with your registrar before registering, since .so policy has changed hands multiple times.

### Does a .so domain affect SEO?

Google does not include .so on its list of ccTLDs treated as generic, so by default a .so site sends a signal that it targets Somalia rather than being read as a neutral global suffix, regardless of how the string is used in branding.

### Is notion.so still used by Notion?

No. Notion switched its primary domain to notion.com in June 2026 after an eight-year effort to acquire it. As of this writing, notion.so redirects to notion.com rather than serving as the live brand domain.

### Who should register a .so domain?

It suits developer tools and brands whose name naturally ends in "so," following examples like Hiro (hiro.so). It is a weaker choice for anyone who needs registration certainty, given the registry history of policy changes, or for a brand chasing global SEO neutrality.
