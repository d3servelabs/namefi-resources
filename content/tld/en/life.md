The **.life domain** is a plain-English top-level domain aimed at wellness, lifestyle, personal-development, and mission-driven brands. It is an open new gTLD that anyone can register, delegated in the 2012-round ICANN New gTLD Program and operated today by **Identity Digital**, one of the largest portfolio registries in the industry. This page covers .life's real ownership history, who actually uses it, and the honest trade-offs, so you can judge whether it fits your project.

## What is .life?

.life is a [generic top-level domain](/en/glossary/tld), not a country-code extension, so it carries no geographic meaning. The string is the ordinary English word for life, which gives it broad, immediately legible use across health, wellness, personal-development, and cause-driven branding without requiring an explanation of what the letters stand for — unlike acronym-based new gTLDs.

Because it is a generic gTLD, Google treats .life like any other new extension: no automatic ranking advantage or penalty, and no geo-targeting applied, per [Google Search Central's guidance on generic vs. country-targeted domains](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites?hl=en#:~:text=more%20generic%20than%20country-targeted). You can confirm the delegation record and current operator on the [IANA root-zone entry for .life](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/life.html).

Google also states that [keywords in a TLD do not give any advantage or disadvantage in search](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2015/07/googles-handling-of-new-top-level?hl=en#:~:text=Keywords%20in%20a%20TLD%20do%20not%20give%20any%20advantage%20or%20disadvantage%20in%20search.), so the extension itself receives no inherent ranking boost or penalty.

## History of .life

.life's registry history traces back to one of the largest portfolio applicants of the original 2012 round, and the [IANA delegation record](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/life.html) documents the path:

- **Original delegation (2014):** The [ICANN Registry Agreement for .life](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/life) was signed on 6 February 2014 by **Binky Moon, LLC**, but IANA's delegation report shows the string was first delegated to **Trixy Oaks, LLC** on 9 May 2014 (registration in the root dates to 8 May 2014). Trixy Oaks was one of the many single-purpose shell entities Donuts Inc. used to apply for its roughly 200 new gTLD strings during the 2012 application round.
- **Consolidation under Binky Moon (2018):** IANA recorded the "Transfer of the .academy domain and 195 others" on 23 March 2018, moving .life and the rest of Donuts' portfolio from its individual shell registrants into a single entity, **Binky Moon, LLC**. Donuts later rebranded as **Identity Digital** after acquiring Afilias, and Identity Digital remains .life's operator today.

So .life began as one string among roughly 200 that Donuts applied for using dedicated shell companies, and has been consolidated under Identity Digital's Binky Moon entity since 2018.

## How people use .life

Real, specific niches where .life shows up:

- **Wellness and health coaching** — fitness trainers, nutritionists, and mental-health professionals building a practice site around the word "life."
- **Personal development and coaching** — life coaches and motivational brands where the suffix reinforces the service.
- **Nonprofits, foundations, and churches** — mission-driven organizations for whom "life" fits the cause, from charitable foundations to faith communities.
- **Lifestyle content and influencer sites** — bloggers and creators covering lifestyle topics under a name/`.life` pairing.
- **Short, brandable names** — the .life namespace still has real availability for concise strings long gone on legacy zones.

**Who it's not ideal for:** businesses with no wellness, lifestyle, or mission-driven angle — the word is specific enough in meaning that it can read as mismatched for, say, a B2B software company or an e-commerce electronics store.

## Notable sites using .life

.life does not have a single widely recognized flagship brand running its primary site on the extension the way [.io](/en/tld/io) does for tech. Its real, verifiable footprint is a portfolio-scale gTLD operated by Identity Digital — one of roughly 200 strings Donuts originally applied for — used in practice by smaller wellness practitioners, coaches, and nonprofit or cause-driven sites rather than by major consumer or enterprise brands. Rather than name a specific site whose ownership or content may not persist, the honest characterization is that .life serves niche, mission-aligned use rather than mainstream brand adoption.

## Why choose .life?

- **Broad, self-explanatory meaning** — "life" needs no translation or acronym explanation.
- **Open and unrestricted** — no paperwork or eligibility checks; register and launch.
- **Fits mission-driven branding** — a natural pairing for wellness, coaching, and nonprofit names.
- **Backed by a major registry** — Identity Digital operates a large, established portfolio of gTLDs, giving .life stable long-term infrastructure.

## Things to consider

- **Broad meaning can feel generic.** "Life" is a big word; a name built around it needs strong content to stand out rather than relying on the suffix alone.
- **Lower mainstream familiarity.** Many users still default to trusting .com first at a glance.
- **Portfolio-scale registry.** As one of roughly 200 strings under a single large operator, .life doesn't get the dedicated marketing push a standalone registry might give a smaller portfolio.
- **Email scrutiny.** As with other budget new gTLDs, some spam filters apply closer scrutiny to newer, low-cost extensions (see Reputation below).

## Who can register a .life domain?

**Registration restrictions: open to all.** .life is an unrestricted generic TLD — there is no credential, professional-membership, community, or local-presence requirement. Any individual or organization worldwide can register a .life name through an accredited [registrar](/en/glossary/registrar).

Names can run from 1–63 characters, use letters, numbers, and hyphens (not in positions three and four), and are handled first-come, first-served, per [GoDaddy's .life domain policy summary](https://www.godaddy.com/help/about-life-domains-12109). The extension supports internationalized domain names (IDNs) and [DNSSEC](/en/glossary/dnssec), and registrations run 1–10 years at a time. The registry also designates a set of [premium domain](/en/glossary/premium-domain) names. The binding legal terms are set out in the [ICANN Registry Agreement for .life](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/life).

## .life pricing and value

.life sits in the budget-to-mid tier of the gTLD market, and that shapes its pricing dynamics rather than any single number. **First-year and renewal pricing typically differ** — an inexpensive introductory rate is common, while the standing renewal rate is what matters long-term. The registry reserves a set of **premium names** (short, dictionary, or high-demand strings) that carry higher registration and renewal pricing than standard names. The main cost drivers are string length and desirability, premium classification, and registrar margin. We list no figures here; check current rates at the point of purchase.

## Reputation and email deliverability

Buyers deserve candor here. The current [Spamhaus Domain Reputation Update for October 2025–March 2026](https://content.spamhaus.org/4d83c22f-84c5-4f82-95ab-4e6b39f73db5.pdf) lists `.life` at **#20 among gTLDs by malicious or suspicious domain count**, with **12,204** listed domains. The same report ranks it **#19 by share of the zone listed**, at **2.99%** (12,204 out of 407,624 domains). Those figures do not mean every `.life` site is risky, but they do show measurable abuse within the zone.

Practically, that means some spam filters and cautious users may apply extra scrutiny to unfamiliar .life links. The extension itself is legitimate and ICANN-accredited, and being operated by an established registry like Identity Digital is a point in its favor — being on .life does not make a site untrustworthy. **Mitigation:** configure SPF, DKIM, and DMARC correctly for any email sent from a .life domain, warm up new sending domains gradually, and keep registrant information clean and verifiable — vital for nonprofit and health-related sites where trust is central.

## Branding and naming tips

- **Lean into a mission or wellness angle.** ".life" reads best when it reinforces a coaching, health, or cause-driven message.
- **Pair it with a specific word.** A name plus "life" (e.g., "balanced" + .life) is more distinctive than a generic one-word .life domain.
- **Keep it short and pronounceable.** Concise names are easiest to remember and speak aloud.
- **Watch for confusable characters.** Zeros vs. the letter "o" and repeated letters are exactly what scammers exploit; a clean name avoids that pattern.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone register a .life domain?

Yes. .life is an open, unrestricted generic top-level domain. There is no credential, membership, or local-presence requirement, so anyone worldwide can register a .life name through an accredited registrar on a first-come, first-served basis.

### Does a .life domain affect SEO?

Google treats .life as a generic top-level domain with no built-in ranking boost or penalty and no geographic bias. Rankings depend on content quality, links, and user experience rather than on the suffix itself.

### Who operates the .life domain?

Binky Moon, LLC, part of Identity Digital, has held the .life registry agreement since 2018 as part of a 195-domain portfolio transfer from Donuts Inc. The string was originally delegated to a Donuts shell entity, Trixy Oaks, LLC, in 2014.

### Who should register a .life domain?

It suits wellness and health coaches, lifestyle and personal-development brands, and nonprofits or churches whose mission fits the word "life." It is a weaker choice for a business with no lifestyle, wellness, or mission-driven angle.
