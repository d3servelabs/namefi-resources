The **.llc domain** takes the most common US small-business entity type — the limited liability company — and turns it directly into a web address. Delegated in the 2012 New gTLD Program and operated by Identity Digital, it launched in 2018 with a deliberately open registration policy: the registry states outright that you don't need to have actually formed your LLC to register the matching domain. This page covers .llc's real history, who runs it, and how it compares to its sibling corporate-identity extensions.

## What is .llc?

.llc is a [generic top-level domain](/en/glossary/gtld) delegated under ICANN's 2012 New gTLD Program. Like any generic suffix, it carries no geographic meaning, so Google's [multi-regional targeting guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites#:~:text=A%20generic%20top-level%20domain%20%28gTLD%29) treats .llc the same as .com or any other gTLD — there is no automatic ranking effect and no built-in country association.

The registry is Identity Digital Domains Limited, formerly Afilias — one of the industry's larger registry operators, also behind extensions like [.info](/en/tld/info) and [.llc](/en/tld/llc)'s corporate sibling [.ltd](/en/tld/ltd). You can confirm the current operator and delegation date in the [IANA root-zone entry for .llc](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/llc.html).

## History of .llc

- **14 December 2017 — ICANN Registry Agreement signed.** Then-Afilias executed a standard "Base, Non-Sponsored" agreement with ICANN, confirmed in the [ICANN Registry Agreement details for .llc](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/llc).
- **16 February 2018 — delegated to the root zone**, per the IANA delegation record.
- **5 June – 5 July 2018 — sunrise period.** Trademark holders with a [Trademark Clearinghouse](/en/glossary/tmch/) record could register first, per [Afilias' official launch announcement](https://afilias.info/news/2018/07/09/new-llc-domain-landrush-period-begins-july-9-general-availability-begins-july-23rd).
- **9–20 July 2018 — landrush period.** The first opportunity for non-trademark-holders to register on a first-come, first-served basis at special pricing.
- **23 July 2018 — general availability.** As the registry's own [press release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/new-llc-domain-landrush-period-begins-july-9-300677484.html) put it plainly: "**.LLC registration is open to anyone; no need to wait till the paperwork is filed**" — a launch stance that has defined how the extension is used since.

## How people use .llc

- **Registered LLCs matching their legal entity name** — `yourbusiness.llc` for companies already formed under state LLC law.
- **Pre-formation reservations** — founders securing the domain while incorporation paperwork is still in process, exactly as the registry's own launch messaging encouraged.
- **Freelancers and consultants planning to incorporate** — using the domain as a placeholder or forward-looking identity before the legal entity exists.
- **Small businesses wanting a matching-entity address** when their ideal .com is taken.

**Who it's not ideal for:** corporations, non-profits, or partnerships whose entity type isn't actually an LLC — the string carries a specific structural meaning that can read as inaccurate if your business isn't organized that way. It's also not the pick for consumer-facing brands seeking maximum mainstream recognition.

## Notable sites using .llc

.llc does not yet have widely recognized flagship public brands in the way [.io](/en/tld/io) or [.com](/en/tld/com) do. According to [nTLDstats' .llc registry data](https://ntldstats.com/tld/llc), the extension's registrant base is spread across more than 120 registrars with no single dominant concentration, consistent with broad small-business adoption rather than a handful of major public companies. The realistic characterization is that .llc is used mainly by small and newly formed US-style LLCs rather than as a household-name flagship domain.

## .llc vs other domains

| Factor | .llc | [.com](/en/tld/com) | [.inc](/en/tld/inc) | [.co](/en/tld/co) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Typical price positioning | Budget-to-standard | Standard | Premium, high flat pricing | Standard |

| Entity-type signal | Explicitly "LLC" | None | "Incorporated" | None |

Pick .com for maximum universal recognition; pick [.inc](/en/tld/inc) if your entity is actually incorporated and budget isn't the deciding factor; pick .llc specifically when your business is (or will be) organized as a limited liability company and you want the domain to say so.

## Why choose .llc?

- **Literal entity-type match.** If your business is an LLC, the domain says exactly what your paperwork says.
- **No formation prerequisite.** The registry explicitly designed .llc so you can register before your LLC paperwork is complete — useful for founders moving fast.
- **Broad registrar availability.** Available through more than 120 registrars per [nTLDstats](https://ntldstats.com/tld/llc), so there's no shortage of places to buy.
- **Clear differentiation from .inc.** Positioned at a more accessible price point than its corporate sibling .inc, per contemporaneous [industry launch coverage](https://domainnamewire.com/2018/06/12/llc-and-inc-domain-names-are-on-their-way/).

## Things to consider

- **Meaning is entity-specific.** A .llc address on a corporation or non-LLC entity can read as a mismatch to a knowledgeable visitor.
- **No formation verification also means no proof of legitimacy.** Since anyone can register regardless of actual LLC status, the domain alone doesn't establish that a business is real or properly formed.
- **Lower mainstream recognition than .com.** Most users still default to typing .com from memory.
- **Fragmented registrant base.** With no single dominant registrar, the extension is still establishing broad brand recognition among everyday internet users.

## Who can register a .llc domain?

**Registration restrictions: open to all.** [Identity Digital's registry agreement with ICANN](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/llc) establishes .llc as a standard, non-sponsored gTLD, and the registry's own 2018 launch materials state plainly that registration does not require proof an LLC has actually been formed. A sunrise period for [Trademark Clearinghouse](/en/glossary/tmch/) holders ran 5 June – 5 July 2018, followed by a landrush phase through 20 July, before general availability opened 23 July 2018 — all phases are long closed, so registration today is first-come, first-served. Standard ICANN lifecycle rules apply, including auto-renew grace, redemption grace, and pending-delete periods, and [WHOIS privacy](/en/glossary/whois-privacy/) is available through most [registrars](/en/glossary/registrar).

## .llc pricing and value

.llc sits in the **budget-to-standard tier** of the gTLD market, distinctly below its premium-positioned sibling .inc, according to [contemporaneous industry coverage](https://domainnamewire.com/2018/06/12/llc-and-inc-domain-names-are-on-their-way/) of both launches. As with most gTLDs, expect **first-year promotional pricing to differ from the standing renewal rate** — budget for the renewal, not the introductory offer. The registry may also reserve certain short or high-demand strings as premium names carrying different pricing. No figures are quoted here; check current rates with your registrar.

## Reputation and email deliverability

.llc has no documented history of spam or abuse concerns. [nTLDstats data](https://ntldstats.com/tld/llc) shows a meaningful share of .llc domains running signed DNSSEC zones and registrations spread across major mainstream registrars including GoDaddy, Squarespace, and Namecheap — a registrant profile consistent with legitimate small-business use rather than bulk, low-cost abuse patterns. Standard email-authentication hygiene (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) remains good practice regardless of suffix.

## Branding and naming tips

- **Match your actual entity type.** Use .llc only if your business is, or will genuinely be, organized as an LLC — the literal meaning is part of its value.
- **Reserve early if incorporating.** The registry's own positioning supports registering `yourbusiness.llc` before your formation paperwork finalizes.
- **Pair with a matching .com if possible.** Many .llc registrants use it as a secondary or defensive registration alongside a primary .com.
- **Keep it short and consistent** with your legal entity name to avoid visitor confusion between the brand and the domain.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone register a .llc domain?

Yes. .llc is an open generic top-level domain. The registry explicitly does not require proof that an LLC has been legally formed, so aspiring business owners can secure a name before completing paperwork, and anyone else can register one too.

### Does a .llc domain affect SEO?

No. Google treats .llc as a generic top-level domain with no inherent ranking boost or geographic bias. Rankings depend on content quality, links, and user experience, not the suffix.

### Do I need to actually form an LLC before registering a .llc domain?

No. The registry has stated publicly that .llc registration is open to anyone regardless of formation status, so you can reserve your name while your paperwork is still in progress or even if you never form an LLC at all.

### Who should register a .llc domain?

US-style limited liability companies, freelancers planning to incorporate as an LLC, and small businesses wanting a domain that matches their actual legal entity type. It suits founders who specifically want the LLC structure reflected in their web address.

### How is .llc different from .inc?

.llc and .inc are both open, 2012-round corporate-identity gTLDs with no eligibility gate, but they target different entity types and sit at very different price points, with .inc positioned as the premium, higher-priced option of the two.
