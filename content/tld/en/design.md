The **.design domain** is an open generic top-level domain purpose-built for one word: design. Since its 2015 launch it has become one of the more genuinely adopted new gTLDs among working designers, with real portfolio sites, design-system hubs, and creative agencies choosing it over a generic .com. This page covers where .design came from, who runs it today, who actually uses it, and the honest trade-offs before you register one.

## What is .design?

.design is a [generic top-level domain](/en/glossary/gtld) (gTLD) approved through ICANN's 2012 New gTLD Program, not a country-code extension, so it carries no geographic meaning. The [IANA root-zone entry for .design](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/design.html) records a **registration date of 2014-12-18** and shows **Registry Services, LLC** — the GoDaddy Registry entity — as the current sponsoring organization.

Because it is a plain generic extension, Google Search Central treats [new gTLDs](/en/glossary/new-gtld) like .design the same as legacy generics such as [.com](/en/tld/com): there is no automatic ranking benefit or penalty tied to the suffix, and no geo-targeting signal is applied.

## History of .design

- **Founded by designers, for designers (2014–2015).** [Top Level Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.design), a Portland, Oregon-based company led by CEO Ray King, won the right to operate .design in a private auction against six other applicants in September 2014. IANA's delegation report shows the domain was formally delegated to Top Level Design, LLC on **2015-01-20**; general availability opened **May 12, 2015**, with more than 5,200 .design domains registered on that first day alone. The underlying [ICANN Registry Agreement for .design was signed on 7 November 2014](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/design).
- **Independent operation (2015–2021).** For roughly six years Top Level Design ran .design as a standalone registry, building it into one of the better-adopted design-focused new gTLDs.
- **GoDaddy Registry acquisition (2021).** In April 2021, [GoDaddy Registry announced it was acquiring the .club and .design domain extensions](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/godaddy-registry-agrees-to-acquire-club-design-and-minds--machines-tlds-to-offer-more-choice-and-value-in-digital-name-options-301264501.html) — a separate transaction from GoDaddy's larger 28-TLD acquisition from Minds + Machines (MMX) announced the same month. IANA's record shows the formal transfer of .design to **Registry Services, LLC** completed on **2021-08-31**. Top Level Design's founders continued operating other strings, including .wiki, .ink, and .gay, after the sale.

## How people use .design

Real, specific niches where .design shows up:

- **Freelance and independent designer portfolios** — a direct, on-the-nose extension for personal design portfolios (`yourname.design`).
- **Design-system and internal design-team hubs** — for example, **[atlassian.design](https://atlassian.design)**, Atlassian's official public design-system and design-team site, runs directly on the .design extension, documenting foundations like color, typography, and accessibility for the company's products.
- **Design and branding agencies** — studios and agencies use it as a clean, discipline-specific alternative when the matching .com is unavailable.
- **UX, product, and industrial design projects** — case studies and process write-ups that want the URL itself to signal the discipline.
- **Design education and community sites** — courses, critique communities, and design-adjacent tools.

**Who it's not ideal for:** businesses outside the design field, since the suffix actively signals "design" and can misdirect visitors if the site is about something unrelated.

## Notable sites using .design

Unlike many newer gTLDs, .design has a genuine, verifiable flagship example: **[atlassian.design](https://atlassian.design)**, Atlassian's live, actively maintained design-system and design-team site, covering design foundations, AI design patterns, and design career content for the company. Beyond that single well-known brand example, .design's broader adoption skews toward independent designer portfolios and small studio sites rather than a long list of major public brands — an honest reflection of a discipline-specific suffix rather than a general-purpose one.

## .design vs other domains

| Factor | .design | [.com](/en/tld/com) | [.studio](/en/tld/studio) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Recognition & trust | Moderate, design-specific | Highest, universal | Moderate, creative-specific |
| Fit for designers/portfolios | Very strong (literal match) | Generic, works for anything | Strong (broader creative scope) |
| Availability of short names | Moderate | Very scarce | Moderate |
| Geo bias in SEO | None (generic) | None (generic) | None (generic) |
| Typical price tier | Standard-to-premium new-gTLD | Standard | Standard new-gTLD |

Pick .com when maximum universal trust matters most and the exact-match string is available; pick [.studio](/en/tld/studio) when the work spans creative disciplines beyond design specifically (video, photo, agency work); pick .design when the site is squarely about design work and you want the extension itself to say so.

## Why choose .design?

- **Literal, self-explanatory meaning.** "Design" is a real word that states exactly what the site is about, with no interpretation needed.
- **Real adoption by working designers.** Unlike many new gTLDs that remain mostly parked or speculative, .design has a track record of active portfolios and at least one major verified brand example ([atlassian.design](https://atlassian.design)).
- **Open and unrestricted.** No paperwork, credentials, or eligibility checks — register and go.
- **Backed by an established operator.** GoDaddy Registry is one of the largest gTLD operators, providing stable technical infrastructure and support.

## Things to consider

- **Narrow semantic fit.** The extension signals design work specifically; a mismatch here (e.g., a bakery or law firm) is more obvious than with a neutral suffix.
- **Still a newer gTLD.** Some users default to trusting .com first at a glance, though this matters less within design-industry audiences who already recognize .design.
- **Ownership transition.** The 2021 GoDaddy acquisition changed the operator after six years under Top Level Design; policy and support now run through GoDaddy Registry rather than the original independent team.

## Who can register a .design domain?

**Registration restrictions: open to all.** .design is an unrestricted generic TLD — there is no credential, professional-membership, community, or local-presence requirement. Any individual or organization worldwide can register a .design name through an accredited [registrar](/en/glossary/registrar).

A trademark **sunrise period** preceded general availability in 2015, so today registrations are first-come, first-served, aside from a short list of names the registry has reserved as premium or restricted. The extension supports internationalized domain names (IDNs) and [DNSSEC](/en/glossary/dnssec). Standard ICANN lifecycle rules apply — auto-renew grace, redemption grace, and pending-delete periods — so a lapsed .design name is not immediately available again. The binding rules are set out in the [ICANN Registry Agreement for .design](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/design) and GoDaddy Registry's own domain policies.

## .design pricing and value

.design sits above the cheapest new-gTLD tier, and the dynamics matter more than any single figure. Expect **first-year and renewal pricing to differ** — introductory rates are often promotional, while the standing renewal price is what you pay long term, so budget for renewal, not the teaser rate. The registry also maintains a list of **premium and reserved names** (short, dictionary, or high-demand design-adjacent strings) that carry higher registration and renewal pricing than standard names. The main cost drivers are string length, dictionary-word desirability, premium classification, and registrar margin. We list no figures here; check current rates at the point of purchase.

## Reputation and email deliverability

.design does not carry a documented history of spam or abuse issues comparable to some cheaper, high-volume new gTLDs — its adoption pattern (deliberate portfolio and agency sites) tends to attract legitimate, low-volume registrants rather than bulk abuse. As with any newer gTLD, some conservative mail providers may apply marginally more scrutiny to unfamiliar domains than to decades-old legacy extensions. **Mitigation:** configure SPF, DKIM, and DMARC correctly, warm up any new sending domain gradually, and keep registrant and content information clean and consistent.

## Branding and naming tips

- **Let the extension do the explaining.** `studio.design` or `yourname.design` tells visitors what the site is before they click.
- **Drop redundant words.** If your business is "Acme Design," `acme.design` is cleaner than `acmedesign.design`.
- **Keep it short and portfolio-ready.** Many .design registrants are individuals; a short, memorable name suits a personal brand well.
- **Watch for confusion outside design circles.** Audiences unfamiliar with new gTLDs may misread `.design` as part of the name rather than the extension — a clear logo lockup or tagline helps.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone register a .design domain?

Yes. .design is an open, unrestricted generic top-level domain operated by Registry Services, LLC (GoDaddy Registry). There is no credential, membership, or local-presence requirement, so any individual or organization worldwide can register one through an accredited registrar.

### Does a .design domain affect SEO?

No. Google treats .design as a standard generic top-level domain with no ranking boost or penalty and no geographic bias. Ranking still depends on content quality, relevance, and links, not the extension itself.

### Who should register a .design domain?

Freelance designers, design agencies, portfolio sites, and design-adjacent products (UX, branding, product, and industrial design) are the strongest fit, since the suffix states the discipline directly in the domain.

### Is .design a good choice for a non-design business?

Generally no. The suffix signals design work specifically, so a company outside that field risks confusing visitors about what the site actually offers. A neutral extension like .com serves general businesses better.
