The **.is domain** is the country-code top-level domain for **Iceland**, and it has built an outsized reputation for something Iceland never planned: **domain hacks**. Because "is" is a common English word ending, brands worldwide register `.is` names to spell out a phrase across the dot — the WHOIS lookup tool [who.is](https://who.is) and the URL-shortening service [this.is](https://this.is) are both live, working products built entirely around the trick. This page covers what .is is, who runs it, the rules for registering, and how Google treats it — so you can judge whether the hack is worth it for your project.

## What is .is?

**.is** is the [ccTLD](/en/glossary/cctld/) assigned to Iceland under the ISO 3166-1 country-code system. It is managed by [**ISNIC — Internet á Íslandi hf.**](https://www.isnic.is/en), a Reykjavík-based company, as confirmed in the [IANA root-zone database entry for .is](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/is.html). ISNIC is a for-profit company, not a government agency or nonprofit — it operates the registry under an agreement with IANA/ICANN following RFC 1591 and ICP-1, the standard framework for ccTLD delegations.

Because .is is a ccTLD tied to a specific country, Google's own guidance explains that country-code domains ["provide a strong signal to both users and search engines that your site is explicitly intended for a certain country"](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites#:~:text=These%20are%20tied%20to%20a%20specific%20country). Google maintains a short list of ccTLDs it treats as generic instead — .ad, .ai, .as, .bz, .cc, .cd, .co, .dj, .fm, .io, .la, .me, .ms, .nu, .sc, .sr, .su, .tv, .tk, .ws — and **.is is not on it**. In practice a .is site is geo-targeted toward Iceland by default, which matters for anyone chasing global rankings rather than a wordplay domain used purely for direct traffic.

## History of .is

ISNIC's own account of its history states that the .is namespace was **delegated to SURIS in 1988** by IANA, after Iceland's early internet infrastructure had been run informally since 1986 by the SURIS and ICEUNET networks. ISNIC itself was formally established as a company in 1995 to take over day-to-day management, and the earliest registered names included `hi.is` (University of Iceland), `os.is`, and `hafro.is`.

Registrations happen directly at the second level (`yourname.is`), with a handful of names reserved for institutional use: `net.is`, `com.is`, `edu.is`, `gov.is`, `org.is`, and `int.is`. Over the following decades, .is grew steadily as an open, low-friction namespace, and by the 2010s the tech world had discovered its value for word-completing domain hacks — the biggest driver of .is registrations from outside Iceland.

## How people use .is

Real, specific niches where .is shows up:

- **Domain hacks that complete an English word** — the defining use case, exemplified by `who.is` and `this.is`.
- **Icelandic businesses, media, and government-adjacent sites**, for whom .is is the natural national identity, much like [.us](/en/tld/us/) is for American organizations.
- **URL-shortening and link-branding services** that lean on the short, two-letter suffix.
- **Icelandic-language content** using ISNIC's supported Icelandic characters (þ, æ, ö, ð) for names that need them.

**Who it's not ideal for:** projects with no natural ".is" wordplay and no Icelandic connection, since the geo-targeting signal works against a name chosen purely for its short length.

## Notable sites using .is

- **[who.is](https://who.is)** — a WHOIS and RDAP domain-lookup tool whose name is a literal question, "who is [this domain]," completed by the .is suffix.
- **[this.is](https://this.is)** — a URL-shortening and custom-link service built entirely around the "this.is/yourname" wordplay, actively marketed and maintained as of 2026.
- **hi.is** — the University of Iceland's own domain, one of the first .is registrations and still in use by Iceland's national academic institution.

## Why choose .is?

- **Genuine wordplay value.** Few suffixes complete as many natural English phrases as ".is" — "who is," "this is," and similar constructions read instantly.
- **No gatekeeping.** ISNIC's own rules confirm registration is open to any person or legal entity, with no residency requirement.
- **Mature, stable registry.** Delegated in 1988, ISNIC is one of the older ccTLD operators, with established DNSSEC and IDN support.
- **Short and memorable.** Two letters, easy to type, easy to say.

## Things to consider

- **Google geo-targets it by default.** Unlike .io or .co, .is is not on Google's generic-ccTLD list, so a global brand may want to set international targeting deliberately rather than assume neutrality.
- **Confusable with "IS" the acronym.** Depending on your brand, the two-letter string can read as an abbreviation rather than as part of a phrase if the surrounding word doesn't land clearly.
- **Icelandic character rules add nuance.** If you want a name using þ, æ, ö, or ð, check ISNIC's exact character and length rules before you commit to a name.
- **Smaller registrar footprint than mainstream ccTLDs.** Fewer registrars carry .is compared with .io or .co, so shop around.

## Who can register a .is domain?

**Registration restrictions: open to all.** ISNIC's [official domain rules](https://www.isnic.is/en/domain/rules) state that any person or legal entity may register a .is domain, and if someone registers on behalf of a rights holder, "that party shall have a clear mandate to bind the right holder" — but there is no citizenship, residency, or local-presence test for the registrant.

Every registration requires a designated **administrative contact**, who is authorized to manage the registration, and a technical contact; neither is required to be based in Iceland. **[IDN](/en/glossary/idn/)** names may use the Icelandic letters á, é, ý, ú, í, ó, þ, æ, ö, and ð alongside standard ASCII letters, numerals, and hyphens — names using those extra characters have a maximum length of 59 characters versus 66 for ASCII-only names. **[DNSSEC](/en/glossary/dnssec/)** is supported. Six second-level names are reserved for institutional use (net.is, com.is, edu.is, gov.is, org.is, int.is). The authoritative source for current eligibility and technical rules is ISNIC's [domain rules page](https://www.isnic.is/en/domain/rules), the ccTLD manager's own policy, since ccTLDs like .is are not governed by an ICANN registry agreement.

## .is pricing and value

.is pricing follows ISNIC's own fee schedule rather than a wholesale-plus-registrar-margin model typical of gTLDs, and ISNIC states that **annual domain fees are generally non-refundable** once paid. As with most TLDs, expect the mechanics to include an **annual renewal fee** distinct from any promotional first-registration rate a reseller might offer, and **premium or short names** (especially one- and two-character strings, where still available) can carry non-standard pricing set directly by ISNIC. This page intentionally quotes no figures — check current ISNIC or registrar pricing at the point of purchase.

## Reputation and email deliverability

.is carries a **neutral-to-positive reputation** as a mature, well-governed ccTLD rather than a bargain-bin new gTLD associated with spam. ISNIC has a track record of acting against abuse on its namespace, and that governance history works in favor of legitimate .is registrants: the suffix itself is not commonly flagged by spam filters the way some ultra-cheap new gTLDs are.

For email deliverability specifically, sender reputation, [SPF/DKIM/DMARC authentication](/en/blog/email-sender-reputation-arms-race/), and list hygiene matter far more than the TLD itself. A properly authenticated .is domain should deliver normally.

## Branding and naming tips

- **Design the whole phrase, not just the domain.** The best .is names read as a complete sentence or phrase the moment you say them aloud — "who is," "this is," "life is."
- **Avoid forcing it.** If your brand name doesn't naturally end in a word that pairs with "is," the hack can feel gimmicky rather than clever.
- **Keep the second-level part short.** Domain hacks lose their charm past a handful of characters; the shortest, punchiest options work best.
- **Check Icelandic character availability separately** if your name could use þ, æ, ö, or ð for a more authentic Icelandic identity.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone register a .is domain?

Yes. ISNIC, the registry for Iceland, imposes no residency, citizenship, or local-presence requirement. Any person or legal entity anywhere in the world can register a .is domain directly through ISNIC or an accredited reseller.

### Does a .is domain affect SEO?

Google does not list .is among the ccTLDs it treats as generic, so by default a .is site sends a strong signal that it targets Iceland. Sites using .is purely for its word-completing domain-hack value should account for that geo-targeting signal.

### Who should register a .is domain?

It suits brands and tools that can form a real word or phrase by ending in "is" (who.is, this.is), plus anyone building for an Icelandic audience. It is a weaker choice for a global brand with no natural ".is" wordplay.

### Does .is support internationalized domain names?

Yes. ISNIC allows the Icelandic letters á, é, ý, ú, í, ó, þ, æ, ö, and ð in addition to the standard ASCII alphabet, numerals, and hyphens, with a shorter maximum length for names using those extra characters.
