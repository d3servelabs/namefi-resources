The **.pro domain** has the most unusual biography of any top-level domain covered on this site: it spent its first eleven years as a gated credential system reserved for licensed professionals, then reopened to the entire world in a single 2015 policy change. That history — not new-gTLD marketing — is what makes .pro worth understanding before you buy one. This page covers where .pro came from, exactly when and how the restriction was lifted, who operates it today, and whether the "professional" positioning still means anything now that anyone can register one.

## What is .pro?

.pro is a [generic top-level domain](/en/glossary/gtld), short for "professional." It carries no geographic meaning, so Google's [multi-regional targeting guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites#:~:text=A%20generic%20top-level%20domain%20%28gTLD%29) treats it like any other generic suffix — you'd need hreflang, URL structure, or Search Console geo-targeting to signal a specific country, the domain itself does not do that for you.

What sets .pro apart from same-era peers like [.biz](/en/tld/biz) or [.info](/en/tld/info) is that it wasn't approved in the 2012 New gTLD Program at all. It belongs to ICANN's much earlier **2000-round expansion**, the first set of new TLDs approved after .com, .net, and .org — a cohort that also produced .biz, .info, .name, .coop, .museum, and .aero. You can confirm the delegation record, current sponsoring organization, and IANA's category label for .pro directly in the [IANA root-zone database entry for .pro](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/pro.html).

## History of .pro

- **May 2002 — ICANN agreement signed.** RegistryPro Ltd. signed the original .pro registry agreement, part of the 2000-round expansion. The [archived 3 May 2002 agreement](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/pro/pro-registry-agreement-signed-3-may-2002-3-5-2002-en) is preserved in ICANN's contract archive.
- **June 23, 2004 — public launch, credential-gated.** .pro opened for registration restricted to four professions — accountants, engineers, lawyers, and medical professionals — and only to applicants in Canada, Germany, the UK, and the US. Every registrant's license had to be verified before the name was issued.
- **September 2008 — eligibility widened.** Following ICANN consultation, the registry expanded eligibility to government-certified professionals in any country, dropping the four-country limit while keeping the credential-verification requirement itself.
- **2012 — acquired by Afilias.** Afilias Limited (later folded into Identity Digital) took over the RegistryPro contract, consolidating .pro into its broader registry-services portfolio.
- **1 October 2015 — new base agreement, restriction lifted.** The [ICANN Registry Agreement for .pro](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/pro) was replaced with a standard "Base, Non-Sponsored" agreement, and on 16 November 2015 .pro became open to any registrant worldwide — no license or credential proof required, though registrants are asked to self-declare a profession.

Notably, IANA's root-zone database still labels .pro's category as a **"Restricted generic top-level domain"** in its [.pro delegation record](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/pro.html) — a leftover classification, not a description of today's open policy.

## How people use .pro

- **Solo consultants and freelancers** — `yourname.pro` reads as a straightforward professional identity page.
- **Small accounting, legal, and engineering practices** — firms that want the word "professional" baked into the address without the credential-verification hurdle of gated alternatives like [.cpa](/en/tld/cpa) or [.law](/en/tld/law).
- **Portfolio and personal-brand sites** — subject-matter experts using .pro as a shorter alternative when a matching .com is unavailable.
- **Multi-brand professional groups** — organizations registering .pro alongside a primary .com as a defensive or vanity registration.

**Who it's not ideal for:** anyone specifically trying to signal *verified* credentials to visitors — since 2015, a .pro address no longer implies the registrant passed any licensing check, so it carries no more built-in trust than [.com](/en/tld/com) itself. It's also a weak fit for consumer brands with no professional-services angle.

## Notable sites using .pro

.pro has no widely recognized flagship consumer brands the way [.io](/en/tld/io) or [.com](/en/tld/com) do; its post-2015 open base skews toward individual consultants, small professional-service firms, and portfolio sites rather than major public companies. The honest characterization: .pro is used mostly for personal and firm identity, not as a household-name flagship domain.

## Why choose .pro?

- **Literal, self-explanatory meaning.** "Professional" is not a stretch reading — it's the actual word, useful when your ideal .com is taken.
- **Longer track record than most alternative gTLDs.** Delegated in 2002 and live since 2004, .pro has over two decades of uptime and registry continuity, unlike many 2012-round strings still finding their footing.
- **Now open, so no paperwork friction.** You get the "professional" branding without the eligibility hurdles that gate .law or .cpa.
- **Operated by an established registry.** Identity Digital runs a large, mature TLD portfolio, reducing registry-stability risk.

## Things to consider

- **The credential signal is gone.** Since 2015, a .pro address does not verify anything about the registrant — competitors know this too, so don't market it as proof of licensure.
- **Lower mainstream recognition than .com.** Most users still default to typing .com from memory.
- **Overlaps with more specific niches.** If you're a lawyer or accountant specifically, a niche extension like [.law](/en/tld/law), [.cpa](/en/tld/cpa), or [.attorney](/en/tld/attorney) may communicate your field more precisely than the generic "professional" of .pro.
- **IANA's stale category label can confuse due-diligence checks.** Because IANA's root-zone entry still reads "Restricted generic top-level domain," a registrar or compliance team doing a quick IANA lookup might wrongly assume .pro still gates registration.

## Who can register a .pro domain?

**Registration restrictions: open to all, since 16 November 2015.** Before that date, .pro required proof of a recognized professional credential (initially limited to accountants, engineers, lawyers, and medical professionals in four countries, later expanded worldwide in 2008). That verification requirement was removed when ICANN and the registry executed a new, non-sponsored base Registry Agreement — you can read the [current ICANN Registry Agreement for .pro](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/pro) directly, including the agreement history back to the original 2002 contract.

There is no trademark sunrise currently running (the 2015 policy change was a re-launch of an existing string, not a new gTLD delegation, so no [Trademark Clearinghouse](/en/glossary/tmch/) sunrise applied). Standard [ICANN](/en/glossary/icann) lifecycle rules apply, including auto-renew grace, redemption grace, and pending-delete periods, and [WHOIS privacy](/en/glossary/whois-privacy/) is available through most [registrars](/en/glossary/registrar).

## .pro pricing and value

.pro pricing follows the same structural pattern as most gTLDs: **first-year promotional rates typically differ from the standing renewal rate**, so budget for what you'll actually pay long-term, not the introductory price. The registry may reserve certain short or high-demand strings as **premium names**, which carry different registration and renewal pricing than standard names. We quote no specific figures here — check current rates with your registrar at the point of purchase.

## Reputation and email deliverability

.pro carries a comparatively clean reputation among gTLDs. Because it launched as a gated, professional-only namespace and only opened in 2015, it did not accumulate the bulk-registration spam history that some budget new gTLDs (like [.sbs](/en/tld/sbs)) picked up during rapid open launches. That said, since 2015 it is registered the same way any open gTLD is, so ordinary email-deliverability hygiene still applies: configure SPF, DKIM, and DMARC correctly, and warm up new sending domains gradually regardless of suffix.

## Branding and naming tips

- **Use the literal meaning.** ".pro" reads instantly as "professional" — lean into that rather than trying to force a clever alternate reading.
- **Pair with your actual field.** `smithlegal.pro` or `janedoecpa.pro` communicates more than a generic name plus the suffix alone.
- **Don't imply verification you don't have.** Since the credential gate is gone, avoid marketing copy that suggests .pro itself proves your license — state your actual credentials separately.
- **Keep it short.** Two decades of availability plus a mid-size registration base means concise, exact-match names are more attainable than on legacy zones like .com.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone register a .pro domain?

Yes, today. From its 2004 launch until 16 November 2015, .pro required proof of a recognized professional credential. ICANN and the registry removed that verification requirement in 2015, and .pro has been open to any individual or business worldwide since.

### Does a .pro domain affect SEO?

No. Google treats .pro as a generic top-level domain with no built-in ranking boost or geographic bias. Rankings depend on content quality, links, and user experience, not the suffix.

### Is .pro a new gTLD like .app or .xyz?

No. .pro predates the 2012 New gTLD Program by a decade. It was approved by ICANN in 2002 as part of the original 2000-round expansion alongside .biz, .info, .name, .coop, .museum, and .aero, and launched in 2004.

### Who should register a .pro domain?

Freelancers, consultants, and licensed professionals such as accountants, lawyers, engineers, and clinicians who want a suffix that literally signals "professional" and a matching .com or industry-specific name is unavailable.

### Why does IANA still list .pro as a restricted TLD?

IANA's root-zone database still classifies .pro under its original registration-era category label, "restricted generic top-level domain," even though the operative registration policy has permitted open registration since 2015. The label reflects the TLD's origin, not its current rules.
