The **.best domain** turns a single superlative word into a top-level domain, letting a name like `coffee.best` or `agency.best` read as a finished sentence. It is an open [new gTLD](/en/glossary/new-gtld/) that anyone can register, delegated in the 2012-round ICANN New gTLD Program and unchanged in registry ownership since 2014. This page covers who actually operates .best, how the superlative-branding pattern works in practice, and the honest trade-offs, so you can judge whether it fits your project.

## What is .best?

.best is a [generic top-level domain](/en/glossary/tld), not a country-code extension, so it carries no geographic meaning. The string itself is the ordinary English superlative "best," which makes it one of the more directly usable words in the new gTLD expansion — a name reads naturally when the word "best" completes it, the way `hosting.best` reads as "hosting, best" or "best hosting."

Because it is a generic gTLD, Google treats .best like any other new extension: no automatic ranking advantage or penalty, and no geo-targeting applied, per [Google Search Central's guidance on generic vs. country-targeted domains](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites?hl=en#:~:text=more%20generic%20than%20country-targeted). You can confirm the delegation record and current operator on the [IANA root-zone entry for .best](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/best.html).

Google also states that [keywords in a TLD do not give any advantage or disadvantage in search](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2015/07/googles-handling-of-new-top-level?hl=en#:~:text=Keywords%20in%20a%20TLD%20do%20not%20give%20any%20advantage%20or%20disadvantage%20in%20search.), so the extension itself receives no inherent ranking boost or penalty.

## History of .best

.best was one of the more than 1,200 strings delegated during ICANN's original 2012-round New gTLD Program. The [ICANN Registry Agreement for .best](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/best) was signed on 19 December 2013 by **BestTLD Pty Ltd**, and IANA's delegation record shows the string went live under that same operator on 25 February 2014 — registration in the root dates to 20 February 2014.

Unlike [.lol](/en/tld/lol) or [.work](/en/tld/work), .best's registry agreement has never been reassigned to a different named operator; BestTLD Pty Ltd remains the sponsoring organization on file more than a decade later. That said, [IANA's current delegation record](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/best.html) lists the administrative contact as **The Best SAS**, a company based in Champigny-sur-Marne, France — meaning day-to-day administration of the registry has shifted to a French entity even though the Australian sponsoring organization's name on the contract has not changed. Technical operations run through **CentralNic**, the same back-end provider used by [.sbs](/en/tld/sbs), [.xyz](/en/tld/xyz), and dozens of other new gTLDs.

## How people use .best

Real, specific niches where .best shows up:

- **Domain-hack branding** — the classic use case, where "best" completes the name as a superlative: `[category].best`.
- **Product and service marketing pages** — landing pages and campaign sites that lean on the built-in "best" claim.
- **Review and comparison sites** — content that literally recommends "the best" of a category.
- **Short, brandable names** — the .best namespace still has real availability for concise strings long taken on [.com](/en/tld/com).
- **Budget alternatives to premium .com names** — a lower-cost way to secure a usable name for a new product line.

**Who it's not ideal for:** brands whose name doesn't naturally pair with the word "best," anything requiring maximum mainstream trust on first glance, or projects where reliable email delivery is mission-critical.

## Notable sites using .best

.best is a lower-profile extension without a widely recognized flagship brand running its primary site on the suffix, the way [.io](/en/tld/io) does for tech startups. In practice, its visible footprint skews toward the domain-hack pattern described above — marketing and campaign pages built around the superlative reading — rather than major companies replacing their main .com. Rather than name a specific site whose ownership or content may not persist, the honest characterization is that .best is used mostly for the wordplay it enables, not as a category-defining namespace.

## Why choose .best?

- **Built-in meaning.** Few new gTLDs let the suffix itself complete a marketing sentence the way .best does.
- **Open and unrestricted** — no paperwork or eligibility checks; register and launch.
- **Stable registry history** — the same operator relationship since 2014, without the ownership churn some budget gTLDs have seen.
- **Strong short-name availability** — concise strings that are long gone on legacy zones.

## Things to consider

- **Only works with the right name.** The superlative reading is the whole appeal; if "best" doesn't fit naturally in front of your word, the domain loses its main advantage.
- **Lower mainstream familiarity.** Many users still default to trusting .com first at a glance.
- **Can read as generic marketing-speak.** A name built purely on "best" can feel like a claim rather than a brand if the content doesn't back it up.
- **Email scrutiny.** As with other budget new gTLDs, some spam filters apply closer scrutiny to newer, low-cost extensions (see Reputation below).

## Who can register a .best domain?

**Registration restrictions: open to all.** .best is an unrestricted generic TLD — there is no credential, professional-membership, community, or local-presence requirement. Any individual or organization worldwide can register a .best name through an accredited [registrar](/en/glossary/registrar).

Names can run from 1–63 characters, use letters, numbers, and hyphens (not in positions three and four), and are handled first-come, first-served, per [GoDaddy's .best domain policy summary](https://www.godaddy.com/help/about-best-domains-12451). The extension supports internationalized domain names (IDNs) and [DNSSEC](/en/glossary/dnssec), and registrations run 1–10 years at a time. The registry also designates a set of [premium domain](/en/glossary/premium-domain) names. The binding legal terms are set out in the [ICANN Registry Agreement for .best](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/best).

## .best pricing and value

.best sits in the budget-to-mid tier of the gTLD market, and that shapes its pricing dynamics rather than any single number. **First-year and renewal pricing typically differ** — an inexpensive introductory rate is common, while the standing renewal rate is what matters long-term. The registry reserves a set of **premium names** (short, dictionary, or high-demand strings) that carry higher registration and renewal pricing than standard names — a domain-hack-friendly extension like .best tends to see many of its most obviously useful short strings classified this way. The main cost drivers are string length and desirability, premium classification, and registrar margin. We list no figures here; check current rates at the point of purchase.

## Reputation and email deliverability

Buyers deserve candor here. We could not find a current, fetchable Spamhaus or Interisle report that names .best specifically among its worst- or most-abused TLD lists — if you find one, treat it as more current than this page. What is well documented at the category level: Interisle's [Cybercrime Supply Chain 2025 research](https://interisle.net/insights/cybercrimesupplychain2025#:~:text=though%20they%20hold%20just%2012%25%20of%20the%20market%2C%20they%20accounted%20for%20nearly%20half%20of%20all%20cybercrime%20domains%20reported) (sponsored by APWG, CAUCE, and M3AAWG) found that new gTLDs as a category hold just 12% of the domain market yet accounted for nearly half of all reported cybercrime domains — a general reminder that low-cost, easy-to-bulk-register extensions draw disproportionate abuse as a category, even without a .best-specific figure to cite.

Practically, that means some spam filters and cautious users may apply extra scrutiny to unfamiliar .best links, particularly ones formed as generic-sounding domain hacks. The extension itself is legitimate and ICANN-accredited — being on .best does not make a site untrustworthy. **Mitigation:** configure SPF, DKIM, and DMARC correctly for any email sent from a .best domain, warm up new sending domains gradually, and back up the "best" claim with real, verifiable content.

## Branding and naming tips

- **Design the domain hack first.** Say the full name out loud — `[word].best` — before committing; the pattern only works when it reads naturally.
- **Avoid literal overclaiming.** "Best" is a strong word; content that doesn't support it undercuts the brand.
- **Keep the prefix short.** Short prefixes plus .best are easiest to remember and type.
- **Watch for confusable characters.** Zeros vs. the letter "o" and repeated letters are exactly what scammers exploit; a clean, pronounceable name distances you from that pattern.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone register a .best domain?

Yes. .best is an open, unrestricted generic top-level domain. There is no credential, membership, or local-presence requirement, so anyone worldwide can register a .best name through an accredited registrar on a first-come, first-served basis.

### Does a .best domain affect SEO?

Google treats .best as a generic top-level domain with no built-in ranking boost or penalty and no geographic bias. Rankings depend on content quality, links, and user experience rather than on the suffix itself.

### Who operates the .best domain?

The registry agreement is held by BestTLD Pty Ltd, an Australian entity, per ICANN records. IANA lists the administrative contact as The Best SAS, based in France, with technical operations run by CentralNic — the domain has kept the same registry lineage since its 2014 delegation.

### Who should register a .best domain?

It suits businesses and products built around a superlative domain hack, such as coffee.best or agency.best, where the word "best" completes a natural phrase. It is a weaker choice when the name needs to stand alone without that wordplay, or for anything that depends heavily on email deliverability.
