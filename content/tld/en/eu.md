The **.eu domain** is the regional top-level domain for the **European Union**, operated by the non-profit registry [EURid](https://eurid.eu/). Unlike a national ccTLD, .eu isn't tied to one country — it's restricted to EU/EEA citizens, residents, and organizations, a rule that produced one of the domain industry's most dramatic real-world eligibility events when Brexit forced the suspension of tens of thousands of UK-held names. This guide covers who can actually register .eu today, that Brexit history, and .eu's unusual multi-script identity.

## What is .eu?

.eu is a **generic regional top-level domain**, not a national ccTLD — its [IANA root-zone record](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/eu.html) lists **EURid vzw**, a Belgium-based non-profit, as the sponsoring organization. EURid was itself founded on 8 April 2003 by the national registries of Belgium, Italy, and Sweden, and the European Commission designated it to run .eu shortly after.

.eu's Google treatment is genuinely unusual among region-associated extensions. Google's own [multi-regional sites guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites?hl=en#:~:text=Generic%20regional%20top-level%20domains) places .eu in a specific category — **"generic regional top-level domains"** — stating that although these domains are associated with a geographical region, "they are generally treated as generic top-level domains (much like .com or .org)." So unlike .de or .uk, a .eu site does not automatically read to Google as targeting a single country.

## History of .eu

.eu was delegated to the root zone on **28 April 2005**, following EURid's 2003 founding and its European Commission appointment. EURid marked **20 years of .eu** in April 2026, per its own [company timeline](https://eurid.eu/en/about-eurid/timeline/). Two multi-script expansions followed: EURid launched a **Cyrillic-script variant, .ею**, in June 2016, and a **Greek-script variant, .ευ**, in November 2019 — both governed by the same eligibility rules as standard Latin-script .eu.

The most consequential event in .eu's history is **Brexit**. As EURid's own [10 February 2021 update](https://eurid.eu/en/news/update-on-brexit-related-domain-names/) states, on **1 January 2021** it moved **over 81,000 domain names** whose registrants had a GB or GI (Gibraltar) country code to **"SUSPENDED"** status, since UK-based registrants no longer met .eu's EU/EEA eligibility rules after the UK left the bloc. Suspended names could be reinstated by updating registration data to show qualifying EU/EEA citizenship, residency, or establishment; names not corrected were moved to **"WITHDRAWN"** and later opened for re-registration by others. Separately, EURid widened eligibility on its own terms: since **2 August 2021**, any citizen of an EU member state, Iceland, Liechtenstein, or Norway can register a .eu domain regardless of where they live — broader than the residency-only test that applied before.

## How people use .eu

- **Pan-European businesses and NGOs** wanting one identity spanning multiple EU member states rather than juggling separate national ccTLDs.
- **EU institutions and EU-adjacent projects** — the European Union's own main web presence, `europa.eu`, is itself a .eu registration.
- **Companies expanding across the EU/EEA** that don't want to commit to one country's ccTLD (like .de) as their primary identity.
- **Trademark holders defensively registering .eu** alongside national ccTLDs to prevent squatting across the EU market.
- **Users of the Cyrillic (.ею) and Greek (.ευ) scripts** wanting a EU-affiliated domain in their own alphabet.

**Who it's not ideal for:** anyone or any organization without a genuine EU/EEA citizenship, residency, or establishment link — including, since 2021, UK-only registrants — and brands wanting a single-country identity rather than a pan-European one.

## Notable sites using .eu

The clearest, most verifiable example is **europa.eu**, the EU's own official web portal — a .eu domain run by the EU institutions themselves. Beyond that flagship case, .eu is used mostly by pan-European organizations and businesses registering it defensively alongside a national ccTLD, rather than by globally recognized consumer brands as their primary address.

Choose **.com** for a globally neutral default with no eligibility test, **.eu** for a pan-European identity if you qualify, and a national ccTLD like **[.de](/en/tld/de/)** or **[.uk](/en/tld/uk/)** when one country is your real focus — noting that, post-Brexit, .uk-only entities can no longer register .eu.

## Why choose .eu?

- **Pan-European positioning.** One extension instead of managing separate ccTLDs across multiple EU countries.
- **SEO-neutral by Google's own classification.** Because Google treats .eu as generic rather than geo-targeted, it doesn't box a site into a single country's search results the way a national ccTLD would.
- **Non-profit, EU-appointed governance.** EURid operates under a European Commission concession, not as a commercial registry chasing volume.
- **Genuine multi-script support.** The Cyrillic and Greek variants are a real, rare feature among regional TLDs.

## Things to consider

- **Real eligibility restrictions.** Unlike most marketed ccTLDs, .eu is not open to anyone, anywhere — you must have a genuine EU/EEA citizenship, residency, or establishment link.
- **Brexit risk is precedent, not hypothetical.** EURid has already shown it will suspend and withdraw domains en masse when a registrant's home jurisdiction leaves the eligibility zone.
- **Thin flagship-brand presence.** Beyond europa.eu itself, .eu has not become a first-choice address for major global consumer brands.
- **Ongoing compliance burden.** Registration data must keep demonstrating eligibility — a change in citizenship, residency, or establishment status can put your domain at risk.

## Who can register a .eu domain?

**Registration restrictions: EU/EEA eligibility required.** Per EURid's own [Rules for domain names](https://eurid.eu/en/knowledge-centre/rules-for-eu-domains/#:~:text=Eligibility%20criteria), registration can be requested by: a citizen of an EU member state, Iceland, Liechtenstein, or Norway, regardless of residence; a natural person who is not such a citizen but resides in one of those countries; an undertaking established in one of those countries; or an organization established there. Citizens registering from outside the EU/EEA must also indicate their country of citizenship as part of the registration data.

Registration must go through an [accredited registrar](/en/glossary/registrar/), with complete data — name or organization, address, email, phone, and preferred language — required for the registration to be considered complete. Terms run 1 to 10 years with tacit annual renewal. [DNSSEC](/en/glossary/dnssec/) is supported, and .eu is available in Latin script plus the Cyrillic (.ею) and Greek (.ευ) variants, all under the same eligibility rules.

## .eu pricing and value

.eu pricing follows the dynamics common to most TLDs rather than any fixed figure: registration terms can run from one to ten years, and **first-year promotional pricing commonly differs from the standing renewal rate**, so budget for the renewal, not the teaser. Because eligibility is restricted, .eu doesn't see the same volume-driven scarcity in short names that a fully open TLD like .com does — availability is shaped more by demand from qualifying EU/EEA registrants than by decades of open, global registration. This page quotes no specific prices — check current rates at registration.

## Reputation and email deliverability

.eu carries a credible, institutional reputation, reinforced by non-profit governance under a European Commission concession and by its citizenship/residency test, which limits the anonymous bulk registration that drives abuse on some open, ultra-cheap gTLDs. For email, deliverability depends far more on sender authentication — SPF, DKIM, and DMARC — than on the TLD itself; a properly authenticated .eu sender reaches inboxes normally. The main practical risk is eligibility, not reputation: as the Brexit suspensions showed, a registrant's own change in citizenship or residency status can put continued use of the domain at risk.

## Branding and naming tips

- **Confirm eligibility before you build a brand on it.** Verify your EU/EEA citizenship, residency, or establishment status before committing to .eu as a primary address.
- **Use it for genuinely pan-European positioning.** .eu works best when "we operate across the EU" is the actual message.
- **Consider the script variants.** The Cyrillic (.ею) and Greek (.ευ) versions suit audiences who read those scripts natively.
- **Pair with a national ccTLD for one primary market.** A single-country ccTLD like .de often communicates more clearly than .eu when your focus is really one country.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone register a .eu domain?

No. .eu is restricted to citizens of an EU member state, Iceland, Liechtenstein, or Norway regardless of where they live; residents of those countries even without that citizenship; and organizations established there. There is no open, anyone-anywhere registration the way there is for most gTLDs.

### What happened to UK-held .eu domains after Brexit?

On 1 January 2021, EURid moved over 81,000 domain names whose registrants were based in the UK or Gibraltar to SUSPENDED status because they no longer met the eligibility rules. Names not updated to show qualifying EU/EEA citizenship, residency, or establishment were later placed into WITHDRAWN status and eventually released for re-registration.

### Does a .eu domain affect SEO?

Unusually for a country-associated suffix, Google explicitly classifies .eu as a "generic regional top-level domain" and treats it much like .com or .org rather than as a geo-targeted ccTLD. That makes .eu one of the few regional extensions that does not automatically signal a single-country audience.

### Does .eu support non-Latin scripts?

Yes. Alongside the standard Latin-script .eu, EURid operates a Cyrillic-script variant, .ею, launched in 2016, and a Greek-script variant, .ευ, launched in 2019 — both under the same eligibility rules as .eu itself.
