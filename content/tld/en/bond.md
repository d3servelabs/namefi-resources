The **.bond domain** started as an Australian university's unused dot-brand and is now one of the most aggressively churned generic top-level domains on the internet — a fact [Spamhaus](https://www.spamhaus.org/) has called out by name in consecutive threat reports. Operated by the registry [ShortDot SA](https://shortdot.bond/), .bond trades on both the financial-instrument and James Bond associations of its string. This page covers what .bond is, its unusual ownership history, and the reputation data buyers should see before registering — verified against IANA, ICANN, and Spamhaus's own reporting.

## What is .bond?

.bond is a [generic top-level domain](/en/glossary/tld) with no country tie. The string evokes both a **financial bond** (a debt instrument) and, informally, the **James Bond** film franchise — associations ShortDot's marketing leans on, though neither is licensed or official. As a generic extension, Google treats .bond like any other gTLD: no automatic ranking boost or penalty and no geo-targeting bias. You can verify the delegation record directly in the [IANA root-zone entry for .bond](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/bond.html).

Google also states that [keywords in a TLD do not give any advantage or disadvantage in search](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2015/07/googles-handling-of-new-top-level?hl=en#:~:text=Keywords%20in%20a%20TLD%20do%20not%20give%20any%20advantage%20or%20disadvantage%20in%20search.), so the extension itself receives no inherent ranking boost or penalty.

## History of .bond

.bond's ownership history is unusual even among ShortDot's portfolio of acquired strings:

- **Original dot-brand (2014–2019):** [Bond University](https://bond.edu.au/), a private university in Queensland, Australia, applied for .bond during ICANN's 2012 New gTLD Program round and signed its [ICANN Registry Agreement on 5 June 2014](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/bond). IANA delegated the string to Bond University Limited on 23 March 2015, but the university never opened it to the public — [Domain Incite reported](https://domainincite.com/24277-dot-brand-bond-has-been-acquired-and-will-relaunch-as-a-generic-this-july) it sat dormant with only a single placeholder domain in its zone file for years.
- **Acquisition by ShortDot (2019):** IANA's delegation record shows the transfer of .bond to Shortdot SA on 20 September 2019. ShortDot then relaunched the string as an open generic TLD, moving its technical back-end from Neustar to CentralNic.
- **Relaunch dates:** According to [ShortDot's own launch announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/shortdot-to-launch-bond-domain-extension-on-october-17-to-trademark-holders-and-to-all-end-users-on-november-18-300925429.html), .bond became available to trademark holders on 17 October 2019 and to all other end users on 19 November 2019.
- **Where it stands now:** As of this writing, [ntldstats.com](https://ntldstats.com/tld/bond) reports .bond at roughly 1.55 million registered domains, with about 1.16 million — over 75% — live in the zone file, a notably high proportion for a ShortDot-run TLD.

So when people ask where .bond is really from, the honest answer is: it began as an Australian university's unused brand domain, and is now a globally available generic extension flagged repeatedly by threat researchers for its registration behavior — covered in detail below.

## How people use .bond

Real, specific niches where .bond shows up:

- **Domain hacks** — names that read naturally with the suffix, e.g. `james.bond` or `secret.bond`, are a common, low-stakes use case.
- **Finance-adjacent branding** — some registrants lean on the financial-instrument association for budget projects, though it carries no regulatory meaning.
- **Short, brandable names** — the .bond namespace still has wide availability for concise names long gone in legacy zones.
- **Bulk, low-cost registration** — as detailed in the reputation section below, a large share of .bond's zone consists of transient, high-turnover registrations rather than settled sites.

**Who it's not ideal for:** any regulated financial business, a flagship brand, or anything where email deliverability and trust are mission-critical. Given the churn data below, a more established extension is the safer default.

## Notable sites using .bond

.bond does not have widely recognized flagship public sites the way [.io](/en/tld/io) or [.app](/en/tld/app) do. One notable, verifiable exception: the registry operator itself, ShortDot SA, runs its own corporate site at [shortdot.bond](https://shortdot.bond/) — a rare case of a registry actually using its own extension. Beyond that, Spamhaus's own reporting characterizes much of .bond's zone as high-churn, disposable registration rather than durable public brands.

## Why choose .bond?

- **Open and unrestricted** — no eligibility checks, and no actual affiliation with Bond University or finance required.
- **Dual wordplay** — the financial-bond and James Bond associations both work for the right project.
- **Strong availability** — short, memorable names remain attainable given the extension's high turnover.
- **Budget-friendly** — among the more affordable extensions, lowering the cost of short-term experimentation.

## Things to consider

- **Named repeatedly by Spamhaus.** In its [Domain Reputation Update for October 2025–March 2026](https://content.spamhaus.org/4d83c22f-84c5-4f82-95ab-4e6b39f73db5.pdf), Spamhaus's spotlight section states: "For three consecutive reports, .bond, operated by Shortdot SA, continues to display patterns of high churn, with new domain registrations (1.13 million) almost equal to its total zone count (1.15 million)." The same report ranks .bond #1 among all gTLDs for the share of its zone made up of newly observed domains — 98.57%, meaning the zone effectively turns over within a six-month window.
- **Meaningful abuse listings.** That same report places .bond among the top TLDs by count of domains flagged malicious or suspicious, a pattern echoed in the prior six-month report.
- **Lower mainstream familiarity.** Many users still equate "a real website" with .com, which can cost a sliver of trust at first glance.
- **No real Bond University or 007 affiliation.** Registering `.bond` implies neither institutional nor franchise endorsement.

## Who can register a .bond domain?

**Registration restrictions: open to all.** .bond is an unrestricted generic TLD — there is no credential, professional-membership, community, or local-presence requirement, and no need to demonstrate any connection to Bond University, finance, or the film franchise. Any individual or organization worldwide can register a .bond name through an accredited [registrar](/en/glossary/registrar).

A trademark sunrise period ran ahead of general availability in 2019, so today registrations are first-come, first-served. The extension supports internationalized domain names (IDNs) and DNSSEC, and standard ICANN lifecycle rules apply — including the auto-renew grace, redemption-grace, and pending-delete periods. The binding rules are set out in the [ICANN Registry Agreement for .bond](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/bond).

## .bond pricing and value

.bond sits in the budget tier of the gTLD market, and that shapes its pricing dynamics rather than any single number. Expect **first-year and renewal pricing to differ** — an introductory rate designed to drive volume, against a standing renewal rate that is typically higher, so budget for the renewal price rather than the first-year teaser. The registry may also reserve certain short or dictionary strings as **premium names** carrying higher registration and renewal fees. We quote no figures here; check current rates at the point of purchase.

## Reputation and email deliverability

This is the section every prospective .bond buyer should read carefully. The [current Spamhaus report, covering October 2025–March 2026](https://content.spamhaus.org/4d83c22f-84c5-4f82-95ab-4e6b39f73db5.pdf), ranks `.bond` **#1 among gTLDs by share of the zone newly observed**, at **98.57%** (1,130,779 new domains out of a 1,147,185-domain zone). It also ranks `.bond` **#4 by malicious or suspicious domain count** with 59,271, and **#8 by share of zone listed** at 5.17%.

Practically, that means .bond traffic draws real scrutiny from mail providers and security tools, independent of any individual site's actual content. **Mitigation:** configure SPF, DKIM, and DMARC properly for any email sent from a .bond domain, warm up sending volume gradually, keep content clean, and for anything trust-sensitive, weigh whether a more established extension serves you better.

## Branding and naming tips

- **Lean into a clear wordplay.** Names that read naturally as English words plus ".bond" work best — the dual financial/007 association is the extension's main creative asset.
- **Set realistic expectations.** Given the churn and abuse data above, treat .bond as disposable or experimental rather than a long-term flagship domain.
- **Avoid look-alike strings.** Confusable characters are exactly what abusive bulk registrants exploit on high-churn TLDs; a clean, pronounceable name sets a legitimate project apart.
- **Don't imply institutional or franchise endorsement** — nothing about the `.bond` string carries university or studio affiliation today.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone register a .bond domain?

Yes. .bond is an open, unrestricted generic top-level domain operated by ShortDot SA. There are no credential, membership, or local-presence requirements, and no affiliation with Bond University or the finance industry is required, so anyone worldwide can register a .bond name.

### Is a .bond domain safe?

The .bond extension itself is legitimate and ICANN-accredited, but Spamhaus has specifically singled out .bond in multiple consecutive reports for unusually high registration churn and a meaningful share of malicious or suspicious domains. The extension does not make a specific site dangerous, but treat unfamiliar .bond links with extra caution.

### Does a .bond domain affect SEO?

Google treats .bond as a generic top-level domain with no automatic ranking boost or penalty and no geographic bias. Its documented churn and abuse history can make some mail and security filters more cautious, which can indirectly affect deliverability and click-through.

### Is .bond connected to Bond University or James Bond?

Historically, yes to the university, no to the film character. Bond University in Australia originally held .bond as a closed dot-brand from 2015 until it never launched it publicly; ShortDot acquired the distribution rights in 2019 and reopened it as a generic extension. Any James Bond or financial-bond association today is purely wordplay, not licensed or official.

### Who should register a .bond domain?

It suits finance-adjacent branding, short domain hacks, and budget projects that can tolerate a documented high-churn reputation. It is a weaker choice for a regulated financial business, a flagship brand, or anything where inbox deliverability is critical.
