The **.icu domain** is one of the cheapest and, at its 2019 peak, one of the largest new [generic top-level domains](/en/glossary/tld) in the world — and also one of the most abused. Operated by the registry [ShortDot SA](https://shortdot.bond/) and marketed as **"I See You,"** .icu built an enormous zone through rock-bottom first-year pricing, then shed millions of names when the discounts ended. This page covers what .icu is, its boom-and-bust history, and the honest reputation trade-offs — verified against IANA, ICANN, and Spamhaus's own reporting — so you can decide whether it fits your project.

## What is .icu?

.icu is a [generic top-level domain](/en/glossary/tld) with no country tie. ShortDot markets the three letters as **"I See You,"** though the string also reads naturally as the medical abbreviation for "intensive care unit," a coincidence ShortDot has never tried to hide. As a generic extension, .icu is treated by Google like any other gTLD — no automatic ranking boost, no penalty, and no geo-targeting bias, per [Google Search Central's guidance on generic domains](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2015/07/googles-handling-of-new-top-level?hl=en#:~:text=Keywords%20in%20a%20TLD%20do%20not%20give%20any%20advantage%20or%20disadvantage%20in%20search.). You can verify the delegation record directly in the [IANA root-zone entry for .icu](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/icu.html).

Google also states that [keywords in a TLD do not give any advantage or disadvantage in search](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2015/07/googles-handling-of-new-top-level?hl=en#:~:text=Keywords%20in%20a%20TLD%20do%20not%20give%20any%20advantage%20or%20disadvantage%20in%20search.), so the extension itself receives no inherent ranking boost or penalty.

## History of .icu

.icu's delegation record tells an unusually documented story:

- **Original delegation (2015):** IANA's own records show .icu was first delegated to **One.com A/S**, a Danish web-hosting company, on 28 April 2015, under an [ICANN Registry Agreement signed 8 January 2015](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/icu).
- **Transfer to ShortDot (2018):** IANA records a transfer of the .icu domain to Shortdot SA on 15 February 2018. ShortDot relaunched it as an open gTLD, running a sunrise period starting 24 April 2018 and reaching general availability by late May 2018.
- **Explosive growth (2018–2019):** Priced as low as $1–2 for a first year, .icu grew from nothing to over 900,000 domains and then crossed one million registrations roughly 13 months after launch — briefly making it one of the largest new gTLDs by zone size, alongside .club, .xyz, and .online, according to [industry tracking by Domain Incite](https://domainincite.com/24469-icu-joins-the-million-domains-club-in-one-year-but-spam-triples).
- **Spam spike (2019):** That same coverage reported Spamhaus's "badness" rating for .icu roughly tripling in two months, from 6.4% in May 2019 to 17.4% in July 2019, while .com held steady over the same period.
- **Mass expiry (2020–2021):** A large share of that first-year volume never renewed at full price. [Domain Incite's 2021 industry analysis](https://domainincite.com/26980-domain-industry-shrinks-again-except-of-course-it-doesnt) attributed roughly six million of the new-gTLD sector's year-over-year registration decline to .icu's "first junk drop" of heavily discounted names.
- **Where it stands now:** As of this writing, [ntldstats.com](https://ntldstats.com/tld/icu) reports .icu at roughly 1.09 million registered domains, with just over half — about 556,600 — actually live in the zone file, reflecting continued high churn between new registrations and non-renewals.

## How people use .icu

Real, specific niches where .icu shows up:

- **Short-lived campaigns and landing pages** — the low first-year price makes it a disposable option for tests and promotions.
- **Personal blogs and small hobby sites** — some registrants use it purely for the "I See You" or ICU wordplay.
- **Budget projects in price-sensitive markets** — a large share of historical registrations originated from China, drawn by aggressive promotional pricing.
- **Domain hacks** — names that read naturally with the suffix, e.g. `spam.icu` or `watch.icu`, for the acronym pun.

**Who it's not ideal for:** any business, e-commerce, financial, or email-dependent site where trust and deliverability matter. Given the documented abuse history below, a more established extension is the safer default for anything customer-facing.

## Notable sites using .icu

.icu does not have widely recognized flagship brands the way [.io](/en/tld/io) or [.app](/en/tld/app) do. Registrar and reseller marketing pages point to small independent blogs and niche hobby sites as typical examples, not established companies. The honest characterization, backed by its own registration data, is that .icu's visible footprint skews toward short-lived, low-budget, and — per Spamhaus's own tracking — a meaningful share of abusive pages, rather than durable public brands.

Pick .com when trust and deliverability are non-negotiable; pick [.sbs](/en/tld/sbs) if you want a similarly budget-friendly ShortDot extension with a somewhat different reputation profile; pick .icu only for short-term, low-stakes use where the wordplay and price genuinely fit.

## Why choose .icu?

- **Very low entry cost** — one of the cheapest ways to register a short, brandable-looking domain.
- **Wide availability** — because so much of the historical zone has churned out, short and clean names are easier to find than in older TLDs.
- **Open and unrestricted** — no eligibility checks or paperwork.
- **The wordplay works** — "I See You" genuinely fits watch-, monitoring-, or attention-themed projects.

## Things to consider

- **Documented spam history.** .icu's badness rating spiked sharply during its 2019 growth surge and it has continued to register meaningfully in Spamhaus's malicious/suspicious TLD rankings in recent reporting periods — this is the central trade-off for the entire extension.
- **High non-renewal churn.** Roughly half of registered .icu domains are not currently live in the zone file, a sign that much of the namespace is transient rather than settled.
- **Low mainstream trust.** Many users still read an unfamiliar three-letter extension as a red flag, independent of the site's actual content.
- **Confusable with "ICU" the medical term**, which can read oddly for some brand contexts.

## Who can register a .icu domain?

**Registration restrictions: open to all.** .icu is an unrestricted generic TLD — there is no credential, professional-membership, community, or local-presence requirement. Any individual or organization worldwide can register a .icu name through an accredited [registrar](/en/glossary/registrar).

A trademark sunrise period ran ahead of general availability in 2018, so today registrations are first-come, first-served. The extension supports internationalized domain names (IDNs) and DNSSEC, and standard ICANN lifecycle rules apply — including the auto-renew grace, redemption-grace, and pending-delete periods — so a lapsed name does not become instantly available. The binding rules are set out in the [ICANN Registry Agreement for .icu](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/icu).

## .icu pricing and value

.icu sits at the extreme budget end of the gTLD market, and its pricing dynamics explain much of its history: an unusually low **first-year rate** designed to drive volume, against a **standing renewal rate** that is typically several times higher — the gap between the two is exactly what fueled the 2018–2019 registration boom and the 2020–2021 non-renewal wave. The registry may also reserve certain short or dictionary strings as **premium names** with higher registration and renewal pricing. We quote no figures here; check current rates at the point of purchase and budget for the renewal price, not the introductory one.

## Reputation and email deliverability

This is the section that matters most for .icu. Its extreme affordability enabled bulk, disposable registration at a scale few other TLDs have seen, and that pattern shows up directly in third-party abuse tracking: Spamhaus's own [Domain Reputation Update](https://content.spamhaus.org/4d83c22f-84c5-4f82-95ab-4e6b39f73db5.pdf) reporting for October 2025–March 2026 places .icu among the top TLDs by count of malicious or suspicious domains detected, alongside a similar showing in the prior six-month period — consistent with the sharp spam-rating spike Domain Incite documented back in 2019.

Practically, that means mail providers, security tools, and cautious users apply extra scrutiny to .icu traffic. The extension itself is not inherently dangerous — being on .icu does not make a specific site malicious — but a legitimate .icu site starts from a reputational deficit. **Mitigation:** configure SPF, DKIM, and DMARC correctly for any email sent from a .icu domain, warm up sending volume gradually, keep content clean, and for anything trust-sensitive, seriously weigh whether a more established extension serves you better.

## Branding and naming tips

- **Lean into the acronym.** "I See You" fits monitoring, security, surveillance, and attention-economy projects naturally.
- **Keep expectations realistic.** Given the reputation issues above, .icu is best treated as disposable or experimental rather than a long-term flagship domain.
- **Avoid look-alike strings.** Confusable characters are exactly what abusive registrants exploit on cheap TLDs; a clean, pronounceable name distances a legitimate project from that pattern.
- **Pair it with a strong redirect strategy** if you ever plan to migrate to a more established extension later.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone register a .icu domain?

Yes. .icu is an open, unrestricted generic top-level domain operated by ShortDot SA. There are no credential, membership, or local-presence requirements, so anyone worldwide can register a .icu name through an accredited registrar.

### Is a .icu domain safe?

The .icu extension itself is legitimate and ICANN-accredited, but its rock-bottom pricing has repeatedly attracted bulk spam and phishing registrations, and Spamhaus has listed tens of thousands of .icu domains as malicious or suspicious in recent reporting periods. The extension does not make a site dangerous, but treat unfamiliar .icu links with extra caution.

### Does a .icu domain affect SEO?

Google treats .icu as a generic top-level domain with no automatic ranking boost or penalty and no geographic bias. In practice, its documented spam history can make some mail and security filters more cautious toward .icu traffic, which can indirectly affect click-through and deliverability.

### Why did so many .icu domains disappear after 2020?

ShortDot priced .icu cheaply to fuel rapid first-year growth, and much of that volume was speculative or abusive registration that was never renewed. Industry tracking attributed millions of the new-gTLD sector-wide registration decline in 2021 to the mass non-renewal of heavily discounted .icu names.

### Who should register a .icu domain?

It suits short-term campaigns, low-budget side projects, and buyers who specifically want the "I See You" wordplay and can tolerate a mixed reputation. It is a poor choice for a primary business domain, a payment site, or anything where inbox deliverability is critical.
