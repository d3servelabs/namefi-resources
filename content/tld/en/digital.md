The **.digital domain** is an open generic top-level domain aimed squarely at the modern economy: digital agencies, technology consultancies, and brands built around "going digital." Operated by **Binky Moon, LLC**, part of the [Identity Digital](https://www.identity.digital/) registry family, .digital has been live since 2014 — and notably, Identity Digital itself uses a .digital address for its own corporate site. This page covers where .digital came from, who actually uses it, and the honest trade-offs before you register one.

## What is .digital?

.digital is a [generic top-level domain](/en/glossary/tld) (gTLD), unrelated to any country or region, approved through [ICANN](/en/glossary/icann)'s 2012 New gTLD Program. The string is self-explanatory: it signals a business built around digital products, services, or transformation before a visitor reads any copy.

Because it's a generic string, Google applies no geo-targeting rule to .digital — it's evaluated like any other gTLD. You can confirm the delegation record directly in the [IANA root-zone entry for .digital](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/digital.html).

## History of .digital

- **Delegation (2014):** .digital was delegated to the root zone on **May 2, 2014** (registration date April 17, 2014), initially assigned to **Dash Park, LLC** — one of the single-purpose entities Donuts Inc. used to file its 2012-round gTLD applications (the same pattern used for sibling strings like .media, filed under "Grand Glen, LLC"). The underlying [ICANN Registry Agreement for .digital](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/digital) was signed **March 6, 2014**.
- **Consolidation into Binky Moon (2018):** On **March 23, 2018**, IANA recorded the transfer of .digital — together with 195 other Donuts-originated strings — to **Binky Moon, LLC**, Donuts's consolidated registry entity.
- **Identity Digital era (2021–present):** Following Ethos Capital's 2021 acquisition of Donuts and its merger with Afilias, the combined company rebranded as **Identity Digital** in June 2022. Identity Digital now runs its own primary corporate website at `identity.digital` rather than a .com address — a direct, self-referential proof point for the extension it sells.

## How people use .digital

- **Digital marketing and creative agencies** — the single most common use case; the name is the pitch.
- **Digital-transformation and technology consultancies** — firms advising enterprises on modernization.
- **News and media outlets in transformation-era rebrands** — some regional broadcasters and newspapers use .digital for their online-only or digital-first arm.
- **SaaS, software, and IT services companies** — a descriptive alternative when the matching .com is unavailable.
- **Government and public-sector digital-services portals** — some agencies use .digital for online-service initiatives.

**Who it's not ideal for:** businesses with no technology, agency, or digital-services angle — the descriptive meaning can feel mismatched on a brand that isn't actually about "digital" anything.

## Notable sites using .digital

- **Identity Digital** (`identity.digital`) — the registry operator's own primary corporate site, a rare case of a registry using its own product as its flagship domain.
- **Citizen Digital** (`citizen.digital`) — the digital news platform of Kenya's Citizen TV, one of the country's largest broadcasters.
- **Luxe Digital** (`luxe.digital`) — an independent lifestyle and luxury-goods publication.

Pick [.com](/en/tld/com/) when universal trust matters most and it's available. Pick [.tech](/en/tld/tech/) when the product itself is a piece of technology rather than a digital service. Pick [.agency](/en/tld/agency/) when the emphasis is on being a service provider rather than "digital" specifically. Pick .digital when the brand's core positioning is transformation, digital services, or digital-first identity.

## Why choose .digital?

- **Immediate, accurate meaning.** ".digital" tells a visitor the business is technology- or transformation-oriented before they click through.
- **Open and unrestricted.** No paperwork or eligibility checks — register directly through any accredited registrar.
- **Credible institutional adoption.** Identity Digital's decision to run its own corporate site on `identity.digital`, and Citizen TV's use of `citizen.digital` for a national broadcaster, both lend the string real-world legitimacy.
- **Availability.** Short, on-brand names remain attainable here where the equivalent .com is long since gone.

## Things to consider

- **Broad, buzzword-adjacent meaning.** "Digital" is a widely used business term, so the suffix alone doesn't differentiate your brand from every other agency using the word.
- **Moderate mainstream recognition.** Many users still default to expecting .com first; a .digital address may take a moment longer to register as legitimate.
- **Single-TLD brand risk.** As with any non-.com extension, anchoring a whole brand on .digital without a backup plan carries the usual concentration risk.

## Who can register a .digital domain?

**Registration restrictions: open to all.** .digital is an unrestricted generic TLD with no credential, professional-membership, community, or local-presence requirement. Any individual or organization worldwide can register through an accredited [registrar](/en/glossary/registrar).

A trademark sunrise period ran ahead of general availability in 2014, so today registration is first-come, first-served. [DNSSEC](/en/glossary/dnssec) is supported at the registry, and [WHOIS privacy](/en/glossary/whois-privacy/) is available through most registrars. Standard ICANN lifecycle rules apply — auto-renew grace, redemption grace, and pending-delete periods — so a lapsed name doesn't become instantly available again. The binding rules are set out in the [ICANN Registry Agreement for .digital](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/digital).

## .digital pricing and value

.digital pricing follows standard new-gTLD dynamics rather than a single number. **First-year and renewal pricing typically differ** — promotional first-year rates are common, while the standing renewal rate is what you pay long term. The registry also reserves certain short, dictionary, or high-demand strings as **premium names**, which can carry higher registration and renewal pricing than standard names. Length, desirability, premium classification, and registrar margin are the main cost drivers. No figures are quoted here — check current rates at the point of purchase.

## Reputation and email deliverability

.digital does not carry the spam-heavy reputation associated with some ultra-low-cost new gTLDs. Its registrant base skews toward agencies, consultancies, and organizations building an intentional brand around the word "digital," including at least one national broadcaster and the registry operator itself, which supports a reasonably trustworthy baseline reputation. As with any newer extension, some conservative spam filters may still apply marginally more scrutiny than they would to .com. **Mitigation:** configure SPF, DKIM, and DMARC correctly, warm up new sending domains gradually, and keep content and outbound mail practices clean.

## Branding and naming tips

- **Let the word carry the positioning.** ".digital" already signals technology and transformation, so the second-level name can stay short (`luxe.digital`, `citizen.digital`).
- **Avoid redundancy.** Don't pair it with a name that repeats "digital," "tech," or "online" — the suffix already supplies that context.
- **Watch for genericness.** Because "digital" is such a common business word, a distinctive brand name matters more here than the suffix does.
- **Say it out loud.** Confirm the full domain is easy to say and spell over the phone before committing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone register a .digital domain?

Yes. .digital is an open, unrestricted generic top-level domain operated by Binky Moon, LLC. There is no credential, membership, or local-presence requirement, so any individual or organization worldwide can register a .digital name through an accredited registrar.

### Does a .digital domain affect SEO?

No. Google treats .digital as a generic top-level domain with no automatic ranking boost, penalty, or geographic bias, so a .digital site can rank globally exactly like a .com site would.

### Who should register a .digital domain?

Digital agencies, digital-transformation consultancies, marketing and technology firms, and any brand whose positioning is explicitly about being digital-first. It is a weaker fit for a business with no digital or tech angle to its name.

### Why does the registry operator of .digital itself use a .digital domain?

Identity Digital, the company that runs the .digital registry through its Binky Moon subsidiary, publishes its own corporate site at identity.digital rather than a .com address, using its own product as a visible demonstration of the extension.
